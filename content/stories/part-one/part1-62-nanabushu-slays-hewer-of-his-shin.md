62\. NÄNABUSHU SLAYS HEWER-OF-HIS-SHIN.

Nänabushu slays Hewer-of-his-Shin

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Mrs. Syrette — Series 8 (Nos. 57–63), Fort William (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīdac ạwe mindimōyä ki‘tciminwändạnk wâbamāt minawā ōjicäyạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash awe mindimooye gichi-minwendang waabamaad miinawaa oozhisheyan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon the old woman was very happy to see her grandson once more.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash awe mindimooye (and so that old woman) gichi-minwendang (was very glad) waabamaad (when she saw him) miinawaa oozhisheyan (her grandson once again).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Kāwīn dạc wīnigu ugi‘kändạⁿzīn pạngī nawatc kī∙ā∙ndci∙u∙skinīgiint.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin dash wiin igo ogikendanziin bangii nawaj gii-aanji-oskiniigiinid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And not at all did she realize that she had been made younger.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin dash wiin igo (and not at all did she) ogikendanziin (know it) bangii nawaj (a little more) gii-aanji-oskiniigiinid (that she had been made young again).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Mīdạc änāt ōjisạn: “Panä awiya ningīpi∙u∙disigōk; ijiwāt kīnisigōyạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash e-naad oozhisan: “Apane awiya ningii-bi’odisigoog; izhi-waad gii-nisigooyan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So then she said to her grandson: “All the time some creatures came to me here; they told me that you were slain.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash e-naad oozhisan (and so she said to her grandson): “Apane awiya (all the time someone) ningii-bi’odisigoog (kept coming to me here); izhi-waad (so they said) gii-nisigooyan (that you had been slain).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Minawā ānint intigōg, ‘Nō‘kumis, nintạgwicin,’ undcita pījiwāt,” udinān ōjisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Miinawaa aanind indigoog, ‘Nookomis, nin-dagwishin,’ onjida bi-izhiwaad,” odinaan oozhisheyan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Again by some would I be told, ‘O my grandmother! I have come home.’ With some design they came speaking thus to me,” she said to her grandson.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Miinawaa aanind indigoog (and again by some I would be told), ‘Nookomis (O my grandmother), nin-dagwishin (I have arrived home),’ onjida bi-izhiwaad (with some design they came saying so),” odinaan oozhisheyan (she said to her grandson).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Mīdạc Nänạbucu niskādisit, mīdạc änāt ō‘kumisạn: “Käyābi kīganạganin.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash Nenaboozhoo nishkaadizid, mii dash e-naad ookomisan: “Geyaabi giga-naganin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon Nänabushu was angry, and he said to his grandmother: “Again shall I leave you.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash Nenaboozhoo nishkaadizid (and so Nänabushu was angry), mii dash e-naad ookomisan (and so he said to his grandmother): “Geyaabi giga-naganin (again I shall leave you).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Kāwīn mạci nimi‘kawāsī awänänītug tcāgi∙ā∙gwän ningitisīmạg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin mashi ni-mikawaasii aweneniidog jaagi’aagwen nin-gitiziimag.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Not yet have I found the probable ones that must have slain all my relatives.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin mashi ni-mikawaasii (not yet have I found them) aweneniidog (whoever they may be) jaagi’aagwen (the ones who must have made an end of) nin-gitiziimag (my elders, my kin).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) “Aⁱ! nojic,” udinān, “kīgīkitimāgis. Kāwīn kitākaski∙o∙sī tcī∙i∙jāyạn iwiti ayāt aᵘ kākīwuci∙i∙‘k,” udinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aa! noozhis,” odinaan, “gigii-gidimaagis. Gaawiin gidaa-gashki’osii ji-izhaayan iwidi ayaad aw gaa-giiwooshi’ik, [gaa-giiwizi’ig]” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Alas! my grandson,” she said to him, “you are to be pitied. You may not be able to go there where abides the one who made you an orphan,” she said to him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aa! noozhis (alas, my grandson),” odinaan (she said to him), “gigii-gidimaagis (you are to be pitied). Gaawiin gidaa-gashki’osii (you will not be able) ji-izhaayan iwidi (to go there) ayaad aw gaa-giiwooshi’ik (where dwells the one who orphaned you), [gaa-giiwizi’ig]” odinaan (she said to him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) “Āntic ayāt?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aandish ayaad?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“And where is it?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aandish ayaad? (and where is it that he dwells?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) “Mī∙i∙witi ayāmāga‘k nāwaya∙ī∙ kiˢtcikiˢtcigạmīnk päji‘k minis.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii iwidi ayaamagag naawaya’ii gichi-gichigamiing bezhig minis.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“At yonder place in the centre of the great sea is an island.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Mii iwidi ayaamagag (there at yonder place is it) naawaya’ii gichi-gichigamiing (in the middle of the great sea) bezhig minis (one island).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Kāwīndạc wī‘kā awiyā ijāsī iwiti.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin dash wiikaa awiya izhaasii iwidi.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And never has any one gone there.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin dash wiikaa awiya (and never has anyone) izhaasii iwidi (gone there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Mīgu āpisi‘kāt awiya äcātcin.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii go aapiisikaad awiya e-zhaajin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And he goes for good whoever goes there.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii go aapiisikaad (and he goes for good — *aapiisikaa- stem flagged*) awiya e-zhaajin (whoever it is that goes there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) Kāwīndạc ki‘kändạⁿzīn ạmạntc tcitagwicinōwāgwän iwiti, kunimā gayä nowāndä nibowāgwän,” udigōn ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin dash gikendanziin amanj ji-dagwishinoowaagwen iwidi, gonimaa gaye nwaande-nibowaagwen,” odigoon ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*I don’t know whether they have ever arrived at the place, or if they died on the way,” he was (thus) told by his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin dash gikendanziin (and it is not known) amanj ji-dagwishinoowaagwen iwidi (whether they ever arrived there), gonimaa gaye nwaande-nibowaagwen (or whether they died on the way — *nwaande- stem flagged*),” odigoon ookomisan (he was told by his grandmother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) “Mīdạc iwiti ayāt a mạnidō; äjini‘kāsut Kātcīkika∙ạ∙nk-uka‘kwạn, mī∙ä∙nint mī a‘panä äjitcīgika∙ạ∙nk iᵘ uga‘kawạn, inā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii dash iwidi ayaad aw manidoo; e-zhinikaazod Gaa-jiigiga’ang-okakwan, mii e-nind mii apane ezhi-jiigiga’ang iw ogakawan, inaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“And over there is a manitou; he is called Hews-upon-his-Shin, for it is said of him that ever is he hewing upon his shin.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Mii dash iwidi ayaad aw manidoo (and over there dwells that manidoo); e-zhinikaazod Gaa-jiigiga’ang-okakwan (he is called Hews-Upon-His-Shin), mii e-nind (so it is said of him) mii apane ezhi-jiigiga’ang iw ogakawan (that always he hews upon his shin), inaa (so they say).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) Kīcpin awiya ijāt iwiti ayābi‘ta iji∙ā∙wạng mītäbwäwä∙ạ∙ng uga‘kwan,” udinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Giishpin awiya izhaad iwidi ayaabita izhi-aawang mii debwewe’ang ogakwan,” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*It is said of him that if any one should go thither, then at half way to the place would one hear the sound he makes upon his shin,” she said to him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Giishpin awiya izhaad iwidi (if anyone goes there) ayaabita izhi-aawang (at the halfway point) mii debwewe’ang ogakwan (one hears the sound he makes upon his shin),” odinaan (she said to him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) “Ōⁿ!” udigōn ōjisạn. Mīdạc kägä‘t kīcändạnk Nänabucu, inändạm wī∙i∙jāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Oo!” odigoon oozhisheyan. Mii dash geget giizhendang Nenaboozhoo, inendam wii-izhaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Really!” was she told by her grandson. Thereupon in truth did Nänabushu make up his mind, he thought that he would go.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Oo! (really!)” odigoon oozhisheyan (she was told by her grandson). Mii dash geget giizhendang Nenaboozhoo (and so truly Nänabushu made up his mind), inendam wii-izhaad (he resolved that he would go).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) Mīdạc änāt ō‘kumisạn: “Pō‘tc ninganatawâbamā,” udinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash e-naad ookomisan: “Booch ninga-nadawaabamaa,” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so he said to his grandmother: “Determined am I to go look for him,” he said to her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash e-naad ookomisan (and so he said to his grandmother): “Booch ninga-nadawaabamaa (I am bound to go look for him),” odinaan (he said to her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) Mīdạc Nänabucu minawā kā∙i∙ji∙u∙jī‘tād minawā kī∙u∙ci‘tōd usawānạn, nisugun mini‘k tci∙ā∙bạtci‘tōd ugī∙i∙ji∙u∙ji‘tōnạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash Nenaboozhoo miinawaa gaa-izhi-ozhiitaad miinawaa gii-ozhitood osaawaanan, nisogon minik ji-aabajitood ogii-izhi-ozhitoonan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon Nänabushu again made ready by making spear-pointed arrows, enough to last him three days was the number he made.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash Nenaboozhoo miinawaa gaa-izhi-ozhiitaad (and so Nänabushu again made ready) miinawaa gii-ozhitood osaawaanan (and made spear-pointed arrows), nisogon minik (enough for three days) ji-aabajitood (that he would use) ogii-izhi-ozhitoonan (so many he made).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) Mīdạc kā‘kīci‘tōd minawā Nänabucu mạmạdäsitōt utcīmānic.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-kiizhitood miinawaa Nenaboozhoo mamadesidood ojiimaanish.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so after he had finished them, then again Nänabushu put his canoe in order.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-kiizhitood (and so after he had finished them) miinawaa Nenaboozhoo (again Nänabushu) mamadesidood ojiimaanish (put his old canoe in order).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(19) Mīdạc mādcāt Nänabucu kwaya‘k kā∙i∙ninạmāgut ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash maajaad Nenaboozhoo gwayak gaa-ininamaagod ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then away went Nänabushu straight towards where it had been pointed out to him by his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash maajaad Nenaboozhoo (and then off went Nänabushu) gwayak gaa-ininamaagod ookomisan (straight where it had been pointed out to him by his grandmother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(20) Ninguting dạc kīwäⁿ Nänabucu anibạbimiskāt, cigwa kägä‘t kägō onōndām kwaya‘k äjāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding dash giiwenh Nenaboozhoo ani-babimishkaad, zhigwa geget gegoo onoondam gwayak e-zhaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, by and by, they say, as Nänabushu went paddling along (in his canoe), presently something he truly heard straight (on the way) whither he was going.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding dash giiwenh (now by and by, they say) Nenaboozhoo ani-babimishkaad (as Nänabushu went paddling along), zhigwa geget gegoo onoondam (presently truly he heard something) gwayak e-zhaad (straight ahead where he was going).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(21) Äji∙ạ∙gwamut ạndutạng; cigwa kägä‘t ōnōndān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ezhi-agwamod nendotang; zhigwa geget onoondaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He let his canoe go floating quietly along upon the water while he listened to the sound; presently he clearly heard a sound.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ezhi-agwamod (then he let his canoe float quietly) nendotang (while he listened); zhigwa geget onoondaan (presently truly he heard it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(22) “Täⁿ täⁿ,” ini‘tạm. Mīc änändạnk: “Na’! mī∙i∙we nō‘kumis kā∙i∙jit,” inändạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Denh denh,” inidam. Mii e-nendang: “Na! mii iwe nookomis gaa-izhid,” inendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Täⁿ, täⁿ,” (such) was the sound he heard. Thereupon he thought: “Hark! that is what my grandmother told me,” he thought.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Denh denh,” inidam (such was the sound he heard). Mii e-nendang (and so he thought): “Na! mii iwe (hark! that is it) nookomis gaa-izhid (what my grandmother told me),” inendam (he thought).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(23) Mīdạc kägä‘t mādcāt änigu‘k.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget maajaad enigok.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so truly he hurried on.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget maajaad enigok (and so truly he set off with all his might).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(24) Nänabucu kumā‘pī minawā nandu‘tạm cigwa minawā onōntām.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenaboozhoo gomaapii miinawaa nandotam zhigwa miinawaa onoondam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Nänabushu later on again listened for the sound, presently again he heard it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nenaboozhoo gomaapii (Nänabushu, after a while) miinawaa nandotam (again listened for the sound) zhigwa miinawaa onoondam (and presently again he heard it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(25) “Twäⁿ twäⁿ, twäⁿ,”¹ ini‘tạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Dwenh dwenh, dwenh,” (1) initam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Twäⁿ, twäⁿ, twäⁿ,” (1) was what he heard.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Dwenh dwenh, dwenh,” (1) initam (such was what he heard).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(26) Nawatc päcutāgwạtini. Mīnawā mādcāt änigu‘k.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nawaj beshodaagwadini. Miinawaa maajaad enigok.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Nearer it now sounded. Again he hastened on.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nawaj beshodaagwadini (nearer now it sounded). Miinawaa maajaad enigok (again he set off with all his might).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(27) Ningutingigu minawā ani∙a∙yinābit, kägō owâbạndān unīgānīmi; intigu kägō pämipäcibī∙i∙gātäg; mīdạc ayīnābit, “Mīmāwīn ‘iᵉi’ᵘ cayīgwa tayäbināgwa‘k minis äjāyān,” inändạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding igo miinawaa ani-ayinaabid, gego owaabandaan oniigaaniimi; indigo gegoo bemibeshibii’igaadeg; mii dash ayinaabid, “Mii maawiin i’iw zhayiigwa dayebinaagwak minis e-zhaayaan,” inendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Another time, as he was looking about, he saw something in the way ahead of him; it was an object like a line drawn across his course; and then, as he looked, “Perhaps that which is now coming into view is the island for which I am bound,” he thought.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding igo miinawaa ani-ayinaabid (another time, as he looked about), gego owaabandaan oniigaaniimi (he saw something ahead of him); indigo gegoo bemibeshibii’igaadeg (like something drawn as a line across); mii dash ayinaabid (and so as he looked), “Mii maawiin i’iw (perhaps that) zhayiigwa dayebinaagwak (now coming into view) minis e-zhaayaan (is the island I am going to),” inendam (he thought).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(28) Mīdạc kägä‘t änigu‘k pimiskāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget enigok bimishkaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon he truly hastened on with his canoe.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget enigok bimishkaad (and so truly with all his might he paddled on).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(29) Kumā‘pīdạc minawā andu‘tạm, mīgäyāpi äni‘tạng. “Twiⁿ twiⁿ,”¹ ini‘tạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gomaapii dash miinawaa nandotam, mii geyaapi enitang. “Dwiinh dwiinh,” (2) initam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Farther on he listened again for the sound, and he heard it the same as before. “Twiⁿ, twiⁿ,” (2) was the sound he heard.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gomaapii dash miinawaa nandotam (and after a while again he listened), mii geyaapi enitang (and still he heard it the same). “Dwiinh dwiinh,” (2) initam (such was the sound he heard).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(30) Mī∙i∙mā gwaya‘k ändạnitạng pimidēpināgwatinig a‘ki.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii imaa gwayak endanitang bi-midii bi-naagwadinig aki.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Straight from yonder place where the land was coming into view he heard the sound.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii imaa gwayak endanitang (from straight over there he heard it) bi-midii bi-naagwadinig aki (where the land was coming into view).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(31) Mīnạngwana kägä‘t iᵘ kā∙i∙jinạng.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii nangwana geget iw gaa-izhinang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*It turned out truly to be (the island) that he had seen.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii nangwana geget iw (it turned out truly to be that) gaa-izhinang (which he had seen).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(32) Mīdạc minawā mādcāt, cayīgwa owâbạndān wäwäni a‘ki; mīdạc minawā äji∙ạ∙ndutạnk, mī minawā nōndạnk.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash miinawaa maajaad, zhayiigwa owaabandaan weweni aki; mii dash miinawaa ezhi-nandotang, mii miinawaa noondang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And now, as he continued on, he presently saw the land in plain sight; thereupon again he listened, and then again he heard the sound.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash miinawaa maajaad (and so again he went on), zhayiigwa owaabandaan weweni aki (now he saw the land plainly); mii dash miinawaa ezhi-nandotang (and so again he listened), mii miinawaa noondang (and again he heard it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(33) “Twiⁿ twiⁿ,” ini‘tạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Dwiinh dwiinh,” (3) initam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Twiⁿ, twiⁿ,” (3) was what he heard.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Dwiinh dwiinh,” (3) initam (such was what he heard).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(34) Mīgu äjikuckwāgạmisäg nibi äpītcikicīwägạnādạnk iᵘ uga‘kwạn aᵘ mạnidō.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii go ezhi-goshkwaagamiseg nibi epiichi-gizhiiweganaadang iw ogakwan aw manidoo.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then the water trembled, so loud was the manitou hewing upon his shin.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii go ezhi-goshkwaagamiseg nibi (and then the water trembled — *goshkwaagamise- stem flagged*) epiichi-gizhiiweganaadang iw ogakwan (so loud was he hewing upon his shin — *gizhiiweganaad- stem flagged*) aw manidoo (that manidoo).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(35) Mīdac kīwäⁿ Nänabucu cayīgwa ạnitcägisät imā kwaya‘k ändạni‘tạnk; kägä‘t änigạbāt owâbạndān undạmuninik mī‘kana.</span>
