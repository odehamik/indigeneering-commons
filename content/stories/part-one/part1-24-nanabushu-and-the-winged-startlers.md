24\. NÄNABUSHU AND THE WINGED STARTLERS.¹

Nänabushu and the Winged Startlers (2)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by probably Midāsugaⁿj (Michelson’s own hedge, carried) — Series 2 (Nos. 19–32), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīsa’ iᵘ äjimādcād Nänabucu pạpimusät;</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa iw ezhi-maajaad Nenaboozhoo babimosed;**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon departed Nänabushu, travelling about;*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa iw (and so it was) ezhi-maajaad Nenaboozhoo (that Nenaboozhoo set out) babimosed (walking about).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) kumāgu a‘pī täkucink owâbạmāᵉ pinäⁿsaᵉ wạdiswạning, ā‘pidcigu mōckinäwaᵉ i∙i∙mā wasiswạning.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**gomaago apii degoshing owaabamaa’ binensa’ wadiswaning, aapiji go mooshkinewa’ i’imaa wadiswaning.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*when he was come a certain distance, he saw some young ruffed rouse in a nest, and very full they filled the place in the nest.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gomaago apii (after a certain distance) degoshing (when he arrived) owaabamaa’ binensa’ (he saw some young birds — little ruffed grouse) wadiswaning (in a nest), aapiji go mooshkinewa’ (very full did they fill it) i’imaa wadiswaning (there in the nest).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Nänabucu udunạbī‘tawā, ā‘pidci ucawänimāᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenaboozhoo odonabiitawaa, aapiji ozhawenimaa’.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Nänabushu sat down beside them, very tender was his feeling for them.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nenaboozhoo odonabiitawaa’ (Nenaboozhoo sat down beside them), aapiji ozhawenimaa’ (very tender was his feeling toward them).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Udạgimā ändạcinint; midāswi ạcinīⁿjtạciwaᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Odagimaa endashinind; midaaswi-ashi-niizh dashiwa’.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He counted how many they were ; twelve was their number.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Odagimaa’ endashinind (he counted how many they were); midaaswi-ashi-niizh dashiwa’ (twelve was their number).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Cigwadạc ugạnōnāᵉ:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa dash oganoonaa’:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then he spoke to them, asking:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa dash oganoonaa’ (and now he spoke to them):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) “Ānīn äcini‘kāsuyäg?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniin ezhinikaazoyeg?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“By what name are you called?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniin ezhinikaazoyeg? (How are you called?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Ānīc sägisiwạg īgi’ᵘ pinäⁿsạg.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish zegiziwag igiw binensag.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Naturally afraid were the little ruffed grouse.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish zegiziwag (naturally they were afraid) igiw binensag (those little grouse).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Kāwīn ugạcki‘tōsīnāwāᵉ tcigīkitōwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin ogashkitoosiinaawaa’ ji-giigidowaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Not were they able to speak.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin ogashkitoosiinaawaa’ (they were not able) ji-giigidowaad (to speak).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Pācīg gīkitō: “Kāwīn niwīsusīmin.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Bezhig giigido: “Gaawiin ni-wiinzosiimin.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*One spoke up: “We have no name.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Bezhig giigido (one spoke up): “Gaawiin ni-wiinzosiimin (we have no name).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Nänabucu niskātcikīgitō:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenaboozhoo niskaaji-giigidoo:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Nänabushu spoke in an angry way:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nenaboozhoo niskaaji-giigidoo (Nenaboozhoo spoke angrily):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) “Ānīn kägīcināgwa‘k käwīsusiwäg?</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniin gegii zhinaagwag ge-wiinzosiweg?**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“How is it possible for you not to have a name?*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniin gegii zhinaagwag (how can it be) ge-wiinzosiweg? (that you would have no name?)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) Kīcpîn tibātcimusiwäg äcini‘kāsuyäg, kạnānīwạnu∙i∙nim.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Giishpin dibaajimosiweg e-zhinikaazoyeg, ganaaniiwano’inim.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*If you do not tell me what you are called, I will club you to death.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Giishpin dibaajimosiweg (if you do not tell) e-zhinikaazoyeg (what you are called), ganaaniiwano’inim (I will club you to death).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) Ānīc ā‘pidci osägimāᵉ; wī‘kā kīgitōwaᵉ:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish aapiji ozegimaa’; wiikaa giigidowa’:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Naturally much did he alarm them ; after a long while they said:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish aapiji ozegimaa’ (naturally he frightened them greatly); wiikaa giigidowa’ (after a long while they spoke):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) “Ānīc, kuckungäcīns mī∙i∙ᵘ äjini‘kāsoyāng.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniish, goshkongeshiins mii i’iw e-zhinikaazoyaang.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Why, Little Frightener is the name we are called.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniish, goshkongeshiins (why, Little Frightener) mii i’iw e-zhinikaazoyaang (is what we are called).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) “Ōᵘⁿ,” Nänabucu i‘kitu; “mīnạngwana iᵘ!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Oonh,” Nenaboozhoo ikido; “mii nangwana iw!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Oh,” Nänabushu said; “that is it!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Oonh,” Nenaboozhoo ikido (“Oh,” said Nenaboozhoo); “mii nangwana iw! (so that’s it!)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) äjipạsigwīt Nänabucu;</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**ezhi-pazigwiid Nenaboozhoo;**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then up to his feet rose Nänabushu ;*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ezhi-pazigwiid Nenaboozhoo (then Nenaboozhoo rose to his feet);</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) äcinisawawāt äcimīdcinät.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ezhi-nisawawaad ezhi-miijinaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*standing over them with legs spread apart, he eased himself upon them.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ezhi-nisawawaad (standing over them with legs apart) ezhi-miijinaad (he relieved himself upon them).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) Pạnägu kāwạsibi‘tōnit, Nänabucu ugạnōnāᵉ:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Bane go gaawasibitoonid, Nenaboozhoo oganoonaa’:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*(Observing) them suddenly groping about in the slush, Nänabushu addressed them, saying:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Bane go gaawasibitoonid (as they floundered about in it), Nenaboozhoo oganoonaa’ (Nenaboozhoo spoke to them):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(19) “Nāpisawugu kuckungäcīns!² Mᵘⁿ!” inwä Nänabucu äjigi‘tcibā‘pi∙ā∙t.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Naapisawogo goshkongeshiins!(2) Mwn!” inwe Nenaboozhoo ezhi-gichi-baapi’aad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Yes, you are a little frightener!(2) Phew!” exclaimed Nänabushu, laughing heartily at them.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Naapisawogo goshkongeshiins!(2) (Yes indeed, a little frightener you are!) Mwn! (Phew!)” inwe Nenaboozhoo (so said Nenaboozhoo) ezhi-gichi-baapi’aad (laughing hard at them).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(20) “Wäwäni wīndamawi‘k kimāmā∙i∙wā tagwicing.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Weweni wiindamawik gi-maamaa’iwaa dagwishing.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Correctly inform your mother when she arrives.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Weweni wiindamawik (rightly tell her) gi-maamaa’iwaa (your mother) dagwishing (when she arrives).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(21) Mīsạgu iᵘ änīcimādcāt Nänabucu;</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa go iw e-ni-izhi-maajaad Nenaboozhoo;**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so upon his way went Nänabushu;*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa go iw (and so it was) e-ni-izhi-maajaad Nenaboozhoo (that Nenaboozhoo went on his way);</span>

<span class="l-orig">(22) päcugu änitạgwicing, panägu micawābạmināgwạtini ä∙i∙cāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Besho go eni-dagwishing, bane go mishawaabaminaagwadini e-izhaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*when a little way on his journey he was come, immediately a wide view opened out, whither he was bound.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Besho go eni-dagwishing (a little way along when he arrived), bane go mishawaabaminaagwadini (suddenly a wide view opened out) e-izhaad (where he was headed).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(23) “Undcitạmāwīn sāga∙i∙gạn mädābīyān,” inändạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Onjida maawiin zaaga’igan medaabiiyaan,” inendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“It seems as if out upon a lake I am coming,” he thought.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Onjida maawiin (it must be on purpose) zaaga’igan medaabiiyaan (that I come out upon a lake),” inendam (he thought).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(24) Kägä‘t sāga∙i∙gạn umạdābīn, ā‘pidcisa’ kīckābi‘kāni.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Geget zaaga’igan omadaabiin, aapiji sa giishkaabikaani.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*In truth, out upon a lake he came, and there was a very steep precipice.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Geget zaaga’igan omadaabiin (truly he came out upon a lake), aapiji sa giishkaabikaani (and there was a very steep cliff).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(25) Kägä‘t nāwināgwạtini ‘iᵉi’ᵘ nibi.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Geget naawinaagwadini i’iw nibi.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Truly distant was the sight of the water.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Geget naawinaagwadini (truly far down it appeared) i’iw nibi (that water).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(26) Kägätsa unicicinini mī∙i∙mā ugidābi‘k.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Geget sa onizhishinini mii i’imaa ogidaabik.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Really beautiful was it there on the summit.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Geget sa onizhishinini (truly it was beautiful) mii i’imaa ogidaabik (there on the top of the rock).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(27) A‘pidci i∙i∙mā ägōkīckābi‘kānig kā∙i∙cāt, äciwānīnigātänit Nänabucu äjikīgitut:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aapiji i’imaa egookiishkaabikaanig gaa-izhaad, ezhi-waaniinigaadenid Nenaboozhoo ezhi-giigidod:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When he had gone over to the very edge of the cliff, then about over the verge Nänabushu swung his leg, saying :*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aapiji i’imaa egookiishkaabikaanig (right there at the edge of the cliff) gaa-izhaad (when he had gone), ezhi-waaniinigaadenid Nenaboozhoo (Nenaboozhoo swung his leg out over) ezhi-giigidod (and said):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(28) “A‘tiwä, ạmbägicsa a‘tāting, māmwä‘tcigu wäckinīgit a∙i∙‘kwä a‘tāting, kayägu ta‘tā‘kutcingwạnät, ā‘pidcigu kayä minugit;</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Etiwe, ambegish sa ataading, maamwechi go weshkiniigid ikwe ataading, gaye go dataakojiingwaned, aapiji go gaye minogid;**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Ah, would that there were a wager, and that a particularly youthful woman were up as the prize, and that she were short from the knee to the groin, and that she were of a very handsome figure !*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Etiwe (ah), ambegish sa ataading (would that there were a wager), maamwechi go weshkiniigid ikwe (especially a youthful woman) ataading (set up as the stake), gaye go dataakojiingwaned (and that she be short from knee to groin), aapiji go gaye minogid (and very fine of figure too);</span>

<span class="l-orig">(29) undcitạmāwin nintā∙i∙cikwāskun, awägwän kä∙i∙cikwäskunigwän mī∙a’∙ᵘāk äwīdigämāt i‘kitung a∙i∙‘kwä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**onjida maawiin nindaa-izhigwaashgon, awegwen ge-izhigwaashgonigwen mii a’aw ge-wiidigemaad ikidong ikwe.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*if such could be, I would jump off, if it were said of the woman that whosoever would leap off would be the one to have her for a wife.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">onjida maawiin nindaa-izhigwaashgon (on purpose I would jump off), awegwen ge-izhigwaashgonigwen (whoever would leap off) mii a’aw ge-wiidigemaad (he would be the one to marry her) ikidong ikwe (the woman, it is said).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(30) Kwaya‘kigu nintā∙i∙cikwāskun.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gwayak igo nindaa-izhigwaashgon.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Actually would I jump off.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gwayak igo nindaa-izhigwaashgon (truly I would jump off).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(31) Mīgu mīnawā äciwānīnigātänit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii go miinawaa ezhi-waaniinigaadenid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so again he swung his leg out over the cliff.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii go miinawaa ezhi-waaniinigaadenid (and so again he swung his leg out over).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(32) “Undcita nintā∙i∙cikwāskun.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Onjida nindaa-izhigwaashgon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“For a purpose would I leap off.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Onjida nindaa-izhigwaashgon (on purpose I would leap off).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(33) Cigwạdạc iwiti pinä upītawāᵉ kämīdcinit unīdcānisaᵉ, ạnīn kä∙i∙cinawāt unīdcānisaᵉ!</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa dash iwidi bine obiidawaa’ ge-miijinid oniijaanisa’, aaniin ge-izhinawaad oniijaanisa’!**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when at yonder place the Ruffed Grouse was come, fetching home to her children some food for them to eat, how was she to find her children !*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa dash iwidi bine obiidawaa’ (and now over there the Ruffed Grouse brought to them) ge-miijinid oniijaanisa’ (food for her children to eat), aaniin ge-izhinawaad oniijaanisa’! (how was she to find her children!)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(34) Ä‘tawā, wäntạgu kāwusibi‘tōnit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Etawaa, wendago gaawosibitoonid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Oh, they were completely submerged in it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Etawaa (alas), wendago gaawosibitoonid (utterly were they buried in it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(35) “Awänäc kā∙i∙nigā∙i∙näk.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Awenesh gaa-inigaa’ineg.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Who has done you the injury?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Awenesh gaa-inigaa’ineg (who has harmed you)?”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(36) “Nänabucu.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Nenaboozhoo.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Nänabushu.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Nenaboozhoo.”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(37) “Kägōna kitigōwâbạn?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gegoo na gidigoowaaban?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Were you told something?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gegoo na gidigoowaaban? (were you told something?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(38) “‘Ānīn äcini‘kāsuyäg?’ i‘kito.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniin e-zhinikaazoyeg? ikido.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“By what are you called?’ he asked.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniin e-zhinikaazoyeg? (How are you called?) ikido (he said).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(39) ‘Kuskungäcīns,’ nintinānābạn, mīdạc ‘iᵉi’ᵘ kā∙i∙cimīdcicīyạngit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**‘Goshkongeshiins,’ nindinaanaaban, mii dash i’iw gaa-izhi-miijishiiyangid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*‘Little Frightener,’ we said to him, and thereupon was when we were eased upon.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">‘Goshkongeshiins (Little Frightener),’ nindinaanaaban (we said to him), mii dash i’iw gaa-izhi-miijishiiyangid (and that was when he relieved himself upon us).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(40) A‘pidạc änimādcāt ki‘tcibā‘pī∙i∙gunān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii dash eni-maajaad gichi-baapi’igonaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when he started away, we were very much laughed at.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii dash eni-maajaad (and when he was leaving) gichi-baapi’igonaan (he laughed at us greatly).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(41) Ningīkạnōnigunān dạc: ‘Wäwäni wīndamawi‘k kimāma∙i∙wā tạgwicin.’</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningii-kanoonigonaan dash: ‘Weweni wiindamawik gi-maama’iwaa dagwishin.’**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And we were told : Correctly inform your mother when she arrives.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningii-kanoonigonaan dash (and he spoke to us): ‘Weweni wiindamawik (rightly tell) gi-maama’iwaa (your mother) dagwishin (when she arrives).’</span>

<span class="l-orig">(42) Mīdạc ‘iᵉi’ᵘ kā∙ạ∙nicimādcāt äjikīgitut.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash i’iw gaa-ani-zhi-maajaad ezhi-giigidod.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so when he was setting out, he then said:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash i’iw gaa-ani-zhi-maajaad (and so when he was setting out) ezhi-giigidod (he said):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(43) ‘Näpisạnä kuskungäcīns!’ nintigunān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**‘Nepisane goshkongeshiins!’ nindigonaan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Yes, you are a little frightener! we were told.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">‘Nepisane goshkongeshiins! (Yes indeed, a little frightener!)’ nindigonaan (we were told).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(44) Ā‘tayä, pinä unawạtạmān päcig kayä udōdä‘pinān päcig;</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ataaya, bine onawadamaan bezhig gaye odoodebinaan bezhig;**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Well, the Ruffed Grouse took up one with her mouth and another with her claws ,*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ataaya (well), bine onawadamaan bezhig (the Ruffed Grouse took up one in her mouth) gaye odoodebinaan bezhig (and another with her claws);</span>

<span class="l-orig">(45) äcipạsigu∙u∙t, sāga∙i∙gạnīng udiciwinān kisībīgināt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ezhi-bazigo’od, zaaga’iganing odizhiwinaan giziibiiginaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*then, flying up, to a lake she carried them to wash them.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ezhi-bazigo’od (then flying up), zaaga’iganing odizhiwinaan (to a lake she carried them) giziibiiginaad (to wash them).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(46) Misa iᵘ kā∙i∙cipīni∙ā∙t,</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa iw gaa-izhi-biini’aad,**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when she had made them clean,*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa iw gaa-izhi-biini’aad (and so when she had made them clean),</span>
