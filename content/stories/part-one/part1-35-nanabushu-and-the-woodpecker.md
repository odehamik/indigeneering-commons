35\. NÄNABUSHU AND THE WOODPECKER.¹

Nenabosho and the Woodpecker (1)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by probably Midāsugaⁿj (Michelson’s own hedge, carried) — Series 3 (Nos. 33–38), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Cigwa pa‘kadäwạg ā‘pidci, mīsa’ äcimādcāt Nänabucu.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa bakadewag aapiji, mii sa ezhi-maajaad Nenabosho.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So were they much in need of food, whereupon off went Nänabushu.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa bakadewag aapiji (now they were very hungry), mii sa ezhi-maajaad Nenabosho (and so off went Nenabosho).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Cigwa, mīnawā udiciwä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa, miinawaa odishiwe.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, again was he a-visiting.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa, miinawaa odishiwe (now, again he went visiting).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Mīsa’ mīnawā äcinawāt ạnicinābäᵉ, nīciwaᵉ unīdcānisini.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa miinawaa ezhi-nawaad anishinaabe, niizhiwag oniijaanisini.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so again they whom he saw seemed like people, (and) they had two children.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa miinawaa ezhi-nawaad anishinaabe (and so again the ones he saw were like people), niizhiwag oniijaanisini (and they had two children).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Kāwīn kägō wâbạndạmawāsīn; wä‘kwāgān ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ändānit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin gegoo waabandamawaasiin; wekwaagaan i’iw endaanid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He saw nothing of what they had; a long lodge they occupied for a home.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin gegoo waabandamawaasiin (he saw nothing of what they had); wekwaagaan i’iw endaanid (a long lodge it was that they lived in).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) “Wägutugwänigic mādcīwāgwän!” inändạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Wegodogwenigish maajiiwaagwen!” inendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Wonder what in the world they eat!” he thought.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Wegodogwenigish maajiiwaagwen!” (“I wonder what in the world they eat!”) inendam (he thought).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) “Cigwa, ānīndạcnā iᵘ kīgä∙ā∙siwạnk ‘aᵉa’ᵘ kipīwitäminān?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Zhigwa, aaniin dash naa iw giige-aasiwang a’aw gibiiwideminaan?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Now, what had we better feed our visitor?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Zhigwa, aaniin dash naa iw (“now, what then) giige-aasiwang a’aw gibiiwideminaan?” (shall we feed our visitor?”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Mīsa’ kägä‘t cigwa ujī‘tāwạn īni’ᵘ ininiwạn; wawäci∙ō∙wạn uka‘tigwạning, udicicimāni īni’ᵘ miskōsānạmạnạn; mīnawā udōntcibitōnini pīwâbi‘k ā‘pidci kācibōtäni.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa geget zhigwa ozhiitaawan iiniw ininiwan; wawezhi’oowan ogatigwaning, odishishimaani iiniw miskoosaanamanan; miinawaa odoonjibidoonini biiwaabik aapiji gaa-zhi-boodeni.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon truly the man began getting ready; he painted himself on the forehead, the kind of paint he used was the red; furthermore, he took up a piece of metal, with a very keen edge due to filing.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa geget zhigwa ozhiitaawan iiniw ininiwan (and so truly now the man got ready); wawezhi’oowan ogatigwaning (he adorned himself on the forehead), odishishimaani iiniw miskoosaanamanan (the red paint he laid on); miinawaa odoonjibidoonini biiwaabik (and he took up a piece of metal) aapiji gaa-zhi-boodeni (filed to a very keen edge — *boodeni stem flagged*).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Ānīc, unisitawinān äyāwạninig, mīnangwạna ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ijictcigäwāt īni’ᵘ o‘kunāsanwīn ² cägunānit i∙i∙mā ucạngwanāning ānīc mägwāgu kīnạmạdạbinit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish, onisitawinaan eyaawaninig, miinangwana i’iw izhichigewaad iiniw okonaasanwiin(2) zhegonaanid i’imaa oshangwanaaning aaniish megwaa go gii-namadabinid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, (Nänabushu) recognized what it was, for the thing that they did was to put the bone-pointed spear(2) into the nostrils while he was seated there.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish, onisitawinaan eyaawaninig (now, he recognized what it was), miinangwana i’iw izhichigewaad (it turned out that what they did) iiniw okonaasanwiin(2) zhegonaanid i’imaa oshangwanaaning (was to set the bone-pointed spear into his nostrils) aaniish megwaa go gii-namadabinid (while he sat there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) A‘pī kā‘kīci∙u∙nit, pānimāgu wạndcinawạtinānit īni’ᵘ mi‘tigōn päta‘kisunit i∙i∙mā ändānit; nōndāgusiwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii gaa-giizhi’onid, baanimaa go wanjinawatinaanid iiniw mitigoon bedakisonid i’imaa endaanid; noondaagosiwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When he had finished painting himself, he suddenly made for a post that was standing there where they lived; the sound of his cry could be heard.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii gaa-giizhi’onid (when he had finished adorning himself), baanimaa go wanjinawatinaanid iiniw mitigoon (suddenly he sprang at a post) bedakisonid i’imaa endaanid (that stood there where they lived); noondaagosiwan (the sound of his voice was heard).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) “Ku‘ku’k, ku’‘kuk!” inwäwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Kokok, kokok!” inwewan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Ku’‘kuk, ku’‘kuk!” was his cry.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Kokok, kokok!” (vocable, carried as printed) inwewan (such was his cry).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Ānīc inābit Nänabucu, mämän owâbạmān; cigwa utạni kwä‘kwänibâbạmāni īni’ᵘ mi‘tigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish inaabid Nenabosho, memen owaabamaan; zhigwa odanigwekwenibaabamaani iiniw mitigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, when Nänabushu looked, he saw the red-head (woodpecker); and (the bird) kept busily nodding his head to see where to find something on the post.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish inaabid Nenabosho (now, when Nenabosho looked), memen owaabamaan (he saw the red-head woodpecker); zhigwa odanigwekwenibaabamaani iiniw mitigoon (and he kept bobbing his head along, eyeing the post).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) Cigu‘ku’ udạnikutcisạgạwāni; mī‘ku äcīgu‘kwäninit i∙i∙mā tcīgā‘tig.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigo ko odanikojisagawaani; mii go ezhi-gokweninid(3) i’imaa jiigaatig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*At last he began to try pecking the post; and occasionally, ceasing from his work, he held his head close to the post.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigo ko odanikojisagawaani (at last he started pecking up along the post); mii go ezhi-gokweninid(3) i’imaa jiigaatig (and he would tip his head close to the post — *gokweni stem flagged; cf. footnote 3*).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) Anīc, pīnicigu i∙i∙witi cpiming ạni∙a∙yāwạn, apī‘tcināg īni’ᵘ cayīgwa mādci‘kwä∙i∙gäwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish, biinish igo i’iwidi shpiming ani-ayaawan, abijiinag (abiijinaag) iiniw zhayiigwa maajikwe’igewan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, by degrees he began making his way upward, every now and then he would begin pecking away.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish, biinish igo i’iwidi shpiming ani-ayaawan (now, little by little he worked his way up high), abijiinag (abiijinaag) iiniw zhayiigwa maajikwe’igewan (every now and then he would start pecking away).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) Mīdạc ningutingiku, ini‘kwänininit, äsibạnạn pimiba‘tōwạn, mīsa’ minawā päjik pimibisōwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash ningoding igo, inikwenininid, esibanan bimibatoowan, mii sa miinawaa bezhig bimibisoowan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so by and by, as he pecked, (Nänabushu saw) a raccoon come running (out), and then another came falling down.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash ningoding igo, inikwenininid (and so by and by, as he pecked), esibanan bimibatoowan (a raccoon came running out), mii sa miinawaa bezhig bimibisoowan (and then another came falling down).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) Mīsa’ nīⁿj kīnisāt ‘iᵉi’ᵘ äsibạnaᵉ, pā∙i∙jinīsā‘kwacimōnunit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa niizh gii-nisaad i’iw esibana, baa-izhi-niisaakwashimoononid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so, after killing the raccoons, he came down from the post.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa niizh gii-nisaad i’iw esibana (and so, after he killed the two raccoons), baa-izhi-niisaakwashimoononid (he came down from the post).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) Mīsa’ äcimạmōnit i∙i∙mā kā∙a∙‘tōnit ucạngwanāning ‘iᵉi’ᵘ pīwâbi‘k.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa ezhi-mamoonid i’imaa gaa-atoonid oshangwanaaning i’iw biiwaabik.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon he removed the metal which he had placed there in his nose.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa ezhi-mamoonid i’imaa gaa-atoonid oshangwanaaning i’iw biiwaabik (thereupon he took out the metal he had put there in his nose).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) Mīsa’ äcigạba‘tawint ‘iᵉi’ᵘ äsibạnaᵉ; cigwa ạgwā∙ạ∙mawā tciwīsinit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa ezhi-gabadawind i’iw esibana; zhigwa agwaa’amawaa ji-wiisinid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then the raccoons were cooked for (Nänabushu); then they were dipped out for him to eat.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa ezhi-gabadawind i’iw esibana (and then the raccoons were boiled for him); zhigwa agwaa’amawaa ji-wiisinid (then they were dipped out for him to eat).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) Mīsa’ cigwa’ kīwīsinit, cigwa’ kạnōnā: “Ānīc mīsa’ kä∙i∙jikīwäwitawatwā kinītcānisạg mini‘k äckwạntcigäyạn,” kī∙i∙nā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa zhigwa gii-wiisinid, zhigwa ganoonaa; “Aaniish mii sa  ge-zhi-kiiwewidawadwaa giniijaanisag minik eshkwanjigeyan,” gii-inaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so then was he eating when he was addressed: “Now, therefore, do you carry back to your children as much as you do not eat,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa zhigwa gii-wiisinid, zhigwa ganoonaa (and so when he was eating, he was spoken to); “Aaniish mii sa ge-zhi-kiiwewidawadwaa giniijaanisag (“now then, carry back to your children) minik eshkwanjigeyan,” (as much as you leave uneaten,”) gii-inaa (he was told).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(19) Mīsa’ mīnawā, wạnimō‘k, ᵉīgu∙i∙mā kā∙u∙ndcicägōntạgināt mīnawā ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ugīcō‘tä∙u∙naᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa miinawaa wanimook, igo imaa gaa-onji-shegoondaginaad miinawaa i’iw ogii-shoode’ona.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon again, without being seen, he slipped his mittens (in the balsam boughs under the mat).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa miinawaa wanimook (thereupon again, unseen), igo imaa gaa-onji-shegoondaginaad miinawaa i’iw ogii-shoode’ona (he tucked away his mittens there — *shegoond-/shoode’on stem flagged*).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(20) Mīsa’ mīnawā ạnimādcāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa miinawaa ani-maajaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So then again he set out on his way.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa miinawaa ani-maajaad (and so again he set out).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(21) Pānimāgu mạdwäpīpāgi, “Aⁱ!” mädwä∙i∙‘kitowạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Baanimaa go madwe-piibaagi, “Ay!” medwe’ikidowan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*After a while he heard somebody calling aloud: “Hey!” he heard some one saying.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Baanimaa go madwe-piibaagi (after a while a voice could be heard calling), “Ay!” medwe’ikidowan (“Hey!” someone was heard saying).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(22) “Kiwạnicigāgomä!” mạdwäi‘kitowạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Giwanishigaagome!” madwe-ikidowan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“You have forgotten something!” he heard him say.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Giwanishigaagome!” (“you’ve forgotten something!”) madwe-ikidowan (he was heard saying).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(23) “Ānīc, wägutugwänigic?” i‘kituwạn ininiwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniish, wegodogwenigish?” ikidowan ininiwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Why, what in the world is it?”(4) said the man.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniish, wegodogwenigish?” (“why, what in the world is it?” — *dubitative; cf. footnote 4*) ikidowan ininiwan (said the man).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(24) Mīnạngwạna guca kägä‘t kābunit ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ugīcōtä∙u∙naᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Miinangwana gosha geget gaabonid i’iw ogiizhoode’ona.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*It was really true that where (Nänabushu) had been were his mittens.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Miinangwana gosha geget gaabonid i’iw ogiizhoode’ona (it turned out, sure enough, that his mittens were left where he had been).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(25) “Wâsạgu undciwäbinạmawi‘k.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Waasa go onji-webinamawik.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“From afar do you fling them to him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Waasa go onji-webinamawik (“from far off fling them to him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(26) Kägu’ wīn i∙i∙witi päcocā‘kägun,” udināᵉ ‘iᵉi’ᵘ unīdcānisaᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gego wiin i’iwidi beshoshaakegon,” odinaa i’iw oniijaanisa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Do not go near to him,” he said to (his boys).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gego wiin i’iwidi beshoshaakegon,” (don’t go near him over there,”) odinaa i’iw oniijaanisa (he said to his children).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(27) “Käya‘ku’ täbiwäbinạmawäg mī∙i∙mā a‘pī undciwäbinạmawi‘k,” udināᵉ ‘iᵉi’ᵘ unīdcānisaᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaye go debiwebinamaweg mii imaa apii onji-webinamawik,” odinaa i’iw oniijaanisa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“And from as far as you can throw is the distance you fling them to him,” he said to his children.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gaye go debiwebinamaweg mii imaa apii onji-webinamawik,” (“and from as far as you can throw, that is where you fling them to him,”) odinaa i’iw oniijaanisa (he said to his children).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(28) Ạnicā’ kägä‘t cigwa’ o∙ō’∙wīdac udināᵉ ‘iᵉi’ᵘ unīdcānisaᵉ: “Kāwīn ningutinō kīgabwā i∙gusīwā.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Anishaa geget zhigwa o’ow idash odinaa i’iw oniijaanisa: “Gaawiin ningodinoo gii-ga-bwaa’igosiiwaa.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, really, for fun only did he say this to his children: “In no way will he refrain from saying something to you.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Anishaa geget zhigwa o’ow idash odinaa i’iw oniijaanisa (now, truly just in jest he said this to his children): “Gaawiin ningodinoo gii-ga-bwaa’igosiiwaa.” (“in no way will he refrain from saying something to you.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(29) Cigwasa’ kägä‘t udānawi∙a∙‘pạgitawāwān, o∙ō’∙widac udināᵉ Nänabucu: “Kägu’ kāwạnāgunäwäbināwāg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa sa geget odaanawi-apagidawaawaan, o’ow idash odinaa Nenabosho: “Gego gaawanaagonewebinaawaag.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When they were really in earnest about throwing them to him, then this Nänabushu said to them: “Do not fling them, lest they become lost in the snow.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa sa geget odaanawi-apagidawaawaan (now, truly when they were set to throw them to him), o’ow idash odinaa Nenabosho (this Nenabosho said to them): “Gego gaawanaagonewebinaawaag.” (“don’t fling them, lest they be lost in the snow.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(30) Mīdạc kägä‘t kā∙i∙cimīnāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget gaa-izhi-miinaawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Whereupon they truly went and handed them to him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget gaa-izhi-miinaawaad (whereupon truly they handed them to him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(31) Ānawi pimādci ba∙i∙tiwạg īgi’ᵘ kwīwicäncạg kā∙i∙ciināt: “A‘kawä, i∙i∙mā ayāyu‘k!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aanawi bimaajiba’idiwag iigiw gwiiwizhenshag gaa-izhi-inaad: “Akawe, i’imaa ayaayog!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*In the act of starting to run away were the boys when (this) he said to them: “Hold, wait there!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aanawi bimaajiba’idiwag iigiw gwiiwizhenshag (the boys were already starting to run off) gaa-izhi-inaad: “Akawe, i’imaa ayaayog!” (when he said to them: “Hold on, stay there!”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(32) Oᵉo’dạc udināᵉ: “Kägä‘tisa’ kiba‘kadämwāsa,” udināᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**O’ow dash odinaa: “Geget isa gibakademwaasa,” odinaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And this he said to them: “Really, you must be pretty hungry,” he said to them.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">O’ow dash odinaa (and this he said to them): “Geget isa gibakademwaasa,” (“truly, you must be quite hungry,”) odinaa (he said to them).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(33) “Mī guca wīni iᵘ päbātạcī‘käwāt īgi’ᵘ kītcikiwäⁿ∙i∙wāg; mīgu iᵘ pimitä päpā∙o∙pạgạmāgạniwāt päpātacī‘käwāt īgi’ᵘ kītcikiwäⁿ∙i∙wāg, ‘iᵉi’ᵘ maskawatcipimitä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii gosha wiini iw bebaadaashiikewaad iigiw giijikiwe’iwaag; mii go  bimide bebaa’opagamaaganiwaad bebaadashiikewaad iigiw giijikiweniwaag, i’iw mashkawajibimide.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“That is exactly the way of your brothers wherever they are; it is grease that your brothers give so bountifully wherever they are, the hard frozen grease.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Mii gosha wiini iw bebaadaashiikewaad iigiw giijikiwe’iwaag (“that is just the way of your brothers wherever they go about); mii go bimide bebaa’opagamaaganiwaad bebaadashiikewaad iigiw giijikiweniwaag (it is grease your brothers hand out as they go about), i’iw mashkawajibimide (the hard-frozen grease).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(34) Ạmbäsinō, a‘pī wâbạng kōsiwā tapī∙i∙cā,” udināᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ambe sa noo, apii waabang koosiwaa da-bii-izhaa,” odinaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Pray, when the morrow comes, let your father come over,” he said to them.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ambe sa noo, apii waabang koosiwaa da-bii-izhaa,” (“come now, when tomorrow comes let your father come over,”) odinaa (he said to them).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(35) Mīsa’ änicimādcāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa eni-maajaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then on his way he went.*</span>
