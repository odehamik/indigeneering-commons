59\. NÄNABUSHU FINDS CRANBERRIES AND BIG CHERRIES.

Nanabushu finds Cranberries and Big Cherries

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Mrs. Syrette — Series 8 (Nos. 57–63), Fort William (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīdạc kägä‘t mindimōyä kā∙i∙jikuntạnk usi‘kwāgạn, mīdạc minawā kīⁿwäⁿ Nänabucu ạnibabimiba‘tōd änigu‘k; wīngä cayīgwa ā‘pidci aiyä‘kusi, mīgu kā∙i∙jipimiba‘tōd kījigạtînig kayä tibi‘kạtinig.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget mindimooye gaa-izhi-gondang ozikwaagan, mii dash miinawaa giiwenh Nenaboozhoo ani-babimibatood enigok; wiinge zhayiigwa aapiji ayekozi, mii go gaa-izhi-bimibatood giizhigadinig gaye dibikadinig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so truly, after the old woman had swallowed her spittle, then again they say that Nanabushu went running along at the top of his speed; ever so tired had he now become, for he had been running day and night.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget (and so truly) gaa-izhi-gondang ozikwaagan mindimooye (after the old woman had swallowed her spittle), mii dash miinawaa giiwenh (then again, it is told) Nenaboozhoo ani-babimibatood enigok (Nanabushu went running along at full speed); wiinge zhayiigwa aapiji ayekozi (utterly worn out he now was), mii go gaa-izhi-bimibatood (for he had been running) giizhigadinig gaye dibikadinig (by day and by night).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Ningudingidạcigu ạnibabimiba‘tōd Nänabucu tibi‘katinigubạn, mīdạc kīⁿwäⁿ änändạnk: “Mīmāwīn cayīgwa wâsa tagwicinōwānän,” inändạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding idash igo ani-babimibatood Nenaboozhoo dibikadinigoban, mii dash giiwenh e-nendang: “Mii maawiin zhayiigwa waasa dagwishinoowaanen,” inendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And once while Nanabushu was running along, it happened to be in the night, whereupon it is said that he thought : “It is plain that I must now have come a long way,” he thought.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding idash igo (and once) ani-babimibatood Nenaboozhoo (while Nanabushu was running along) dibikadinigoban (it being night), mii dash giiwenh e-nendang (whereupon, it is told, he thought): “Mii maawiin zhayiigwa (“it must be that by now) waasa dagwishinoowaanen,” (I have come a long way,”) inendam (he thought).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Nawạtcidạcigu anīgakipi‘tciba‘tō; mägwā dạc pabimusät, kägō uda‘ku‘kātānạn, tibickō asînīⁿsạg.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nawaj idash igo aniigakipichibatoo; megwaa dash babimosed, gegoo odakokaadaanan, dibishkoo asiniinsag.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Slower then he ran; and while he was walking along, something he then stepped upon, it seemed like pebbles.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nawaj idash igo aniigakipichibatoo (slower then he ran); megwaa dash babimosed (and while he was walking along), gegoo odakokaadaanan (something he stepped upon with his foot), dibishkoo asiniinsag (it seemed like little stones).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Mīdạc ạnimānạk äji∙ạ∙ndōtcipitōd; mīdạc äjimi‘kōtcīnạng, udā‘pinạng.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash animaanag ezhi-andoojiibidood; mii dash ezhi-mikoojiinang, odaapinang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so, not making out what they were, he felt of them; and as he felt of them, he took them up.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash animaanag (and so, puzzled by them) ezhi-andoojiibidood (he felt about for them by hand); mii dash ezhi-mikoojiinang (and as he found them by touch), odaapinang (he took them up).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) “Nō‘komis, naskä kuca owä!</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Nookomis, naske gosha owe!**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“O my grandmother, do look at these!*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Nookomis (“my grandmother), naske gosha owe! (just look at this!)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Wägunän owä?” udinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Wegonen owe?” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*What are these things?” he said to her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Wegonen owe?” (“what is this?”) odinaan (he said to her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Mīdạc mindimōyä ōdā‘pinạng.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash mindimooye odaapinang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then the old woman took them up.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash mindimooye odaapinang (and then the old woman took them up).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) “Maskīgimin iᵘ,” udinān; “unicicinōn mītcināniwạng,” udinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mashkiigimin iw,” odinaan; “onizhishinoon miijinaniwang,” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Cranberries these,” she said to him. “Good are they to eat,” she said to him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Mashkiigimin iw,” odinaan (“cranberries those,” she said to him); “onizhishinoon miijinaniwang,” odinaan (“they are good when one eats them,” she said to him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Ā‘pidci mạmāngiminagạtiniwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aapiji mamaangiminagadiniwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Very large were they in size.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aapiji mamaangiminagadiniwan (very large in their berry-size they were).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Mīdạc minawā mādcāwād, anipạbimusät minawā Nänabucū.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash miinawaa maajaawaad, ani-babimosed miinawaa Nenaboozhoo.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon again they started, on his way again went Nanabushu walking.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash miinawaa maajaawaad (thereupon again they set out), ani-babimosed miinawaa Nenaboozhoo (on his way again Nanabushu went walking).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Anidātatagi‘kwänit, kägō ubi‘tā‘kuskānạn uskījigunk; mīdec äji∙ạ∙ndōtcipitcigät, mīnạngwana mînạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ani-daatagikwenid, gegoo obi-taakoskaanan oshkiizhigong; mii dash ezhi-andoojiibijiged, mii nangwana miinan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*As he went along with his head lifted up, something touched him on the face ; and as he felt of them, they turned out to be berries.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ani-daatagikwenid (as he went along with head tilted up), gegoo obi-taakoskaanan oshkiizhigong (something brushed against him on the eye/face); mii dash ezhi-andoojiibijiged (and as he felt about for them), mii nangwana miinan (they turned out to be berries).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) Mīdạc äji∙o∙dā‘pinạng, äjiwâbanda∙ā∙t ō‘kumisạn, ināt: “Wägunän owä?” udinān ō‘kumisạn wâbạnda∙ā∙t.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash ezhi-odaapinang, ezhi-waabanda’aad ookomisan, inaad: “Wegonen owe?” odinaan ookomisan waabanda’aad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And as he picked them, he showed them to his grandmother, saying to her: “What are these things?” he said to his grand mother as he showed them to her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash ezhi-odaapinang (and as he picked them), ezhi-waabanda’aad ookomisan (showing them to his grandmother), inaad: “Wegonen owe?” (saying: “what is this?”) odinaan ookomisan waabanda’aad (he said to his grandmother as he showed her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) “Ōᵘ, ki‘tci∙ạ∙sisawäminạn,” udinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Oow, gichi-asasaweminan,” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Why, big cherries,” she said to him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Oow, gichi-asasaweminan,” odinaan (“why, big cherries,” she said to him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) “Onīcicinōn mīdcigātäg,” udigōn ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Onizhishinoon miijigaadeg,” odigoon ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Good are they to eat,” he was told by his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Onizhishinoon miijigaadeg,” odigoon ookomisan (“they are good when eaten,” he was told by his grandmother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) “Mī cayīgwa tci∙a∙niandawâbạndạmạn käda∙ī∙ndaiyạng,” udigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii zhayiigwa ji-ani-andawaabandaman geda-daayang,” odigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Soon shall you now look for a place where we are to dwell,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Mii zhayiigwa ji-ani-andawaabandaman (“now you shall go and seek out) geda-daayang,” odigoon (where we are to dwell,” he was told).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) Mīdạc kägä‘t Nänabucu kī∙ạ∙nipagitciwanānāt ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget Nenaboozhoo gii-ani-pagijiwanaanaad ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then truly did Nanabushu put his grandmother down from off his back.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget Nenaboozhoo (and then truly Nanabushu) gii-ani-pagijiwanaanaad ookomisan (let down his pack-burden, his grandmother, from his back).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) Mīdạc kī∙u∙cigät Nänabucu mägwä nängawi mini‘kāng, ānindi nībiwa kägō ni‘tāwiging māniwạng.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gii-ozhiged Nenaboozhoo megwe-nengawiminikaang, aaniindi niibiwa gegoo nitaawiging maaniwang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon did Nanabushu make a camp in among the sand-berries, in where there was an abundance of various kinds of berries growing in the ground.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gii-ozhiged Nenaboozhoo (thereupon Nanabushu made camp) megwe-nengawiminikaang (in among the sand-berry patch), aaniindi niibiwa gegoo nitaawiging maaniwang (where many sorts of things were growing in abundance).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) Mī∙i∙mā kī∙u∙nābạndạnk tcitāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii imaa gii-onaabandang ji-daawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And there he chose a place for them to live.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii imaa gii-onaabandang ji-daawaad (and there he chose the place for them to dwell).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(19) Mīdạc imạ kā∙a∙ī∙ndāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash imaa gaa-daawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So there was where they continued.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash imaa gaa-daawaad (so there was where they came to dwell).</span>
