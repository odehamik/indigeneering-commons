17\. NÄNABUSHU PRETENDS TO BE A WOMAN.

Nanabushu Pretends to be a Woman

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Wâsagunäckạnk — Series 1 (Nos. 1–18), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīsa’ äni∙i∙cimādcāt papimusät.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa eni-izhi-maajaad babimosed.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then away he started upon his journey, travelling afoot.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa eni-izhi-maajaad (and so then off he set out) babimosed (travelling along on foot).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Mīsa’ ugītäbi‘tawāᵉ i‘kwäwaᵉ mạnisänit; ānīc ugīmi‘tawāᵉ:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa ogii-debitawaa’ ikwewa’ manisenid; aaniish ogiimitawaa’:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so he came within the sound of some women who were gathering fire-wood; now he secretly overheard them saying:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa ogii-debitawaa’ ikwewa’ (and so he came within hearing of some women) manisenid (who were gathering firewood); aaniish ogiimitawaa’ (now he secretly overheard them):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) “Ạmạntcigic kä∙i∙jictcigä∙u∙ngubạnän tciwītigämạnk ‘aᵉa’ᵘ inini?” i‘kitōwaᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Amanjigish ge-izhchige’ongobanen ji-wiidigemang a’aw inini?” ikidowa’.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“(I) wonder how we can bring it to pass so that we can marry that man!” they said.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Amanjigish ge-izhchige’ongobanen (“I wonder however we might manage it) ji-wiidigemang a’aw inini?” (that we could marry that man?”) ikidowa’ (they said).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) “Ạmbäsạnō, wāwiyạc ningatōtawāg awägwäniwigwänạg,” kī∙i∙nändạm Nänabucu.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Ambe sa noo, wawiyazh ninga-doodawaag awegweniwigwenag,” gii-inendam Nenaboozhoo**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Now, a trick I am going to play on them, whoever they are,” thought Nanabushu.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Ambe sa noo (“now then), wawiyazh ninga-doodawaag (I will play a trick on them) awegweniwigwenag,” (whoever they may be,”) gii-inendam Nenaboozhoo (thought Nanabushu).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Ugi‘känimān kämawinit wägwisisinit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ogikenimaan ge-mawinid wegwisisinid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He knew that the mother (of the man) would cry.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ogikenimaan (he knew of her) ge-mawinid (that she would cry) wegwisisinid (the one who was his mother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Mīdạc kā∙i∙ciwawäci∙u∙t kā∙i∙ci∙i∙‘kwä‘kāsut.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-wawezhi’od gaa-izhi-ikwekaazod.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so he got into gay attire after he had taken on the form of a woman.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-wawezhi’od (and so then he dressed himself up finely) gaa-izhi-ikwekaazod (after he had made himself out to be a woman).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Mīdạc ạdi‘kō∙u∙bīnisạgusīn mīdạc īni’ᵘ kā∙ā∙wä‘tcigät i∙i∙ᵘ i‘kwäng.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash adikoo’obiinisagosiin mii dash iniw gaa-aawechiged i’iw ikweng.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*There was a caribou spleen which he turned into a woman’s thing.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash adikoo’obiinisagosiin (and there was a caribou spleen) mii dash iniw gaa-aawechiged i’iw ikweng (and that was the thing he made into a woman’s part).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Kā∙i∙cināgwu∙u∙t, kā∙i∙cināsi‘kawāt i∙i∙ᵘ i‘kwäwaᵉ, o∙ō∙widạc ugī∙i∙nāᵉ a‘pīkā∙u∙disāt:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaa-izhinaagwo’od, gaa-izhi-naazikawaad i’iw ikwewa’, o’ow idash ogii-inaa’ apii gaa-odisaad:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*After he had taken on the form (of a woman), (and) after he had gone over to where the women were, this he then said to them when he came upon them:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaa-izhinaagwo’od (after he had given himself that look), gaa-izhi-naazikawaad i’iw ikwewa’ (after he had gone over to those women), o’ow idash ogii-inaa’ (this he then said to them) apii gaa-odisaad (when he came upon them):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) “Ānīnti āyāt ‘aᵉa’ᵘ inini cīngänimāt ‘iᵉi’ᵘ i‘kwäwaᵉ kā∙i∙nint?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniindi ayaad a’aw inini zhiingenimaad i’iw ikwewa’ gaa-inind?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Where is the man who is said to be a hater of women?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniindi ayaad a’aw inini (“where is that man) zhiingenimaad i’iw ikwewa’ (who hates women) gaa-inind?” (so it is said of him?”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Mīdạc kā∙i∙gut: “Mī∙u∙māⁿ nāwōtäna ayāt,” ugī∙i∙gōᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-igod: “Mii omaa naaw-oodena ayaad,” ogii-igoo’.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Whereupon he was told: “Here in the centre of the town he is,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-igod (whereupon he was told): “Mii omaa naaw-oodena ayaad,” (“here in the middle of the town he is,”) ogii-igoo’ (he was told by them).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) “Gagwānisạgisi, äntugwän tcī∙i∙nändạnk.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gagwaanisagizi, endogwen ji-inendang.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“He is hopelessly impossible, it is uncertain what his feeling would be (concerning you).”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gagwaanisagizi (“he is hopeless), endogwen ji-inendang.” (who knows what he would think.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) “Tạgackumā, awī∙i∙ni‘k,” udināᵉ; “‘Ninbi∙i∙cinica∙u∙gō ninīgī∙i∙gōk,’” udināᵉ iᵘ i‘kwäwaᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Daga shkomaa, awii-inik,” odinaa’; “Nin-bi-izhi-nisha’ogoo ni-niigi’igoog,’” odinaa’ iw ikwewa’.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Then pray, do you go and give him a message,” he said to them; “I have been sent hither by my parents,” he said to the women.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Daga shkomaa (“then come now), awii-inik,” (go and tell him,”) odinaa’ (he said to them); “Nin-bi-izhi-nisha’ogoo (“I have been sent here) ni-niigi’igoog,’” (by those who gave me birth,”) odinaa’ iw ikwewa’ (he said to the women).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) Misa’ kägä‘t kā∙i∙cikīwät päcik, kā∙i∙ciwīndamawint waᵉa’ᵘ mindimōⁿyä wägusisit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa geget gaa-izhi-giiwed bezhig, gaa-izhi-wiindamawind wa’aw mindimooyenh wegosisid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so truly, when back one (of them) went, then was the old woman who was mother (to the man) given the message.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa geget gaa-izhi-giiwed bezhig (and so truly, when one of them went back home), gaa-izhi-wiindamawind wa’aw mindimooyenh (then this old woman was given the message) wegosisid (the one who was his mother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) Ä‘kitut ‘aᵉa’ᵘ i‘kwä mayātcī∙ā∙tcimut: “Pīwitä ōmāⁿ ayā.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**E-kidod a’aw ikwe mayaajii’aajimod: “Biiwide omaa ayaa.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then said the woman who had conveyed the message: “A stranger is here.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">E-kidod a’aw ikwe (then said the woman) mayaajii’aajimod (who had carried the message): “Biiwide omaa ayaa.” (“a stranger is here.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) Ōdạc i‘kito: “Nimbī∙i∙cinica∙u∙gō ninīgī∙i∙gōk,” i‘kito.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**O’ow dash ikido: “Nimbi-izhinizha’ogoo ni-niigi’igoog,” ikido.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And this she said: “I have been sent hither by my parents,” she said.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">O’ow dash ikido (and this she said): “Nimbi-izhinizha’ogoo (“I have been sent here) ni-niigi’igoog,” (by those who gave me birth,”) ikido (she said).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) “Mīdạc kā‘pīcimādcinica∙u∙t, ‘awitibātcimun,’ nintik.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii dash gaa-pi-izhi-maajinizha’od, ‘awi-dibaajimon,’ nindig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“And so when I was set upon my way hitherward, ‘Go give the news,’ I was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Mii dash gaa-pi-izhi-maajinizha’od (“and so when I was sent off this way), ‘awi-dibaajimon,’ (‘go and tell the news,’) nindig (I was told).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) Nicāgwänim. ‘Tabīcāwạg nintāngwaiyạg.’”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ni-zhaagwenim. ‘Daa-bi-izhaawag nindaangwayag.’(1)”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*I was loath (to go). ‘Let my friends come hither.’ (said the woman(1)).”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ni-zhaagwenim (I was unwilling). ‘Daa-bi-izhaawag nindaangwayag.’ (‘let my sisters-in-law come here.’)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) Mīdạc ä‘kitut ‘aᵘ mintimōyä: “Ānīn dạc ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ạntawābạmāsiwäk,” utināᵉ ‘iᵉi’ᵘ udānisaᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash e-kidod aw mindimooye(2): “Aaniin dash i’iw andawaabamaasiweg,(1)” odinaa’ i’iw odaanisa’.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon said the old woman(2): “Why do you not go look for her(1)?” she said to her daughters.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash e-kidod aw mindimooye (thereupon the old woman said): “Aaniin dash i’iw andawaabamaasiweg,” (“why is it you do not go seek her,”) odinaa’ i’iw odaanisa’ (she said to her daughters).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(19) Mīdạc kägä‘t pa∙i∙cinạntawâbạmāwāt īgi’ᵘ i‘kwäwạg, mīsa’ kā∙i∙kīwäwītcīwāwāt īgi’ᵘ i‘kwäwạg.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget ba-izhi-nandawaabamaawaad igiw ikwewag, mii sa gaa-giiwewiijiiwaawaad igiw ikwewag. (1)**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so truly came the women seeking for her, whereupon back home the women went, taking her(1) with them.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget ba-izhi-nandawaabamaawaad igiw ikwewag (and so truly those women came seeking her), mii sa gaa-giiwewiijiiwaawaad igiw ikwewag (whereupon those women went back home taking her along).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(20) Mīdạc kā∙i∙ci∙o∙nōtä∙i∙nt iwiti wäntapinit īni’ᵘ niniwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi’onoode’ind iwidi wendabinid iniw niniwan. (3)**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then a place was made for her there where the man(3) was seated.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi’onoode’ind (and then a place was made for her) iwidi wendabinid iniw niniwan (there where that man was seated).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(21) Mīsa’ cigwa’ ki∙u∙nāpämit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa zhigwa gii-onaabemid. (1) (4)**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Therefore she(1) now had a husband(4).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa zhigwa gii-onaabemid (and so now she had a husband).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(22) Cigwa uwīcāmāᵉ utāngwäyaᵉ tcīmạnisäwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa owiishaamaa’ odaangweya’ ji-manisewaad(5).**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*By and by she wished her sisters-in-law to go with her to gather fire-wood(5).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa owiishaamaa’ odaangweya’ (by and by she invited her sisters-in-law along) ji-manisewaad (so that they would gather firewood).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(23) Ānīc ạtcinạgu kīmạmạtwä∙i∙gäwạn, āca nībiwa misạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish ajina go gii-mamadwe’igewan, aazha niibiwa misan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So in a little while after the sound of her chopping was heard, already (was there) much fire-wood.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish ajina go gii-mamadwe’igewan (now, in just a little while the sound of her chopping was heard), aazha niibiwa misan (already there was much firewood).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(24) “Awänän dạc ‘aᵘ mämindagä käjiⁿcawisit?” utināwān, uwīntamawāwān ugiwān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Awenen dash aw memindage ge-zhi-nshawisid?” odinaawaan, owiindamawaawaan ogiwaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Who is she that is such a remarkable worker?” they said to their mother, they said to her, telling her about it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Awenen dash aw memindage (“who then is she that is so exceedingly) ge-zhi-nshawisid?” (such a good worker?”) odinaawaan (they said to her), owiindamawaawaan ogiwaan (they told their mother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(25) “Kägätsa kīciⁿcawisī ‘aᵉa’ᵘ nintāngwänān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Geget sa gii-zhi-nshawisii a’aw nindaangwewaan.” (6)**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Truly a good worker is our sister-in-law.” (6)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Geget sa gii-zhi-nshawisii a’aw nindaangwewaan.” (“truly such a good worker is our sister-in-law.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(26) Anīc kägätsa minwäntạm ‘aᵉa’ᵘ mindimōyä, kayä ‘aᵉa’ᵘ a‘kiwäⁿzī kicincawisinit una∙ā∙ngani‘kwämiwān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish geget sa minwendam a’aw mindimooye, gaye a’aw akiwenzii gii-zhinshawisinid ona’aanganikwemiwaan.(7)**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, thoroughly pleased was the old woman, as was also the old man, that such a good worker was their daughter-in-law.(7)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish geget sa minwendam a’aw mindimooye (now, truly pleased was the old woman), gaye a’aw akiwenzii (and so too the old man) gii-zhinshawisinid ona’aanganikwemiwaan (that their daughter-in-law was such a good worker).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(27) Mīdạc kā∙i∙cikạnōnāt wâbicäciwạn:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-ganoonaad waabizheshiwan:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then she (Nanabushu) addressed the Marten, saying:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-ganoonaad waabizheshiwan (and then she addressed the Marten):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(28) “Ạmbäsạnō wītō‘kawicin o∙ō∙äcictcigäyān,” ugī∙i∙nān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Ambe sa noo wiidookawishin o’ow ezhichigeyaan,” ogii-inaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“I wish you would help me in this that I am undertaking,” she said to it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Ambe sa noo wiidookawishin (“come now, help me) o’ow ezhichigeyaan,” (in this that I am doing,”) ogii-inaan (she said to it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(29) Mīdạc īni’ᵘ kā∙u∙nītcānisit; o∙ō∙widạc ugī∙i∙nān:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash iniw gaa-oniijaanisid; o’ow idash ogii-inaan:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so that was the creature she had for child; and this she said to it:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash iniw gaa-oniijaanisid (and so that was the one she had for a child); o’ow idash ogii-inaan (and this she said to it):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(30) “Ạmbäsạnō, mōⁿjạg mawi’n,” ugī∙i∙nān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Ambe sa noo, moozhag mawin,” ogii-inaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Come, now, all the while do you cry,” she said to it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Ambe sa noo (“come now), moozhag mawin,” (cry all the while,”) ogii-inaan (she said to it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(31) Mīdạc kägä‘t kā∙i∙cictcigänit,</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget gaa-izhichigenid,**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And that truly was what (the Marten) did.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget gaa-izhichigenid (and that truly was what it did),</span>

<span class="l-orig">(32) ta‘kubinät äcictcigät mīyä‘ta ‘i∙ᵉi’∙māⁿ skījiguning saga‘pinät; ta‘kubinät pimūmāwisut.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**dakobinaad ezhichiged mii eta i’imaa shkiinzhigoning zagapinaad; dakobinaad bimoomaawizod.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When she had it strapped to the cradle-board, her arrangement was such that she had it bound up as far as over the eyes; with it bound to the cradle-board, she played the nurse carrying it about on her back.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">dakobinaad ezhichiged (binding it up, her way of doing it) mii eta i’imaa shkiinzhigoning zagapinaad (was to lash it only as far as over the eyes); dakobinaad bimoomaawizod (binding it up, she carried it about on her back as a nurse).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(33) Misagu‘pạnä mawinit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa go pane mawinid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so all the while did (the Marten) weep.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa go pane mawinid (and so all the while it wept).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(34) “Wo∙ō∙widạc i‘kidun,” ugī∙i∙nān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Wo’ow idash ikidon,” ogii-inaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Now, this do you say,” she said to it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Wo’ow idash ikidon,” (“and this do you say,”) ogii-inaan (she said to it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(35) “‘Tạgwāgicōp niwī∙ạ∙mwā,’ i‘kitun ‘iᵉi’ᵘ tcimamāwiyạn,” ugī∙i∙nān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Dagwaagishoob niwii-amwaa,’ ikidon i’iw ji-mamawiyan,” ogii-inaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“‘Some tenderloin do I wish to eat,’ do you say, so that you may cry,” she said to it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Dagwaagishoob niwii-amwaa,’ (“‘some tenderloin I want to eat,’) ikidon i’iw ji-mamawiyan,” (say it so that you will cry,”) ogii-inaan (she said to it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(36) Mīdạc kägä‘t änwät ‘aᵉa’ᵘ ạbinōdcī.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget enwed a’aw abinoojii.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And that truly was what the infant cried.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget enwed a’aw abinoojii (and that truly was what the infant cried).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(37) “Tagwāgicōp nīwī∙ạ∙mwā!” inwät.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Dagwaagishoob niwii-amwaa!” inwed.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Some tenderloin do I want to eat!” it cried.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Dagwaagishoob niwii-amwaa!” (“some tenderloin I want to eat!”) inwed (it cried).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(38) Cayīgwa nisitu‘tawā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhayiigwa nisidotawaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Presently they understood what it wanted.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhayiigwa nisidotawaa (presently he was understood).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(39) Ānīc cigwa sāgitōwạn usinisạn, ānīc ugimāwiwạn; pō‘tc kīcitcigänit ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ạnicinābä kä∙i∙‘kītut ‘aᵉa’ᵘ a‘kiwäⁿzī.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish zhigwa zaagidoowan ozhinisan, aaniish ogimaawiwan; booch gii-zhichigenid i’iw anishinaabe ge-ikidod a’aw akiwenzii.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now then out went her father-in-law to cry aloud, for he was chief; for of necessity were the people bound to do whatever the old man should say.*</span>

(17) Nanabushu Pretends to be a Woman

(1) The woman impersonated by Nenaboozhoo.

(2) A woman to whom the message had been given.

(3) The woman-hater.

(4) The formality of leading a daughter to that part of the lodge where a man seats himself, and having her seat herself by him, is the public announcement that she is his wife.

(5) One of the first things a bride does is to go for fire-wood or for water, a convention by which she enters her new station.

(6) A pleasing compliment which a wife enjoys is to hear it said of her that she is a good worker.

(7) Pleased because their maintenance is assured. A son is seldom encouraged to marry a woman simply because she is handsome or that she is of a pleasing disposition; but if she can work, if she can turn her hands to something useful, then she is the one for him to get. Behind all this is the desire of the old folks to be cared for in their old age.
