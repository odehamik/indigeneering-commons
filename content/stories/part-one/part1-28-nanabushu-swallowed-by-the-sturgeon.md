28\. NÄNABUSHU SWALLOWED BY THE STURGEON.¹

<span class="l-fiero">**28. NAANABUSHU GAA-GONID MICHI-NAMEGWAN.**</span>

Nänabushu Swallowed by the Sturgeon (1)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by probably Midāsugaⁿj (Michelson’s own hedge, carried) — Series 2 (Nos. 19–32), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīsa´ äjitibi‘kạtinik; weyābạninig ayạbi ändānsiwiwāt. Abā‘pic nāwa‘kwänig ugạnōnān ō‘kumisạn: “Nō‘ko, kanabạtc ningacacigạtạb. Kāwīninā wäwäbạnābān kitayāsīn?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa ezhi-dibikadinig; weyaabaninig ayaabi endaansiwiwaad. Abaapizh naawakwenig oganoonaan ookomisan: “Nooko, ganabaj ningazhazhigatab. Gaawiin ina wewebanaabaan gidayaasiin?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then night came on; on the morrow he remained idle at their little home. And when it came noon, he spoke to his grandmother, saying: “My grandmother, perhaps I shall grow weary with being idle. Have you not a hook and line?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa ezhi-dibikadinig (and so then night came on), weyaabaninig (when it was morning), ayaabi endaansiwiwaad (he stayed idle at their little dwelling), Abaapizh naawakwenig (and when it came noon), oganoonaan ookomisan (he spoke to his grandmother), “Nooko (my grandmother), ganabaj ningazhazhigatab (perhaps I will grow weary/idle), Gaawiin ina wewebanaabaan gidayaasiin? (have you not a hook-and-line?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) “Nōjis, kägä‘t nintaiyān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Noozhis, geget nindayaan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“My grandchild, to be sure, I have one.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Noozhis (my grandchild), geget (truly), nindayaan (I have one).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Ā‘tayā, kägä‘t minwäntạm Nänabucu. “Nō‘kō, ạmbāsa niwīwäwäbạnābī. Intawā iᵘ kitcīmān ningayābạtci‘tōn.” Nänabucu äjipōsit micawạgām äji∙i∙cāt. Ā‘pidci micawạgām kā‘tạgwicing, ānīc kiˢ‘tcigạmi, “Mīsa´ omā kädạcigwāgwāckwäpitcigäyān,” i‘kido. Nänabucu ubōna‘kạtōn ‘iᵉi´ᵘ umīgiskạn; kāmica‘kīsänik mādcī∙ạ∙m:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aatayaa, geget minwendam Naanabozho. “Nookoo, ambe sa niwii-wewebanaabii. Indawaa iw gijiimaan ningad-aabajitoon.” Naanabozho ezhi-boozid mishawagaam ezhi-izhaad. Aapiji mishawagaam gaa-dagwishing, aaniish gichigami, “Mii sa omaa gedazhi-gwaagwaashkwebijigeyaan,” ikido. Naanabozho oboonakadoon i’iw omiigiskan; gaa-mizhakiizenig maajii’am:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Oh, truly pleased was Nänabushu. “My grandmother, therefore do I wish to fish with hook and line. Accordingly your canoe will I use.” When Nänabushu got into (the canoe), then out to sea he put. When very far out on the water he was come, for it was the sea, “Here is where I will fish with my hook and line,” he said. Nänabushu cast his hook into the water; when it touched the floor (of the sea), he began singing:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aatayaa, geget minwendam Naanabozho (oh, truly pleased was Naanabozho). “Nookoo (my grandmother), ambe sa niwii-wewebanaabii (come now, I wish to fish with hook-and-line). Indawaa iw gijiimaan ningad-aabajitoon (so then your canoe I will use).” Naanabozho ezhi-boozid (Naanabozho got aboard), mishawagaam ezhi-izhaad (out on the open water he went). Aapiji mishawagaam gaa-dagwishing (when he arrived very far out on the water), aaniish gichigami (for it was the sea/great-water), “Mii sa omaa gedazhi-gwaagwaashkwebijigeyaan (here is where I will bob my line),” ikido (he said). Naanabozho oboonakadoon i’iw omiigiskan (Naanabozho set down his hook), gaa-mizhakiizenig (when it touched the bottom), maajii’am (he began to sing):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) “Micīnạmägwä, pinawä‘kuntci∙ạ∙n i‘ku‘käyaᵘ.  
Micīnạmägwä, pinawä‘kuntci∙ạ∙n i‘ku‘cäyaᵘ.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Michiinamegwe *flagged*, binawakojii’an *flagged* i’ko’kayanh *flagged*. Michiinamegwe *flagged*, binawakojii’an *flagged* i’ko’zhayanh *flagged*.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“O big sturgeon! come swallow me, here is my decoy. O big sturgeon! come swallow me, here is my decoy.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Michiinamegwe (O big sturgeon), binawakojii’an *flagged* (come swallow me), i’ko’kayanh *flagged* (here is my decoy). Michiinamegwe (O big sturgeon), binawakojii’an *flagged* (come swallow me), i’ko’zhayanh *flagged* (here is my decoy).” [song-vocables carried verbatim; the two lines differ only in käyaⁿ/cäyaⁿ as printed]</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Mīdạc i∙i∙mā na∙i∙tāg ayāt aᵉa´ᵘ micīnạmägwä. Māmwä‘tcidạc imā unowängigạnig mīsa imā siniguskāgut. Käga‘pi ingutci ānu∙i∙cā, mīsa´ untcita ‘iᵉi´ᵘ siniguskāgut ‘iᵉi´ᵘ migiskạn. Käga‘pīsa ogạnōnān ạdi‘kamägwạn:² “Adi‘kạmäg, ạmbāsạnō, nawatantama´ᵘ Nänabucu iwä wäwäbanābān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash i’imaa na’itaag ayaad a’aw mishiinamegwe. Mamwezhi dash imaa onowengiganig mii sa imaa zinigoshkaagod. Gega-bii ingoji aanawi-izhaa, mii sa onjida i’iw zinigoshkaagod i’iw miigiskan. Gega-bii sa oganoonaan adikamegwan: “Adikameg, ambe sa noo, nawadandamaw *flagged* Naanabozho iwe wewebanaabaan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so at that very place the big sturgeon happened to be. And it was there that he felt a rubbing on the cheeks (by the hook). At last away he tried in vain to go, but exactly as before he felt the rubbing of the hook. Then finally he spoke to the Whitefish, saying: “O Whitefish! please seize that bobbing (hook) of Nänabushu’s with your mouth.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash i’imaa na’itaag (and so right at that place), ayaad a’aw mishiinamegwe (the big sturgeon happened to be). Mamwezhi dash imaa onowengiganig *flagged* (and there against his cheeks), mii sa imaa zinigoshkaagod (it was there it rubbed him). Gega-bii ingoji aanawi-izhaa (at last he tried in vain to go somewhere), mii sa onjida i’iw zinigoshkaagod i’iw miigiskan (but on purpose/exactly the hook kept rubbing him). Gega-bii sa oganoonaan adikamegwan (at last he spoke to the whitefish): “Adikameg (O whitefish), ambe sa noo (come now), nawadandamaw *flagged* Naanabozho iwe wewebanaabaan (seize with your mouth that bobbing hook of Naanabozho’s).”</span>

*Ạdi‘kamägwan, “white fish;” literally, “caribou fish.”*

*Ạdi‘kamägwan, “white fish;” literally, “caribou fish.”*

<span class="l-orig">(6) Kägä‘t äcinawatatạnk ‘iᵉi´ᵘ uwäwäbanābānini. Ki‘tciwīckāni ‘iᵉi´ᵘ owäwäbạnābān. Äjiwī‘kubidōt, “‘Ā‘tā, mīsa´ mīsa´, mīsa´ wā‘tiyān!” Cigwa omō‘kibinān ạdi‘kạmägwạn. “Isä,³ kāwīn kīn kīnantawänimisinōn! Kīwīnantān ‘iᵉi´ᵘ wäwäbanābān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Geget ezhi-nawadandang i’iw owewebanaabaanini. Gichi-wiishkaani i’iw owewebanaabaan. Ezhi-wiikobidood, “Aataa, mii sa mii sa, mii sa waa-’idiyaan!” Zhigwa omookibinaan adikamegwan. “Ise, gaawiin giin ginandawenimisinoon! Giwii-nandaan i’iw wewebanaabaan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Truly then it seized that bobbing (hook) with its mouth. There was a hard pull on the bobbing (hook). When he pulled on it, “Ah, that’s it, that’s it, that’s what I want!” Presently he drew the Whitefish to the surface of the water. “Bah! I don’t want you. You befoul the bobbing (hook).”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Geget ezhi-nawadandang i’iw owewebanaabaanini (truly it seized the bobbing hook with its mouth). Gichi-wiishkaani i’iw owewebanaabaan *flagged* (there was a hard pull on the bobbing hook). Ezhi-wiikobidood (when he pulled on it), “Aataa, mii sa mii sa, mii sa waa-’idiyaan! (ah, that’s it, that’s what I want! — *flagged*)” Zhigwa omookibinaan adikamegwan (presently he drew up the whitefish). “Ise (bah!), gaawiin giin ginandawenimisinoon (I don’t want you)! Giwii-nandaan *flagged* i’iw wewebanaabaan (you befoul the bobbing hook).”</span>

*Isä, “Bah!” an exclamation of reproach; with most Ojibwa dialects it is uttered only by the feminine sex.*

*Isä, “Bah!” an exclamation of reproach; with most Ojibwa dialects it is uttered only by the feminine sex.*

<span class="l-orig">(7) Kägä‘t intawā upạgidạndān ‘iᵉi´ᵘ wäwäbạnābān. Intawā äjikīwät ạdi‘kạmäg. Cigwa ạnitạgwicin. Micīnạmägwän äjikakwätcimigut: “Ānīntạc ä‘kitut?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Geget indawaa obagidandaan i’iw wewebanaabaan. Indawaa ezhi-giiwed adikameg. Zhigwa ani-dagwishin. Mishiinamegwen ezhi-gagwejimigod: “Aaniindash ekidod?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Truly, therefore, (the Whitefish) let go from his mouth the bobbing (hook). And so back home went the Whitefish. Soon he came home. By the big Sturgeon was he asked: “And what did he say?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Geget indawaa obagidandaan i’iw wewebanaabaan (truly then he let go the bobbing hook from his mouth). Indawaa ezhi-giiwed adikameg (so back home went the whitefish). Zhigwa ani-dagwishin (soon he arrived home). Mishiinamegwen ezhi-gagwejimigod (by the big sturgeon he was asked): “Aaniindash ekidod? (and what did he say?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) “Kā, ‘Kiwīnạntān iᵘ wäwäbạnābān,’ i‘kitō Nänabucu Micīnamägwä yä‘ta ninạntawänimā.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaa, ‘Giwii-nandaan i’iw wewebanaabaan,’ ikido Naanabozho. ‘Mishiinamegwe yeta ninandawenimaa.’”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Oh, ’you befoul the bobbing (hook),’ said Nänabushu. ’It is the big Sturgeon I want.’”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gaa (oh), ‘Giwii-nandaan i’iw wewebanaabaan (you befoul the bobbing hook — *flagged*),’ ikido Naanabozho (said Naanabozho). ‘Mishiinamegwe yeta ninandawenimaa (it is only the big sturgeon I want).’”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Mīsa keyābi onowängikạnig sinigwisänig ‘iᵉi´wä uwäwäbanābānini. Ā‘pidcisa umiguckātäntān micīnạmägwä. “Tạga, kīn, nạmägus, awinawạtạntạn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa geyaabi onowengiganig zinigwisenig i’iwe owewebanaabaanini. Aapiji sa omigoshkaadendam mishiinamegwe. “Daga, giin, namegos, awi-nawadandan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so once more against his cheeks rubbed the bobbing (hook). Very much was the big Sturgeon annoyed: “I say, you, Trout, go seize it with your mouth.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa geyaabi onowengiganig zinigwisenig i’iwe owewebanaabaanini (and so once more against his cheeks the bobbing hook rubbed). Aapiji sa omigoshkaadendam mishiinamegwe (very much was the big sturgeon annoyed). “Daga, giin, namegos (now then, you, Trout), awi-nawadandan (go seize it with your mouth).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Tōkisäni iᵘ wäwäbạnābān. Äjiwī‘kubitōt Nänabucu uwawäbạnābān, mīsa´ nāsāo ä‘kitut: “Mīsa wā‘tiyān.” Omō‘kibạnān īni´ᵘ nạmägusạn. “Isä, kāwīn kīn kinạntawänimisinōn! Kīwinạntān ‘iᵉi´ᵘ niwäwäbạnābān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Dookizeni i’iw wewebanaabaan. Ezhi-wiikobidood Naanabozho owewebanaabaan, mii sa naasaab ekidod: “Mii sa waa-’idiyaan.” Omookibanaan iniw namegosan. “Ise, gaawiin giin ginandawenimisinoon! Giwii-nandaan i’iw niwewebanaabaan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*There was a gentle pull on the bobbing (hook). As on his bobbing (hook) Nänabushu pulled, so the same thing as before he said: “This is what I want.” Out of the water he pulled the Trout. “Bah! you are not the one I want. You befoul my bobbing (hook).”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Dookizeni i’iw wewebanaabaan (there was a gentle pull on the bobbing hook). Ezhi-wiikobidood Naanabozho owewebanaabaan (as Naanabozho pulled his bobbing hook), mii sa naasaab ekidod (the same as before he said): “Mii sa waa-’idiyaan (this is what I want — *flagged*).” Omookibanaan iniw namegosan (he drew up the trout). “Ise (bah!), gaawiin giin ginandawenimisinoon (you are not the one I want)! Giwii-nandaan i’iw niwewebanaabaan (you befoul my bobbing hook — *flagged*).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Mīnawā ubạgitạntān nạmägus. Äjimādcāt; tägwicing udōgimāmạn uganōnigōn. “Ānīc ä‘kitut?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Miinawaa obagidandaan namegos. Ezhi-maajaad; dagwishing odoogimaaman oganoonigoon. “Aaniish ekidod?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*The Trout was the next to let go from his mouth the bobbing (hook). Then he departed; when he got home, by his chief was he asked: “What did he say?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Miinawaa obagidandaan namegos (again the trout let go the hook from his mouth). Ezhi-maajaad (then he departed); dagwishing odoogimaaman oganoonigoon (arriving, by his chief he was spoken to). “Aaniish ekidod? (what did he say?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) “Kāwīn kuca kīn kinantawänimisinōn. Micinạmägwä yäta ninạntawänimā.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaawiin gosha giin ginandawenimisinoon. Mishiinamegwe yeta ninandawenimaa.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“You are not the one I really want. It is only the big Sturgeon I wish.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gaawiin gosha giin ginandawenimisinoon (you are truly not the one I want). Mishiinamegwe yeta ninandawenimaa (it is only the big sturgeon I want).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) Mīsa ‘i´ᵘ ingutci äji∙i∙cāt micīnamägwä. Kāwäsa ugacki‘tōsīn pa‘kān tciwäwäbạnābinit. “Ācimādcisawīna∙a´∙ᵘ Nänabucu! Mini‘k ningitạcī‘kāg wīnicki∙i∙t!” Nänabucōwan unawatantamawān iᵉiwä uwäwäbạnābānini.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa iw ingoji ezhi-izhaad mishiinamegwe. Gawesa ogashkitoosiin bakaan ji-wewebanaabinid. “Azhi-maajisawina a’aw Naanabozho! Minik ningidazhi’kaag wii-nishki’id!” Naanabozhowan onawadandamawaan i’iwe owewebanaabaanini.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon away went the big Sturgeon. He found it impossible to make (Nänabushu) fish elsewhere with his hook and line. “Confound that Nänabushu! He has been pestering me so long as to anger me!” He then seized hold of Nänabushu’s bobbing (hook) with his mouth.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa iw ingoji ezhi-izhaad mishiinamegwe (thereupon the big sturgeon went off somewhere). Gawesa ogashkitoosiin *flagged* bakaan ji-wewebanaabinid (he could not make him fish elsewhere). “Azhi-maajisawina *flagged* a’aw Naanabozho! (confound that Naanabozho!) <span class="l-orig">(14) Nänabucu mīgu iᵘ äjiki‘tcīwibitōt iᵉi´ᵘ wäwäbạnābān. Inābit micawagām, minisäns inäntạm wändcimō‘kibīnik. Mīnạngwạna iᵘ ucigwạnāni īni´ᵘ micīnamägwän. <span class="l-fiero">**Naanabozho mii go iw ezhi-gichi-wiibidood i’iw wewebanaabaan. Inaabid mishawagaam, minisens inendam wenji-mookibinig. Minangwana iw ozhigwanani iniw mishiinamegwen. <span class="l-orig">(16) Agāwāgu pimādisiwạn. Cigwa kīgitōwạn: “Nänabucu, mīsa kayä kīn kī‘kuni‘k aᵉa´ᵘ micinạmägwä.”</span>
