NÄNABUSHU IS GIVEN POWER BY THE SKUNK, BUT WASTES IT.

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by probably Midāsugaⁿj (Michelson’s own hedge, carried) — Series 3 (Nos. 33–38), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Cigwa mīnawā mādcā bạbāmusät. Ningutingiku sāga∙i∙gạn umạdā‘kạn, owâbạmā ki‘kināntawānaᵉ¹ pata‘kisunit. Mīdạc änäntạnk: “Ạnicinābäg inangwạna ayāwạg,” inäntạm. Ạnimādcāt. <span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa miinawaa maajaa babaamosed. Ningodingiko zaaga’igan omadaakan, owaabamaa gikinaandawaanan* batakizonid. Miidash enendang: “Anishinaabeg inange ayaawag,” inendam. Ani-maajaad. Zhigwa geget owaabandaan dwaa’igan wendi-binid; moozojiid* wedwaa-baaninid, gegetsa mizhi-de’akwadini*.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Soon again he was off travelling afoot. When once out upon the ice of a lake he came, he saw a balsam¹ standing. <span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa miinawaa (now once again), maajaa babaamosed (off he set out walking about). Miidash enendang (and so he thought): “Anishinaabeg inange ayaawag (people, it seems, are living here),” inendam (he thought). Ani-maajaad (on he went). <span class="l-eng">*And so, truly, he then got down on his hands and knees. Presently from the other direction faced the rear (of the Skunk), who broke wind into (Nänabushu).
