57\. NÄNABUSHU AND THE FISH-TRAP.

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Mrs. Syrette — Series 8 (Nos. 57–63), Fort William (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ningutingsa kīwäⁿ a∙ī∙ndāwạg Nänabucō ō‘kumisạn kayä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding sa giiwenh endaawag Nenabozho ookomisan gaye.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Once on a time they say that Nänabushu and his grandmother were abiding there.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding sa (once, indeed), giiwenh (they say), endaawag (they were dwelling there — flagged), Nenabozho ookomisan gaye (Nenabozho and his grandmother).</span>

*endaawag (a∙ī∙ndāwạg) has no direct lexicon hit; built by analogy to the well-attested -daawaad/endaa- ’dwell’ stem. The vowel onset a∙ī∙- doesn’t match the ä- onset seen in the story’s other endaa- forms.*

<span class="l-orig">(2) Mīdạc kīwäⁿ änāt ōcisạn: “Nōjis,” udinān, “iwä zībi päcu’ kā∙a∙yāmạga‘k, mī‘ku imā pīndcibōnāgạnạn uji∙ā∙wā‘pạn i‘ku kicicäⁿibạnīg,” udinān. Nänabucudạc wīn kāwīn kägō i‘kitusī.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash giiwenh enaad ozhishenyan: “Noozhis,” odinaan, “iwe ziibi besho gaa-ayaamagag, mii go imaa biinjibonaaganan ozhiaawaapan igo gizhishenyibaniig,” odinaan. Nenabozho dash wiin gaawiin gegoo ikidosiin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so they say that she said to her grandson: “My grandson,” she said to him, “over there hard by is a river, and it was there your uncles of old used to set fish-traps,” she said to him. Now, Nänabushu, so far as he was concerned, had nothing to say.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash giiwenh enaad ozhishenyan (and then, they say, she said to her grandson — flagged): “Noozhis,” odinaan (“My grandson,” she said to him), “iwe ziibi besho gaa-ayaamagag (over there a river lies nearby), mii go imaa biinjibonaaganan ozhiaawaapan (right there is where fish-traps used to be made — flagged), igo gizhishenyibaniig (by your uncles of old — flagged),” odinaan (she said to him). Nenabozho dash wiin gaawiin gegoo ikidosiin (Nenabozho, for his part, said nothing).</span>

*Four flags: ōcisạn (grandson-obviative form), the pīndcibōnāgạn ’fish-trap’ compound (opens a recurring flag), uji∙ā∙wā’pạn (habitual-preterite construction), and kicicäⁿibạnīg ’uncles of old’ (root attested, suffix not). i‘kitusī rendered ikidosiin per the VAI-negative -n rule rather than the count-1 lexicon variant lacking the final n.*

<span class="l-orig">(3) Miyä‘tagu a‘pạnä nạndawäntcigät pābītōd kägō ändāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii eta-go apane nandawenjiged baabiidood gegoo endaawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*His only occupation was always hunting for game (and) bringing something home.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii eta-go (his only occupation was), apane (always), nandawenjiged (hunting for game), baabiidood gegoo (bringing something along — flagged), endaawaad (to where they dwelt).</span>

*pābītōd (’bringing something home’) has no lexicon attestation; rendered by mechanical character conversion only.*

<span class="l-orig">(4) Ā‘pidcimā kayä Nänabucu kīmī‘kawiininīwi īnā‘tisōkāsu.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aapiji maa gaye Nenabozho gii-mikawi’ininiwi izhi-aadizookaazo.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And very lucky too was Nänabushu at getting game, to judge from his fame in story.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aapiji maa (very indeed), gaye (also), Nenabozho gii-mikawi’ininiwi (Nenabozho was a lucky one at finding game — flagged), izhi-aadizookaazo (so he is storied that way in the telling — flagged).</span>

*Two soft flags: kīmī’kawiininīwi and īnā’tisōkāsu are both built from attested roots (mi’kawi- ’lucky/find’, aadizookaan ’story’) but the full compounds are not directly attested.*

<span class="l-orig">(5) Ningutingidạc, kīwäku a∙ī∙ndāwāt, omi‘kwändān kā∙i∙gu‘pạn ō‘kumisạn undci tcipīntcibōnāganikät pāwi‘tigunk.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding idash, giiwenh go endaawaad, omikwendaan gaa-igopan ookomisan onji ji-biinjibonaaganiked baawitigong.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, once on a time they say that while they were living, (there), he remembered what his grandmother had said to him about going to catch fish with the fish-trap at the rapids.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding idash (now, once), giiwenh go endaawaad (they say, while they were living there — see 57-1a), omikwendaan (he remembered it), gaa-igopan ookomisan (what his grandmother had once said to him), onji (about), ji-biinjibonaaganiked (going to fish with the trap — flagged), baawitigong (at the rapids).</span>

*a∙ī∙ndāwāt continues the section-1 vowel-onset flag (57-1a). tcipīntcibōnāganikät continues the fish-trap flag (57-FT1), spelled ’nt’ rather than ’ndc’ — treated as the same morpheme.*

<span class="l-orig">(6) Mīdec Nänabucu änändạnk: “Ictä mītạgic kā∙i∙ci‘pạn nō‘komis tcipīntcibōnāgạni‘käyān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash Nenabozho enendang: “Ishte, miidagish gaa-izhi’ipan nookomis ji-biinjibonaagani’keyaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon Nänabushu thought: “Quite so, that is what my grandmother had told me, that I should go to catch fish with the fish-trap.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash Nenabozho enendang (thereupon Nenabozho thought): “Ishte, miidagish (quite so, that’s just it — flagged), gaa-izhi’ipan nookomis (that’s what my grandmother had once told me — flagged construction), ji-biinjibonaagani’keyaan (that I should go fish-trapping — flagged).</span>

*mītạgic and the kā∙i∙ci’pạn preterite construction are both flagged (no direct lexicon form found for either). tcipīntcibōnāgạni’käyān continues the fish-trap flag (57-FT1).*

<span class="l-orig">(7) Māgicā nō‘kumis aiyä‘kusitug pạnä wīyās mīdcît,” inändạm Nänabucō.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Maagizhaa nookomis ayekozidog pane wiiyaas miijid,” inendam Nenabozho.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Perhaps my grandmother may have grown tired of always eating meat,” thought Nänabushu.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Maagizhaa nookomis (perhaps my grandmother), ayekozidog (may have grown tired), pane wiiyaas miijid (of always eating meat),” inendam Nenabozho (thought Nenabozho).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) “Kīⁿgōⁿyạndạc kạnạbạtc uwī∙ạ∙mwān,” inändạm. Mīdạc änāt: “Nō‘kumis, kā∙i∙ciyạmbạnidạc undcipīndcibōnāgạni‘käyān?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Giigoonyan dash ganabaj owii-amwaan,” inendam. Mii dash enaad: “Nookomis, gaa-izhi-amban idash onji-biinjibonaagani’keyaan?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Now, fish she probably wants to eat,” he thought. Thereupon he said to her: “My grandmother, (you remember) what you told me about catching fish with a fish-trap?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Giigoonyan dash ganabaj owii-amwaan (fish, probably, she wants to eat — flagged),” inendam (he thought). Mii dash enaad (thereupon he said to her): “Nookomis, gaa-izhi-amban idash (my grandmother, what you had once told me — flagged), onji-biinjibonaagani’keyaan (about my going fish-trapping — flagged)?”</span>

*Continues the fish-trap flag (57-FT1) in undcipīndcibōnāgạni’käyān. uwī∙ạ∙mwān and kā∙i∙ciyạmbạnidạc are both constructions built from low-count (n=1) stems, flagged.*

<span class="l-orig">(9) “Äyäᵉ,” i‘kitu mindimōyä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Eya’,” ikido mindimooye.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Yes,” said the old woman.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Eya’ (yes),” ikido mindimooye (said the old woman).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) “Imāguta bāwi‘tigunk mīimāa‘ku pīndcibōnāwā‘pạn kīⁿgōⁿyaᵉ kicicäyabạnīg,” udinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Imaagoda baawitigong miimaago biinjibonaawaapan giigoonya’ gizhishenyabaniig,” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“It was at yonder rapids where your uncles of old used to fish with a fish-trap,” she said to him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Imaagoda baawitigong (right there at the rapids — flagged), miimaago (that’s just the place — flagged), biinjibonaawaapan (they used to fish-trap — flagged), giigoonya’ (fish), gizhishenyabaniig (your uncles of old — see 57-UNC1),” odinaan (she said to him).</span>

*Imāguta and miimāa’ku have no lexicon attestation, flagged; pīndcibōnāwā’pạn continues the fish-trap flag (57-FT1). kicicäyabạnīg is this section’s spelling of the section-2 ’uncles of old’ word (57-UNC1), rendered to its own page per the no-leveling rule.*

<span class="l-orig">(11) “Ki‘tcinänībiwa, kînīni‘ku, unisāwâbạnīn kīⁿgōⁿyaᵉ,” udigōn ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gichi-nenibiwa, giininigo, nisaawaabaniin giigoonya’,” odigoon ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Oh, great indeed was the number of fishes they used to kill,” he was told by his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gichi-nenibiwa (oh, greatly numerous — flagged), giininigo (indeed — flagged, uncertain particle), nisaawaabaniin giigoonya’ (fish they used to kill), odigoon ookomisan (he was told by his grandmother).</span>

*Ki’tcinänībiwa is flagged: the rarer nänībiwa/nenibiwa variant (1 hit) is used rather than the common nibiwa/niibiwa (13+ hits). kînīni’ku has no lexicon match at all and is flagged as an unresolved particle.*

<span class="l-orig">(12) Mīdec kägä‘t Nänabucō mādci‘tād uji∙ā∙t pīndcibōnāgạnạn, kiˢtcimi‘tigōn udayāwatcinigānān, wâsa kayä udōndāwạnān, wīsōngi‘tōd upīndcibōnāgạnan. Mīdạc kā‘kīci∙ā∙t wīndạmawāt ō‘kumisạn, mīdạc änāt: “Mī, nō‘kumis, kī‘kīci∙a∙g pīndcibōnāgạn, mīdạc kīⁿgōⁿ tci∙ạ∙mwat,” udinān ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget Nenabozho maajitaad ozhiaad biinjibonaaganan, gichi-mitigoon odayaawachiniganaan, waasa gaye odoondaawanaan, wiizoongitood obiinjibonaaganan. Mii dash gaa-giizhi’aad wiindamawaad ookomisan, mii dash enaad: “Mii, nookomis, gii-giizhi’ag biinjibonaagan, mii dash giigoonh ji-amwad,” odinaan ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then truly did Nänabushu begin making his fish-traps, huge logs he carried on his shoulders, and from afar he carried them on his back, (for) he wanted to make his traps strong. And then after he had finished them he notified his grandmother, and this he said to her: “There, my grandmother, have I finished the fish-traps, and now some fish will you eat,” he (thus) said to his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget Nenabozho maajitaad (and then truly Nenabozho began making) ozhiaad biinjibonaaganan (his fish-traps — flagged), giˢchi-mitigoon (huge logs; the raised ˢ is an unresolved HARD-flag glyph — flagged) odayaawachiniganaan (he carried on his shoulders — flagged), waasa gaye odoondaawanaan (and from afar he carried them on his back — flagged), wiizoongitood obiinjibonaaganan (for he wanted to make his traps strong — flagged). Mii dash gaa-giizhi’aad (and then after he had finished them) wiindamawaad ookomisan (he notified his grandmother), mii dash enaad (and this he said to her): “Mii, nookomis (there, my grandmother), gii-giizhi’ag biinjibonaagan (I have finished the fish-trap), mii dash giigoonh ji-amwad (now some fish will you eat),” odinaan ookomisan (he said to his grandmother).</span>

*Six flags this section, detailed in the flags list. kiˢtcimi’tigōn: the ˢ is the open HARD-flag glyph; the surrounding stem is otherwise rule-grade (gichi-mitigoon, ’big logs’). The raised superior ˢ Jones prints in this word is carried in the Jones layer only and given no Fiero value — the ˢ family is an open HARD flag on M.’s docket.*

<span class="l-orig">(13) “Ayaᵉ,” i‘kitu mindimōyä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Eya’,” ikido mindimooye.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Ay,” said the old woman.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Eya’ (ay, yes),” ikido mindimooye (said the old woman).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) Mīdạc weyābaninig Nänabucu kī∙a∙wiwâbạmāt upīndcibōnāgạnạn, nībawa dạc kīⁿgōⁿyạn kīpīndcipōsōwạn; mīdạc Nänabucu kī‘kīwäwạnät.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash weyaabaninig Nenabozho gii-awi-waabamaad obiinjibonaaganan, niibawa idash giigoonyan gii-biinjibosowan; mii dash Nenabozho gii-giiwewanaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So then in the morning Nänabushu went to see his fish-traps, and many the fish that were drawn into them; thereupon Nänabushu went back home, carrying them along.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash weyaabaninig (so then in the morning), Nenabozho gii-awi-waabamaad obiinjibonaaganan (Nenabozho went to see his fish-traps — noun flagged), niibawa idash giigoonyan gii-biinjibosowan (and many fish had been drawn into them — flagged), mii dash Nenabozho gii-giiwewanaad (thereupon Nenabozho went back home, carrying them along — flagged).</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Eni-ayaad idash besho (and as he was drawing near), ani-ikido Nenabozho (Nenabozho went along, saying): “Nookomis! (O my grandmother!)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) nībawa kīⁿgōyạg mingīnisāg,” i‘kitō.</span>
