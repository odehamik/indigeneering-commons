37\. NÄNABUSHU AND THE MALLARD.¹

<span class="l-fiero">**37. Nanabozho gaye Zhiishiib.**</span>

Nanabushu and the Mallard (1)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by probably Midāsugaⁿj (Michelson’s own hedge, carried) — Series 3 (Nos. 33–38), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ānīc, cayīgwa mīnawā pa‘kadä Nänabucu. Ningutingiku mīnawā udōtisān ạnicinābäᵉ, mī käyābi nīⁿciwaᵉ unītcānisinini. Mīsa´ ani∙ī∙nābit, “Wägutugwän mādcīwāgwän?” inändạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish, zhigwa miinawaa bakade Nanabozho. Ningodingigo miinawaa odoodisaan anishinaaben, mii geyaabi niizhiwa’ onijaanisinini. Miisa’ ani-inaabid, “Wegonen-igo madiiwaagwen?” inendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Well, already was Nänabushu again becoming hungry. And one other time he came to some people, and they also had two children. And now, as he looked about, “What in the world must they have to eat?” he thought.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish (well), zhigwa miinawaa bakade Nanabozho (now again Nanabozho is hungry), ningodingigo miinawaa odoodisaan anishinaaben (one time again he visits some people), mii geyaabi niizhiwa’ onijaanisinini (they too have two children), miisa’ ani-inaabid (so as he looks about), Wegonen-igo madiiwaagwen (what could there be to eat), inendam (he thinks).</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Miisa’ geget anagota’ ki-gikwewan iniw ikwewan. Zhigwa waawezhi’owan iniw ininiwan ozhaawashko-osaanamanan, owiinge oshtigwaaning odizhizhimaanini*. Zhigwa sa giikizhi’owan. Onji-bazigwa’owan ininishiban ezhi-booninid i’imaa obimidaakobijiganiwaang*, noondaagoziwan: “Gwiizh, gwiizh, gwiizh, gwiizh!” inwewan. Owaawaabamaan mizinid bekish giigidowan: “ ‘O, ‘o, ‘o, mindimooye! ane’an*,” odinaan. Aaniish mii apane miizinid, “ ‘O, ‘o, ‘o, mindimooye! ane’an*.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon truly the woman went and hung up the kettle. Presently the man painted himself with a green color, all around over his head did he put it. In time he was done with painting himself. And while yet seated, and of a sudden, he started forth from the place, uttering: “Kwīsh, kwīsh, kwīsh, kwīsh!” (such) was the sound of his voice. Up flew a Mallard² that alighted yonder on the cross-pole, he was heard saying: “Kwīsh, kwīsh, kwīsh, kwīsh!” (such) was the sound of his voice. (Nänabushu) observed him muting, while at the same time (he heard him) saying: “Ho, ho, ho, old woman! keep it stirring,” he said to her. And all the while (the Mallard) muted, (he was saying): “Ho, ho, ho, old woman! keep it stirring.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Miisa’ geget anagota’ ki-gikwewan iniw ikwewan (so truly the woman hangs up the kettle), zhigwa waawezhi’owan iniw ininiwan (presently the man adorns/paints himself), ozhaawashko-osaanamanan (with green paint), owiinge oshtigwaaning odizhizhimaanini (all over the top of his head he puts the painting), zhigwa sa giikizhi’owan (now he has finished), megwaago ginamadabinid (while he sits there), baanimaago wenji-noondaagozinid (suddenly there comes the sound of him), Gwiizh, gwiizh, gwiizh, gwiizh inwewan (such is the sound of his voice), onji-bazigwa’owan ininishiban (the (former) man flies up from there), ezhi-booninid i’imaa obimidaakobijiganiwaang (alights yonder on their cross-pole), noondaagoziwan (he is heard), owaawaabamaan mizinid (he watches him muting), bekish giigidowan (at the same time he speaks), ‘O, ‘o, ‘o, mindimooye, ane’an (Ho, ho, ho, old woman, keep it stirring), odinaan (he says to her), aaniish mii apane miizinid (and all the while he mutes).</span>

<span class="l-eng">*The woman truly hung up the kettle. He had his green paint spread out; in painting himself he colored his head green.
