3\. NĀNABUSHU SLAYS HIS YOUNGER BROTHER.

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenabozho Gii-nisaad Oshiimeyan**</span>

Nanabushu Slays His Younger Brother

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Wâsagunäckạnk — Series 1 (Nos. 1–18), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ānīc mīsai iᵘ cigwa kī∙o∙tickutämiwāt. Ānīc mīsai kā∙i∙jiwāwītigämāt īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn wīn kāwi‘kā pizān kī∙a∙yāsinōn wuᵉō ki‘tcigạmi. Wâbōs nämatạpit ijini‘kātätug woᵉō’ ki‘tcigạmi. Mīdạc a‘pạnä mōjạg ‘iᵉiwidi kīa’winạmạtạpit. O∙o∙widạc ogī∙i∙nān īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn: “Ambäsạnō, nō‘kō, mī∙i’∙ᵘ mini‘k käwâbōⁿsōyān.” Mīdạc ‘iᵉ∙i∙widi ki‘tciyānäyābi‘kāgwän, mīdạc i∙i∙widi ugitāpi‘k panä kī∙u∙nạbit. Mīdạc kā∙i∙cināgwa‘k ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ạsin. Mīdạc kā∙i∙‘kitut: “Wâbōs nämadạbit uga∙i∙cini‘kātānāwa ogūᵘ ạnicinābäg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aanish miisa-i’iw gizhwa gii-odishkodademiwaad. Aanish miisa-i’iw gaa-izhi-waawiitigamaad iniw ookomisan wiin gaawiikaa bizaan gii-ayaasinoon wewe(?) gichigami. Waabooz namadabid izhinikaadedog wewe(?) gichigami. Miidash apane moozhag i’iwidi gii-aa-winamadabid. Oo-owidash ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan: “Ambesano, nookoo, mii-i’iw minik gewaaboonzoyaan.” Miidash i’iwidi gichi-aayaanebi’agwaan(?), miidash i’iwidi ogidaabi’k bane gii-onabid. Miidash gaa-izhi-inaagwa’k i’iw asin. Miidash gaa-izhi-gidod: “Waabooz namadabid oga-izhinikaadaanaawaa ogow anishinaabeg.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, therefore, were they then in possession of fire. So now afterwards, while he lived with his grandmother, never still was this sea. Seated-Hare was perhaps the name of this sea, for it was always his custom to go frequently over there and sit. And this he said to his grandmother: “Now, my grandmother, it is now long enough that I have been a hare.” And so at that place there must have been a promontory jutting (out into the sea), and over there on the top was where he always sat. And now such was the way that rock looked. And this was what he said: “Seated-Hare shall these people call it.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aanish miisa-i’iw gizhwa (now, that is the time) ∙ gii-odishkodademiwaad (when they were given their way of life) ∙ gaa-izhi-waawiitigamaad iniw ookomisan (after he stayed there with his grandmother) ∙ wiin gaawiikaa bizaan gii-ayaasinoon gichigami (the great water was never calm) ∙ waabooz namadabid izhinikaadedog gichigami (a Hare sits there, so the great water is named, it seems) ∙ miidash apane moozhag (and so always, continually) ∙ gii-aa-winamadabid (he kept sitting and gazing) ∙ ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan (he said to his grandmother) ∙ “ambesano, nookoo” (pray, grandmother) ∙ “mii-i’iw minik gewaaboonzoyaan” (that is how long I shall be a Hare) ∙ i’iwidi gichi-... ogidaabi’k bane gii-onabid (over there on the high rock he always sat) ∙ gaa-izhi-inaagwa’k i’iw asin (so it looked like that rock) ∙ “waabooz namadabid oga-izhinikaadaanaawaa ogow anishinaabeg” (these people will call it ’where the Hare sits’)</span>

*Printed footnote on p.14: “¹ That is, as if he had been scorched by fire.” (refers to käwâbōⁿsōyān, the Hare’s color)*

<span class="l-orig">(2) Ānīc mīsa iᵘ cīgwa kī∙ạ∙nicināpäwit. Kāwīn käyābⁱ kī∙a∙binōtci∙ī∙wisī. Wu∙ō∙ ugī∙i∙nān īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn: “Kigi‘känimîna ayāwiyān?”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Ōᵉ∙ō∙widec ogī∙i∙nān: “Ạmbäsanō mānū wīndạmawicin,” ogī∙i∙nān īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn. “Kunigä ningi∙o∙o∙simitug!”</span>

<span class="l-gloss">oo-owidash ogii-inaan (and this he said to her) ∙ “ambesano maanoo wiindamawishin” (please, do tell me) ∙ ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan (he said to his grandmother) ∙ “gunige ningii-oosimidog!” (I wonder if I had a father!)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) “Äyeᵉ,” ōgī∙i∙gōn; “kāwīn kīnāgusisī awägwän ‘aᵉa’ᵘ kā∙a∙o∙siyạn,” ogī∙i∙gōn īni’ᵘ o‘kumisạn. Oᵉōwidạc ogi∙i∙gōn īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn: “Kinibu ‘aᵉaᵘ ogīyạmbạn: mīsạgui∙i∙ᵘ äjiwīndạmōnān. Kāwīn kitāgātamōsinōn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aye’,” ogii-igoon; “gaawiin gii-naagozisii awegwen a’aw gaa-oosiyan,” ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan. Oo-owidash ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan: “Ginibo a’aw ogii’-ayaambon: miisa-gu-i’iw ezhi-wiindamoonaan. Gaawiin gidaa-gaagiidamosinoon(?).”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Yes,” he was told. “They could not see whoever he was that was your father,” he was told by his grandmother. And this he was told by his grandmother: “Dead is she who was your mother: that is all I can tell you. Not would I hide it from you.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aye’” (yes) ∙ ogii-igoon (he was told) ∙ “gaawiin gii-naagozisii awegwen a’aw gaa-oosiyan” (the one who was your father was not seen) ∙ ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan (he was told by his grandmother) ∙ “ginibo a’aw...” (the one who was your mother is dead) ∙ “miisa-gu-i’iw ezhi-wiindamoonaan” (that is all I tell you) ∙ “gaawiin gidaa-...” (I would not hide it from you)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Oᵉ∙o∙widạc ogī∙i∙nān ‘aᵉa’ᵘ Nänabucu: “Ānīn käkicināgwa‘k käpäciguyān? kägātạmawiyạn,” ogī∙i∙nān īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn. Mīdạc cayīgwa kī∙i∙‘kitut: “Ānīn wändci∙i∙citcigäyạn ‘iᵉi’ᵘ kātạmawiyạn ‘iᵉiwisa kā∙i∙jiwäbisiyāng? Kāwīn pō‘tc niwạnändazin i∙i∙wisa kā∙i∙jiwäbisiyāng. Ayāwagîsa nintinändạm niⁿtcikiwäyạng. Mānō tạga wīndạmawicin i∙i∙wisa kā∙i∙jiwäbisiyāng.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Oo-owidash ogii-inaan a’aw Nenabozho: “Aaniin gekizhinaagwa’k gebezhigoyaan? gegaadamawiyan,” ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan. <span class="l-eng">*And this to her said Nänabushu: “How could it possibly be that I should be the only (child)? You must be hiding it from me,” he said to his grandmother. <span class="l-gloss">ogii-inaan a’aw Nenabozho (Nenabozho said to her) ∙ “aaniin gekizhinaagwa’k gebezhigoyaan?” (how does it appear that I am alone?) ∙ “gegaadamawiyan” (that you keep it from me) ∙ miidash zayiigwa gii-ikidod (and then he said) ∙ “aaniin wenji-izhichigeyan i’iw gaadamawiyan...” (why do you do this, hiding it from me, what happened to us?) ∙ “gaawiin booch niwanendaziin” (I do not forget it) ∙ “nindinendam ninjikiwenyang” (I think of my brothers) ∙ “maanoo daga wiindamawishin” (please do tell me what happened to us)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Mīdạc kīsägimigut ‘aᵉa’ᵘ mindimoyä. O∙ō∙widic ogī∙i∙nān īni’ᵘ ōcicäⁿyạn: “Ānīc, kīgawīndạmōn, käᵉgä‘t kāwīn kigīpäjikuzī i∙i∙wisa kīnīgiyäg. Käᵉgä‘t kānangwanawīn¹ wu∙ō∙ kigī∙i∙jiwäpisim, kigīnisāwā ‘a∙a∙ᵘ kigiwā ‘iᵉiᵘ a‘pī nāguyäg. Käᵉgä‘t ạnicā kāmi‘kwändạmān ‘iᵉi’ᵘ wā∙i∙jitcigäyān ‘iᵉi’wisa dạc kā∙u∙ndcini‘tāwigi∙i∙nān.”²</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Miidash gii-zegimigod a’aw mindimooye. Oo-owidish ogii-inaan iniw ozhishenyan: “Aanish, gigawiindamoon, gegget gaawiin gigii-bezhigozii i’iwisa gii-niigiyeg. <span class="l-gloss">miidash gii-zegimigod a’aw mindimooye (and so the old woman was frightened by him) ∙ ogii-inaan iniw ozhishenyan (she said to her grandson) ∙ “aanish, gigawiindamoon” (well, I will tell you) ∙ “gegget gaawiin gigii-bezhigozii i’iwisa gii-niigiyeg” (truly you were not alone when you were born) ∙ “gigii-nisaawaa a’aw gigiwaa i’iw apii naagoyeg” (you killed your mother at the time you were born) ∙ “gegget anishaa gaa-mikwendamaan i’iw waa-izhichigeyaan” (truly it was for nothing that I thought of what I would do) ∙ “gaa-onji-nitaawigi’inaan” (the reason I raised you)</span>
