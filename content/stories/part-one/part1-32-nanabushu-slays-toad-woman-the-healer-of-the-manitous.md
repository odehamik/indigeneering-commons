32\. NÄNABUSHU SLAYS TOAD-WOMAN, THE HEALER OF THE MANITOUS.¹

Nenabosho slays Toad-Woman, the healer of the Manitous (2)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by probably Midāsugaⁿj (Michelson’s own hedge, carried) — Series 2 (Nos. 19–32), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīsa’ näyāp ä‘kubīyānigibạn kī∙a∙‘kubīyāni‘k.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa neyaap eko-biiyaanigiban gii-ko-biiyaanig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon back to its former depth did the water recede.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa neyaap (thereupon back again) eko-biiyaanigiban (to its former depth) gii-ko-biiyaanig (where the water had stood).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Abā‘pic näyāp kā∙a∙‘kubīyānik, mīsa’ cigwa wäwänigu udōnābandān wā‘tāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Abaapish neyaap gaa-ako-biiyaanig, miisa zhigwa weweni go odoonaabandaan waa-daad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When the water got to where it was before, he accordingly then with care selected a place where he would have his lodge.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Abaapish neyaap gaa-ako-biiyaanig (when the water had gone back to where it stood before), miisa zhigwa (and so then) weweni go (carefully) odoonaabandaan (he chose with his eyes) waa-daad (the place where he would dwell).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Mīdạc i∙i∙mā wā∙u∙ntcīt ‘iᵉi’ᵘ tayōc wīnạntunä∙i∙gät.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash i’imaa waa-onjiid i’iw dayoosh* wii-nandone’iged.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so from there he intended yet to seek (for his nephew).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash i’imaa (and so from there) waa-onjiid (where he meant to set out) i’iw dayoosh* (still — *editorial mark carried*) wii-nandone’iged (intending to search).</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa geyaabi babaamademod, ningoding sa awiya onoondawaan biminagamonid:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so while wandering about weeping, he once heard somebody going along singing:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa geyaabi babaamademod (and so still as he wandered about weeping), ningoding sa awiya (once someone) onoondawaan biminagamonid (he heard going along singing):</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aki ekwaag nimbishinawishin, nimbishinawishin saa

<span class="l-eng">*“From the ends of the earth do I come with the sound of my rattles, saa (X2)”(1)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">(song carried verbatim, sung again) “Aki ekwaag (from the ends of the earth) nimbishinawishin, nimbishinawishin, saa.”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Udini‘tawān awiya piminạgamunit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Odinitawaan awiya biminagamonid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Such was the way he heard some one sing while going along.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Odinitawaan awiya (such was how he heard someone) biminagamonid (going along singing).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Wägunänīwinān unāⁿzi‘tawān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Wegoneniiwinaan onaazitawaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*What should he do but go towards the sound of the being.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Wegoneniiwinaan (what should he do but) onaazitawaan (go toward the sound of it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) A‘pī täbābạmāt, kuniginīn, umaka‘kīn pimiyāntcikwāskuniwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii debaabamaad, goniginiin, omakakiin bimiyaanjigwaashkoniwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When he came in sight of the being, lo, (he saw that) it was a toad(2) leaping along from place to place.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii debaabamaad (when he caught sight of it), goniginiin (behold), omakakiin (a toad) bimiyaanjigwaashkoniwan (leaping along from place to place).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Wīgubīn pämōtạminit, kayä cicīgwạnạn upimiwinānini udōdạnāning ta‘kubisōwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Wiigobiin bemoondaminid, gaye zhiishiigwanan obimiwinaanini odoodanaaning dakobizowan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Some bast she bore upon her back, and some rattles she carried bound to her heels.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Wiigobiin bemoondaminid (some basswood bark she carried on her back), gaye zhiishiigwanan (and some rattles) obimiwinaanini (she carried along) odoodanaaning dakobizowan (bound at her heels).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Nībiwadạc īni’ᵘ wīgubīn bämōndạminit äjimawinạnāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Niibiwa dash iiniw wiigobiin bemoondaminid ezhi-mawinanaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, a good deal was the bast she carried on her back when he rushed up to her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Niibiwa dash iiniw wiigobiin (now, a good deal of that basswood bark) bemoondaminid (she carried on her back) ezhi-mawinanaad (when he rushed up at her).</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ezhi-odisaad, aaniish, aapiji mindimooyenyan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*On coming up to her, why, she was a very old woman.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ezhi-odisaad (on coming up to her), aaniish (why), aapiji mindimooyenyan (a very old woman she was).</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Nookoo (O my grandmother), aaniin wenji-nagamoyan? (why is it you are singing?)”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Kaa, Nenabosho gosha wii-nandobikaaso.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Oh, a snare is really to be laid for Nänabushu.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Kaa (oh), Nenabosho gosha (for Nenabosho indeed) wii-nandobikaaso (a snare is to be set).</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And this bast which I carry upon my back is the thing to be used for the purpose.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii wa nino* (and these — *editorial mark carried*) wayaabadakin (that are to be used) onoow wiigobiin (these basswood barks) gaa-bimoondamaan (which I carry on my back).</span>

<span class="l-eng">*It was Nänabushu who really shot the chief of the big lynxes.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nenabosho gosha wiin (it was Nenabosho indeed) gaa-bimwaad (who shot) iiniw ogimaa mishibizhiin (the chief of the big lynxes).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) “Nō‘ku, ānīn wīn iᵉi’ᵘ wā∙u∙ndcinạndōbi‘kāsut ‘aᵉa’ᵘ Nänabucu?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Nookoo, aaniin wiin i’iw waa-onji-nandoobikaazod a’aw Nenabosho?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“O my grandmother! pray, why is a snare to be set for Nänabushu?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Nookoo (O my grandmother), aaniin wiin i’iw (why is it) waa-onji-nandoobikaazod (that a snare is to be set for) a’aw Nenabosho? (that Nenabosho?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) “Kā, kāwīnạc wīn mīgu iᵘ äjisāgisininik ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ubikwa‘k aᵉa’ᵘ Nänabucu.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Kaa, gaawiin ash wiin mii go iw ezhi-saagisininig i’iw obikwak a’aw Nenabosho.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Oh, well! it is for the arrow of Nänabushu, which is now sticking out of (the chief of the big lynxes).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Kaa, gaawiin ash wiin (oh, well — *editorial query carried*) mii go iw ezhi-saagisininig (it is because it is sticking out) i’iw obikwak (that arrow) a’aw Nenabosho (of Nenabosho).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) Oᵉo’ᵘ ōdäna mī∙i∙mā pā∙u∙ndcīyān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**O’ow oodena mi i’imaa baa-onjiiyaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*From this town yonder do I come.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">O’ow oodena (this town) mi i’imaa baa-onjiiyaan (it is from there I come).</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And over there hardly alive is the chief of the big lynxes.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash imaa ayaawa (and there) bimaadizi a’aw ogimaamishibizhii (the chief big-lynx is barely alive).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(20) “Nō‘kumis! ānīn dạc wīn iᵉiᵘ ‘iᵉi’wä kipiminạgạmuyạn?”</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Nookomis! (O my grandmother!) Aaniin dash wiin i’iw i’iwe (and what was that) gibiminagamoyan? (that you were singing along?)”</span>
