49\. NÄNABUSHU AND THE LITTLE FISHERS.

Nanabushu and the Little Fishers

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Penessi — Series 7 (Nos. 44–56), Fort William (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ningudingidạc ạnipabimosäd, ogīmi‘kawaᵉ udcīgänsaᵉ äyānit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding idash ani-pabimosed, ogii-mikawaa’ ojiigensa’ e-yaanid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And once, when travelling along, he came upon some young Fishers.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding idash (and once) ani-babimosed (as he was walking along), ogii-mikawaa’ (he found them) ojiigensa’ (the little fishers) e-yaanid (who were there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Mīdạc änād: “Ānīndi gīgiwā?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash e-naad: “Aaniindi gigawaa?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And this he said to them : “ Where is your mother?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash e-naad (and so this he said to them): “Aaniindi (where is) gigawaa? (your mother?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) “Pabānạndawändcigä.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Babaanandawenjige.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“She is off somewhere hunting for game.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Babaanandawenjige. (She is off somewhere hunting for food.)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Mī∙i∙dạc kā∙i∙jipōpō‘kutcībināt, ugīmīdcināᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii iw dash gaa-izhi-poopookojiibinaad, ogii-miijinaa’.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when he had broken them in two at the wrist, he eased upon them.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii iw dash (and so then) gaa-izhi-poopookojiibinaad (when he had broken them in two at the wrist), ogii-miijinaa’ (he ate them).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Mīdạc kī∙ạ∙nimādcād.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gii-ani-maajaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then on his way he went.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash (and so) gii-ani-maajaad (then he went off on his way).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) A‘pī∙i∙dac tägucing udcīg ugīmi‘kawāᵉ unīdcānesaᵉ nibunit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii dash degoshing ojiig ogii-mikawaa’ oniijaanesa’ nibonid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when home was come the Fisher, she found that her children were dead.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii dash degoshing (and when she came home) ojiig (the Fisher) ogii-mikawaa’ (she found them) oniijaanesa’ (her children) nibonid (being dead).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Mīdạc äjinō‘pinạnād Nänabujūn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash ezhi-noopinanaad Nenaboozhoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon she pursued Nanabushu.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash (and so) ezhi-noopinanaad (then she pursued him) Nenaboozhoon (Nanabushu).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) A‘pī∙i∙dạc ädimint Nänabujū, ạninạgạmō Nänabujū:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii dash edimind Nenaboozhoo, ani-nagamo Nenaboozhoo:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when Nanabushu was overtaken, he was going along singing a song :*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii dash edimind (and when he was overtaken) Nenaboozhoo (Nanabushu), ani-nagamo (he was going along singing) Nenaboozhoo (Nanabushu):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) “Udcīgänsạg īnā kābōpō‘kudcīpināgwā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Ojiigensag inaa gaa-poopookojiibinagwaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Little fishers are the ones that I have broken in two at the wrists.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Ojiigensag (little fishers) inaa (indeed) gaa-poopookojiibinagwaa (are the ones I broke in two at the wrists).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Udcīgänsạg īnā kābōpō‘kudcīpināgwā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ojiigensag inaa gaa-poopookojiibinagwaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Little fishers are the ones that I have broken in two at the wrists.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ojiigensag (little fishers) inaa (indeed) gaa-poopookojiibinagwaa (are the ones I broke in two at the wrists).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Udcīgänsạg īnā kābōpō‘kudcīpināgwā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ojiigensag inaa gaa-poopookojiibinagwaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Little fishers are the ones that I have broken in two at the wrists.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ojiigensag (little fishers) inaa (indeed) gaa-poopookojiibinagwaa (are the ones I broke in two at the wrists).  
Ojiigensag (little fishers) inaa (indeed) gaa-poopookojiibinagwaa (are the ones I broke in two at the wrists).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) Udcīgänsạg īnā kābōpō‘kudcīpināgwā.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ojiigensag inaa gaa-poopookojiibinagwaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Little fishers are the ones that I have broken in two at the wrists.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ojiigensag (little fishers) inaa (they are the ones) gaa-poopookojiibinagwaa (that I have broken in two at the wrists).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) Udcīganidạc ogạnōnigōn: “Kīnmāwīn, kigīnisāg ninīdcānisạg!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ojiigan idash oganoonigoon: “Giin maawiin, gigii-nisaag ni-niijaanisag!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*By the Fisher was he addressed: “Then it was you, you slew my children !”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ojiigan idash (and the Fisher) oganoonigoon (addressed him): “Giin maawiin (then it was surely you), gigii-nisaag (you killed them) ni-niijaanisag! (my children!)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) “Kāwīn!” i‘kidō Nänabujū.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaawiin!” ikido Nenaboozhoo.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“No!” said Nanabushu.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gaawiin! (No!)” ikido (says) Nenaboozhoo (Nanabushu).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) A‘pī∙i∙dac ädimint, mi‘tigunk kī∙ā∙‘pạgizo.¹</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii dash edimind, mitigoong gii-apagizo. (1)**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when he was overtaken, he turned instantly into a log. (1)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii dash edimind (and when he was overtaken), mitigoong (onto a log) gii-apagizo (he threw himself). (1)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) Udcīgidạc ugīmīgādān ‘iᵉi’ᵘ mi‘tik, ā‘pidci ugīpīgwạndān ᵉi‘i’ᵘ mi‘tig.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ojiig idash ogii-miigaadaan i’iw mitig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And the Fisher fought the log, ever so much did she tear the log with her teeth.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ojiig idash (and the Fisher) ogii-miigaadaan (fought it) i’iw mitig (that log).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) Mēdạc kägä‘t Nänabujū wīsạgändạnk udiyānk.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget Nenaboozhoo wiisagendang odiyaang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon truly did Nanabushu suffer pain in his bottom.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget (and so truly) Nenaboozhoo (Nanabushu) wiisagendang (felt pain) odiyaang (in his bottom).</span>

(49) Nanabushu and the Little Fishers

(1) Mitigong gii’aapagizo, “he turned instantly into a log;” literally, “into or on to a log he threw himself,” but the sense is as given in the translation.
