10\. THE DEATH OF NÄNABUSHU’S NEPHEW, THE WOLF.

The Death of Nanabushu’s Nephew, the Wolf (1)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Wâsagunäckạnk — Series 1 (Nos. 1–18), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīdạc kā∙i∙jipa‘käguzītā‘tiwāt. Mīdạc kā∙i∙jikạbäciwāt, mīsa’ kägä‘t unisäni īni’ᵘ awäsīyạn. Ānīc kāwīnigu päcigwạnunk ayāsīwạg. Pạnägu pạpāmigusiwạg. Mīdạc kägä‘t ugitcimāmī‘kawi∙ā∙nini īni’ᵘ awäsīyạn. Kägä‘t mīno∙ā∙yā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-bakagozitaadiwaad. Mii dash gaa-izhi-gabeshiwaad, mii sa gegeph onisaanii iniw awesiyan. Aandi gaawiinigo bezhigwaning ayaasiwag. Banaagigo babaamigoziwag. Mii dash gegeph ogichimamikawiniwaa* iniw awesiyan. Gegeph minwaayaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon they separated from one another. And when (he and the Wolf) went into camp, it was truly (the Wolf) that killed the game. Naturally not in one spot they remained, always from one place to another they went. And so truly was (the Wolf) ever killing the pick of game. Truly was he living well.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-bakagozitaadiwaad (and so then they parted from each other) ∙ gaa-izhi-gabeshiwaad (when they made camp) ∙ mii sa gegeph onisaanii iniw awesiyan (truly it was he that killed those game-animals) ∙ aandi gaawiinigo bezhigwaning ayaasiwag (nowhere in one place did they stay) ∙ banaagigo babaamigoziwag (always they kept moving camp about) ∙ mii dash gegeph ogichimamikawiniwaa iniw awesiyan (and so truly he kept coming easily upon those game-animals) ∙ gegeph minwaayaa (truly he was living well).</span>

The printed heading carries the diaeresis: NÄNABUSHU’S (p.88 l.12, 12×) — title corrected.*

<span class="l-orig">(2) Ningutingigu pạpāmigusiwāt, äcimawīngwāminit īni’ᵘ umicōmäyạn. ‘Aᵉa’ᵘ ma∙ī∙ngạn äcinimîskạmāt, “Intạckā nīnītug mānāpạmigwän,” udinān ‘iᵉi’ᵘ kīcigīnipānit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningodingigo babaamigoziwaad, ezhi-mawingwaamini* iniw oziishenyan. A’aw ma’iingan ezhi-iniminika*mad, “Intashke* nindinitog menaapamigwen,” odinaan iw gii-giniginibenid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, once while they were moving about, in his sleep was (the Wolf’s) uncle weeping. The Wolf indifferently signed to him with the hand. “I fancy that probably he may be having a bad dream about me,” he said of him who then was taking his nap.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningodingigo babaamigoziwaad (now once while they were moving camp about) ∙ ezhi-mawingwaamini* iniw oziishenyan (his uncle was weeping in his sleep) ∙ a’aw ma’iingan ezhi-iniminika*mad (that wolf gestured toward him with his hand) ∙ “Intashke* nindinitog menaapamigwen” (“I suppose he must be having a bad dream”) ∙ odinaan iw gii-giniginibenid (he said of the one who was napping).</span>

*Kinship: Jones’s English p.89 names the dreamer’s relation reciprocally (uncle/nephew). The Ojibwe oziishenyan marks the one spoken-of. Jones’s English preserved verbatim; pronoun reference FLAGGED for M. — B18 re-read (2026-08-19): the English is restored to the print (“in his sleep was (the Wolf’s) uncle weeping”, p.89); the kinship flag for M. stands.*

<span class="l-orig">(3) Mīdạc kā∙i∙cikuskusinit, cīgwa kägä‘t uwīndạmāgōn: “Kägätsa, nintōjîm, kigic‘tcimanābạmin. Ạmbäsạnō, mānū pîsindawicîn kä∙i∙ninān; mānū icictcigän ‘iᵉi’ᵘ käininān. Kīcpîn wīnōntawisiwạn ᵉii’wisa käininān, kägä‘t kīga∙i∙nigā‘tōn ‘iᵉi’ᵘ kīya’ᵘ. Ạmbäsạnō, mîsawā‘ku ạni∙ạ∙timạt waᵉa’ᵘ awäsīⁿ, mānū mi‘tigōns kīgạnabō‘kubitōn mîsawā pạngiⁿs sībīnsiwa‘kamigāk, mīdạc ‘iᵉi’māⁿ käni∙a∙pạgitōyan ‘iᵉi’ᵘ mi‘tigōns. Mīsạgu ‘pạnä kätōtạmạn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-goshkozinid, zhigwa gegeph owiindamaagoon: “Gegezha, nindoozhim, gigichi-jimanaabamin. Ambesano, manoo bizindawishin gaa-izhi-ininaan; manoo izhichigen iw ge-ininaan. Giishpin wii-noondawisiwan iwsa ge-ininaan, gegeph giga-inigaa’ton iw giiyaw. Ambesano, mizhishewi-go ani-adimad a’aw awesiinh, manoo mitigoons giga-naboohkobiton mizhishewe bangiinh zibinzhiwa’kamigek, mii dash iwmaa gaa-izhi-(a)bagidooyan iw mitigoons. Mii sa go bana getoodaman.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon when (Nänabushu) woke, then truly was (the Wolf) informed by him, saying: “Verily, my nephew, have I had an exceedingly bad dream about you. I beg of you, please listen to what I shall say to you; please do what I tell you. If you have no desire to listen to what I have to tell you, truly then will you do yourself an injury. So please, even if it be when you are overtaking game, as you go along break off a little stick, no matter how small the dry bed of a brook may be, and there shall you fling the little stick. That is what you should always do.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-goshkozinid (and so then when he woke) ∙ zhigwa gegeph owiindamaagoon (now truly he was told by him) ∙ “Gegezha, nindoozhim, gigichi-jimanaabamin” (“Verily, my nephew, I have had a hard dream about you”) ∙ ambesano, manoo bizindawishin gaa-izhi-ininaan (“please, do listen to what I tell you”) ∙ manoo izhichigen iw ge-ininaan (“do that which I shall tell you”) ∙ giishpin wii-noondawisiwan iwsa ge-ininaan (“if you will not listen to what I tell you”) ∙ gegeph giga-inigaa’ton iw giiyaw (“truly you will harm your own body”) ∙ ambesano, mizhishewi-go ani-adimad a’aw awesiinh (“so please, even when you are overtaking that game”) ∙ manoo mitigoons giga-naboohkobiton mizhishewe bangiinh zibinzhiwa’kamigek (“do break off a little stick, no matter how small the dry brook-bed”) ∙ mii dash iwmaa gaa-izhi-abagidooyan iw mitigoons (“and there is where you throw the little stick”) ∙ mii sa go bana getoodaman (“that is just what you should always do”).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Ānīc mīsa gägä‘t äcictcigät mîsawāgu cigwa adimāt. Ānīc ningutingigu piminicawāt īni’ᵘ awäsīⁿyạn — ānīc mīgu ‘panä ‘iᵉi’ᵘ äcictcigät — kägätsa cīgạtci. Ningutingigu kāni∙u∙ndciidạcpiminicawāt īni’ᵘ mōⁿsōn — ānīc mīcigwa ānawi sīgwạninig — mīdạc änäntank ᵉa∙a∙ᵘ ma∙ī∙ngạn piminijawāt īni’ᵘ mōⁿzon, kägä‘t ki‘tciunītcāniwạn īni’ᵘ päminicawāt. Mīdạc cigwa täbābạmāt, mīgu ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ā‘pitci cigwa täbipināt, ugīwâbạndān pạngīⁿs sībīnsiwā‘kamigānig.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aanish mii sa gegeph ezhichiged mizhishewe-go zhigwa adimad. Aanish ningodingigo bimini-zhaawaad iniw awesiinhyan — aaniish mii go bana iw ezhichiged — gegezha zhiigaji. Ningodingigo gaa-onji-daazhi-bimini-zhaawaad iniw moozoon — aaniish mii zhigwa aanawi ziigwaninig — mii dash enendang a’aw ma’iingan bimini-zhaawaad iniw moozoon, gegeph gichi-oniijaaniwan iniw bemini-zhaawaad. Mii dash zhigwa debaabamaad, mii go iw ezhi-apiichi zhigwa debibinaad, ogii-waabandaan bangiinh zibinzhiwa’kamigenig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Accordingly that truly was what he did whenever he was about to overtake (the game). Now, once while he was in pursuit of some game, — for that was what he always was doing, — truly, he grew tired (of throwing a stick into the dry bed of brooks). For when once away from a certain place he was in pursuit of a moose, — since it was now getting well on towards the spring, — this was the feeling of the Wolf when in pursuit of the moose, truly a big cow was he following after. And then presently, when he came in sight of her, — indeed, when he was on the very point of seizing her, — he saw the dry bed of a small brook.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aanish mii sa gegeph ezhichiged (so truly that is what he did) ∙ mizhishewe-go zhigwa adimad (whenever he was overtaking it) ∙ ningodingigo bimini-zhaawaad iniw awesiinhyan (once while chasing those game-animals) ∙ aaniish mii go bana iw ezhichiged (for that is what he always did) ∙ gegezha zhiigaji (truly he grew tired) ∙ gaa-onji-daazhi-bimini-zhaawaad iniw moozoon (when off chasing a moose) ∙ mii zhigwa aanawi ziigwaninig (it being now nearly spring) ∙ mii dash enendang a’aw ma’iingan (and so this is what that wolf was thinking) ∙ gegeph gichi-oniijaaniwan iniw bemini-zhaawaad (truly a big cow was the one he was following) ∙ zhigwa debaabamaad (now he caught sight of her) ∙ ezhi-apiichi zhigwa debibinaad (just when he was about to catch her) ∙ ogii-waabandaan bangiinh zibinzhiwa’kamigenig (he saw a little dry brook-bed).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Kīⁿwäⁿ kī∙i∙nändạm: “Ạmbäsạnō, mīgu iᵘ känī∙i∙ji∙ā∙cạwigwāckwạniyān ‘iᵉi’māⁿ sībīnsiwa‘kạmigānig.” Pạnägu nāwạgām kāpạngicink ᵉi∙i∙māⁿ ki‘tcisibīnk, panägu kāsāswänik u‘tawạgạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Giiwenh gaa-inendang: “Ambesano, mii go iw ge-ni-aji-aazhawi-gweshkwaniyaan iwmaa zibinzhiwa’kamigenig.” Banaagigo naawagaam gaa-bangishing iwmaa gichi-zibing, banaagigo gaa-zeeswenik odawagan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*They say (that thus) he thought: “Well, now, (without throwing the stick ahead of me,) I will leap right on across the dry bed of this brook.” Then straightway down into the middle of a great stream he fell, and all the while was there a ringing in his ears.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Giiwenh gaa-inendang (so they say, this is what he thought) ∙ “Ambesano, mii go iw ge-ni-aji-aazhawi-gweshkwaniyaan iwmaa zibinzhiwa’kamigenig” (“Well, this is where I shall leap across the dry brook-bed”) ∙ banaagigo naawagaam gaa-bangishing iwmaa gichi-zibing (instead, into the middle he fell, there in the great river) ∙ banaagigo gaa-zeeswenik odawagan (instead, his ears were set ringing).</span>
