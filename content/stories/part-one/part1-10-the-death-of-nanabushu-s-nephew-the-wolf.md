10\. THE DEATH OF NÄNABUSHU’S NEPHEW, THE WOLF.

The Death of Nanabushu’s Nephew, the Wolf (1)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Wâsagunäckạnk — Series 1 (Nos. 1–18), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīdạc kā∙i∙jipa‘käguzītā‘tiwāt. Mīdạc kā∙i∙jikạbäciwāt, mīsa’ kägä‘t unisäni īni’ᵘ awäsīyạn. Ānīc kāwīnigu päcigwạnunk ayāsīwạg. Pạnägu pạpāmigusiwạg. Mīdạc kägä‘t ugitcimāmī‘kawi∙ā∙nini īni’ᵘ awäsīyạn. Kägä‘t mīno∙ā∙yā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-bakagozitaadiwaad. Mii dash gaa-izhi-gabeshiwaad, mii sa geget onisaanii iniw awesiyan. Aaniish gaawiinigo bezhigwanong ayaasiwag. Apane go babaamigoziwag. Mii dash geget ogichi-maamiikawaanini* iniw awesiyan. Geget minwaayaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon they separated from one another. And when (he and the Wolf) went into camp, it was truly (the Wolf) that killed the game. Naturally not in one spot they remained, always from one place to another they went. And so truly was (the Wolf) ever killing the pick of game. Truly was he living well.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-bakagozitaadiwaad (and so then they parted from each other) ∙ gaa-izhi-gabeshiwaad (when they made camp) ∙ mii sa geget onisaanii iniw awesiyan (truly it was he that killed those game-animals) ∙ Aaniish gaawiinigo bezhigwanong ayaasiwag (naturally not in one place did they stay) ∙ Apane go babaamigoziwag (always they kept moving camp about) ∙ Mii dash geget ogichi-maamiikawaanini* iniw awesiyan (and so truly he kept coming easily upon those game-animals — *stem flagged*) ∙ Geget minwaayaa (truly he was living well).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Ningutingigu pạpāmigusiwāt, äcimawīngwāminit īni’ᵘ umicōmäyạn. ‘Aᵉa’ᵘ ma∙ī∙ngạn äcinimîskạmāt, “Intạckā nīnītug mānāpạmigwän,” udinān ‘iᵉi’ᵘ kīcigīnipānit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningodingigo babaamigoziwaad, ezhi-mawiingwaaminid iniw omishoomeyan*. A’aw ma’iingan ezhi-nimiskamaad*, “Indashkaa niin idog maanaabamigwen,” odinaan iw giizhi-gii-nibaanid*.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, once while they were moving about, in his sleep was (the Wolf’s) uncle weeping. The Wolf indifferently signed to him with the hand. “I fancy that probably he may be having a bad dream about me,” he said of him who then was taking his nap.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningodingigo babaamigoziwaad (now once while they were moving camp about) ∙ ezhi-mawiingwaaminid iniw omishoomeyan* (his uncle was weeping in his sleep — *kin term flagged*) ∙ A’aw ma’iingan ezhi-nimiskamaad* (that wolf signed to him with his hand — *stem flagged*) ∙ “Indashkaa niin idog maanaabamigwen” (“Behold, I suppose he must be dreaming badly of me”) ∙ odinaan iw giizhi-gii-nibaanid* (he said of the one who had gone to sleep — *stem flagged*).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Mīdạc kā∙i∙cikuskusinit, cīgwa kägä‘t uwīndạmāgōn: “Kägätsa, nintōjîm, kigic‘tcimanābạmin. Ạmbäsạnō, mānū pîsindawicîn kä∙i∙ninān; mānū icictcigän ‘iᵉi’ᵘ käininān. Kīcpîn wīnōntawisiwạn ᵉii’wisa käininān, kägä‘t kīga∙i∙nigā‘tōn ‘iᵉi’ᵘ kīya’ᵘ. Ạmbäsạnō, mîsawā‘ku ạni∙ạ∙timạt waᵉa’ᵘ awäsīⁿ, mānū mi‘tigōns kīgạnabō‘kubitōn mîsawā pạngiⁿs sībīnsiwa‘kamigāk, mīdạc ‘iᵉi’māⁿ käni∙a∙pạgitōyan ‘iᵉi’ᵘ mi‘tigōns. Mīsạgu ‘pạnä kätōtạmạn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-goshkozinid, zhigwa geget owiindamaagoon: “Geget sa, nindoozhim, gigichi-jimanaabamin. Ambesano, manoo bizindawishin gaa-izhi-ininaan; manoo izhichigen iw ge-ininaan. Giishpin wii-noondawisiwan iwsa ge-ininaan, geget giga-inigaa’ton iw giiyaw. Ambesano, mizhishewi-go ani-adimad a’aw awesiinh, manoo mitigoons giga-naboohkobiton mizhishewe bangiinh ziibiinsiwakamigaag, mii dash imaa ge-ni-abagidooyan iw mitigoons. Mii sa go apane ge-doodaman.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon when (Nänabushu) woke, then truly was (the Wolf) informed by him, saying: “Verily, my nephew, have I had an exceedingly bad dream about you. I beg of you, please listen to what I shall say to you; please do what I tell you. If you have no desire to listen to what I have to tell you, truly then will you do yourself an injury. So please, even if it be when you are overtaking game, as you go along break off a little stick, no matter how small the dry bed of a brook may be, and there shall you fling the little stick. That is what you should always do.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-goshkozinid (and so then when he woke) ∙ zhigwa geget owiindamaagoon (now truly he was told by him) ∙ “Geget sa, nindoozhim, gigichi-jimanaabamin” (“Verily, my nephew, I have had a hard dream about you”) ∙ Ambesano, manoo bizindawishin gaa-izhi-ininaan (“please, do listen to what I tell you”) ∙ manoo izhichigen iw ge-ininaan (“do that which I shall tell you”) ∙ Giishpin wii-noondawisiwan iwsa ge-ininaan (“if you will not listen to what I tell you”) ∙ geget giga-inigaa’ton iw giiyaw (“truly you will harm your own body”) ∙ Ambesano, mizhishewi-go ani-adimad a’aw awesiinh (“so please, even when you are overtaking that game”) ∙ manoo mitigoons giga-naboohkobiton mizhishewe bangiinh ziibiinsiwakamigaag (“do break off a little stick, no matter how small the dry brook-bed”) ∙ mii dash imaa ge-ni-abagidooyan iw mitigoons (“and there is where you throw the little stick”) ∙ Mii sa go apane ge-doodaman (“that is just what you should always do”).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Ānīc mīsa gägä‘t äcictcigät mîsawāgu cigwa adimāt. Ānīc ningutingigu piminicawāt īni’ᵘ awäsīⁿyạn — ānīc mīgu ‘panä ‘iᵉi’ᵘ äcictcigät — kägätsa cīgạtci. Ningutingigu kāni∙u∙ndciidạcpiminicawāt īni’ᵘ mōⁿsōn — ānīc mīcigwa ānawi sīgwạninig — mīdạc änäntank ᵉa∙a∙ᵘ ma∙ī∙ngạn piminijawāt īni’ᵘ mōⁿzon, kägä‘t ki‘tciunītcāniwạn īni’ᵘ päminicawāt. Mīdạc cigwa täbābạmāt, mīgu ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ā‘pitci cigwa täbipināt, ugīwâbạndān pạngīⁿs sībīnsiwā‘kamigānig.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aanish mii sa geget ezhichiged mizhishewe-go zhigwa adimad. Aanish ningodingigo bimini-zhaawaad iniw awesiinhyan — aaniish mii go apane iw ezhichiged — geget sa zhiigaji. Ningodingigo gaa-onji-daazhi-bimini-zhaawaad iniw moonzoon — aaniish mii zhigwa aanawi ziigwaninig — mii dash enendang a’aw ma’iingan bimini-zhaawaad iniw moonzoon, geget gichi-oniijaaniwan iniw bemini-zhaawaad. Mii dash zhigwa debaabamaad, mii go iw ezhi-apiichi zhigwa debibinaad, ogii-waabandaan bangiinh ziibiinsiwaakamigaanig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Accordingly that truly was what he did whenever he was about to overtake (the game). Now, once while he was in pursuit of some game, — for that was what he always was doing, — truly, he grew tired (of throwing a stick into the dry bed of brooks). For when once away from a certain place he was in pursuit of a moose, — since it was now getting well on towards the spring, — this was the feeling of the Wolf when in pursuit of the moose, truly a big cow was he following after. And then presently, when he came in sight of her, — indeed, when he was on the very point of seizing her, — he saw the dry bed of a small brook.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aanish mii sa geget ezhichiged (so truly that is what he did) ∙ mizhishewe-go zhigwa adimad (whenever he was overtaking it) ∙ ningodingigo bimini-zhaawaad iniw awesiinhyan (once while chasing those game-animals) ∙ aaniish mii go apane iw ezhichiged (for that is what he always did) ∙ geget sa zhiigaji (truly he grew tired) ∙ gaa-onji-daazhi-bimini-zhaawaad iniw moonzoon (when off chasing a moose) ∙ mii zhigwa aanawi ziigwaninig (it being now nearly spring) ∙ mii dash enendang a’aw ma’iingan (and so this is what that wolf was thinking) ∙ geget gichi-oniijaaniwan iniw bemini-zhaawaad (truly a big cow was the one he was following) ∙ zhigwa debaabamaad (now he caught sight of her) ∙ ezhi-apiichi zhigwa debibinaad (just when he was about to catch her) ∙ ogii-waabandaan bangiinh ziibiinsiwaakamigaanig (he saw a little dry brook-bed).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Kīⁿwäⁿ kī∙i∙nändạm: “Ạmbäsạnō, mīgu iᵘ känī∙i∙ji∙ā∙cạwigwāckwạniyān ‘iᵉi’māⁿ sībīnsiwa‘kạmigānig.” Pạnägu nāwạgām kāpạngicink ᵉi∙i∙māⁿ ki‘tcisibīnk, panägu kāsāswänik u‘tawạgạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Giiwenh gaa-inendang: “Ambesano, mii go iw ge-ni-izhi-aazhawi-gwaashkwaniyaan imaa ziibiinsiwakamigaanig.” Apane go naawagaam gaa-bangishing imaa gichi-zibing, apane go gaa-zeeswenik odawagan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*They say (that thus) he thought: “Well, now, (without throwing the stick ahead of me,) I will leap right on across the dry bed of this brook.” Then straightway down into the middle of a great stream he fell, and all the while was there a ringing in his ears.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Giiwenh gaa-inendang (so they say, this is what he thought) ∙ “Ambesano, mii go iw ge-ni-izhi-aazhawi-gwaashkwaniyaan imaa ziibiinsiwakamigaanig” (“Well, this is where I shall leap across the dry brook-bed”) ∙ Apane go naawagaam gaa-bangishing imaa gichi-zibing (instead, into the middle he fell, there in the great river) ∙ apane go gaa-zeeswenik odawagan (instead, his ears were set ringing).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Mīsa’ pạpimusät Nänabucu äcinīca∙i∙kä‘kawänit īni’ᵘ utōcimạn, cigwa unisitō‘kawä∙ā∙n kā‘ki∙ā∙nigubạnän ningutingigu ạnī∙i∙nābit, pạnägu ki‘tcisībi kābimi‘tigwäyānig. Mīdạc äcimawit Nänabucu. Mīnawā‘ku ningutci ānuwī∙i∙cā, a‘panägu pạbāmạtämut. Kägätsa umindcinawäsinạn īni’ᵘ utōcimạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa baa-bimosed Naanabozho ezhi-niizhaigekawenid* iniw odoozhiman, zhigwa onisidoohkawaan gaa-gii-aanigobanen ningodingigo ani-inaabid, apane go gichi-zibi gaa-bimitigweyaanig. Mii dash ezhi-mawid Naanabozho. Minawaa-go ningoji aano-wii-izhaa, apane go babaamademo. Geget sa ominjinawezaan* iniw odoozhiman.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And now, while along was walking Nänabushu as he followed the trail of his nephew, he presently noticed by the sign of the tracks that (his nephew) was pressing close (upon the moose); and once as he looked while going along, there, to his surprise, was a great river flowing across his path. Thereupon wept Nänabushu. Then repeatedly from place to place in vain he went (to get across), and all the while he wept as he wandered about. Truly sad he felt for his nephew.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa baa-bimosed Naanabozho (and so while Naanabozho was walking about) ∙ ezhi-niizhaigekawenid* iniw odoozhiman (following the trail of his nephew — *stem flagged*) ∙ zhigwa onisidoohkawaan gaa-gii-aanigobanen (now he read in the tracks that he had been gaining on it) ∙ ningodingigo ani-inaabid (once as he looked along) ∙ apane go gichi-zibi gaa-bimitigweyaanig (there, a great river flowing across) ∙ Mii dash ezhi-mawid Naanabozho (and so Naanabozho wept) ∙ Minawaa-go ningoji aano-wii-izhaa (again here and there he tried in vain) ∙ apane go babaamademo (continually he wept as he wandered) ∙ Geget sa ominjinawezaan* iniw odoozhiman (truly he loved his nephew dearly — *stem flagged*)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Ānīc ugi‘käntān awīya tōtāgut. Kā∙i∙jimādcāt nīsātciwạn ‘iᵉi’ᵘ sībi. Ānīc ạnibimạtämu. Mīdạc kā∙i∙jisāgitawācạgāmät ‘iᵉi’ᵘ sībi, mīdạc i∙i∙māⁿ owâbạmān īni’ᵘ ugīckimạnisīn inābiwạn i∙i∙māⁿ nibī‘kāng. Äcinawạtinät, mīdạc kā∙i∙cipicigunāt uctigwānining ānugīna∙u∙tînāt. Mīdạc kā∙i∙gut: “Micạnîm Nänabucu! Niwīwīndạmawābạn wīn aᵘ,” ugī∙i∙gōn. “Tạga, wīndamawicîn,” ugī∙i∙nān. Mīdạc kägä‘t kā‘pī∙i∙ji∙i∙cānit. “Tạgackumā, wīndạmawicîn, nîcimîsa,” ugī∙i nān īni’ᵘ ugīckimạnisīn. O∙ō∙widạc ugī i∙gōn: “Äyeᵉ, kīgawīndamōn. Kīn māwīn Nänabucu,” ugī∙i∙gōn. “Kāwīn,” ugī∙i∙nān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aanish ogikendaan awiya doodaagod. Gaa-izhi-maajaad niisaajiwan i’iw ziibi. Aanish ani-bimademo. Mii dash gaa-izhi-zaagidawaazhagaamed i’iw ziibi, mii dash imaa owaabamaan iniw ozhishkimaniisin inaabiwan imaa nibiikaang. Ezhi-nawadinad*, mii dash gaa-izhi-bizhigonad oshtigwaanining aano-ginaawa’odinad. Mii dash gaa-ig: “Mizhanim Naanabozho! Niwii-wiindamawaaban win a’aw,” ogii-igoon. “Daga, wiindamawishin,” ogii-inaan. Mii dash geget gaa-bizhi-izhaanid. “Daga shkomaa, wiindamawishin, nishiimisa,” ogii-inaan iniw ozhishkimaniisin. O’o-widash ogii-igoon: “Eye’, giga-wiindamoon. Giin maawiin Naanabozho,” ogii-igoon. “Gaawiin,” ogii-inaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, (Nänabushu) knew that by somebody was he (thus) treated. Afterwards he started down the course of the river. Now, he wept as he went. And now, when he had followed the course of the river to where it opened out (into another body of water), then there he beheld the kingfisher looking down into the water. He made a grab for him, but he slipped hold of him at the head when he tried to seize him. And this was what he was told: “Confound Nänabushu! I meant to tell him something,” he was told. “Pray, do tell me,” he said to him. Thereupon truly hither came (the Kingfisher). “Do please tell me, my little brother,” he said to the Kingfisher. So this he was told: “Yes, I will tell you. But you must be Nänabushu,” he was told. “No,” he said to him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aanish ogikendaan awiya doodaagod (now he knew that someone had done this to him) ∙ Gaa-izhi-maajaad niisaajiwan i’iw ziibi (he started off down the course of that river) ∙ Aanish ani-bimademo (and he wept as he went) ∙ Mii dash gaa-izhi-zaagidawaazhagaamed i’iw ziibi (and then, where that river opened out at its mouth) ∙ mii dash imaa owaabamaan iniw ozhishkimaniisin inaabiwan imaa nibiikaang (and there he saw the kingfisher looking into the water) ∙ Ezhi-nawadinad* (so he grabbed at it — *sort flagged*) ∙ mii dash gaa-izhi-bizhigonad oshtigwaanining (and it slipped from him by the head) ∙ aano-ginaawa’odinad (as he tried in vain to hold it) ∙ Mii dash gaa-ig: “Mizhanim Naanabozho! <span class="l-orig">(8) Wo∙ō∙ idạc ugī∙i∙gōn: “Kā, ạnicā Nänabucu udōciman kīma‘kạmā. Mī∙a’∙ᵘ ugimāmîcīpicī, mī∙a’∙ᵘ kāma‘kạmāt īni’ᵘ Nänabucōwạn udōcimini. <span class="l-fiero">**O’o-widash ogii-igoon: “Gaa, aanisha Naanabozho odoozhiman gii-makamaa. Mii a’aw ogimaa-mishibizhii, mii a’aw gaa-makamaad iniw Naanabozhowan odoozhimini. <span class="l-eng">*So this he was told: “Ay, without reason was Nänabushu’s nephew taken away from him. It was the chief of the great lynxes,¹ it was he who took away Nänabushu’s nephew. <span class="l-fiero">**“Geget sa awawa, ambesano, waawiindamawishin,” ogii-inaan iniw ozhishkimaniisin. “Meshkwad ji-onizhishiyan giga-izhi’in.” Mii dash geget gaa-izhi-naakomigod. <span class="l-eng">*“Truly, then, all right! Pray, go ahead and tell me about it,” he said to the Kingfisher. “In return I will make you so that you will be beautiful.” Thereupon he truly was willing to do what he was asked.
