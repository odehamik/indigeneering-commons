30\. NÄNABUSHU AND THE WOLVES.¹

<span class="l-fiero">**30. Nenabozho gaye ma’iinganag.**</span>

Nänabushu and the Wolves (2)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by probably Midāsugaⁿj (Michelson’s own hedge, carried) — Series 2 (Nos. 19–32), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ningutingigu sāga∙i∙gạn omạdā‘kun. Äjimādcī∙ā∙dạgā‘kut, wạbigạmānig päcwâbandạnk, awiya owâbạmān pimādagā‘kupa‘tōnit; āca mīnawā, nīwiwaᵉ. Kuniginīn, ma∙ī∙nganaᵉ! Äjipīpāgimāt: “Niˢtcīmiˢtcā, a‘kawä, kawâbạmininim!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningodingigo zaaga’igan omadaakon. Ezhi-maajii’aadagaakod, waabigamaanig beshwaabandang, awiya owaabamaan bimaadagaa-gobadoonid; aazha minawaa, niiwiwag. Goniginin, ma’iinganag! Ezhi-biibaagimaad: “Nishiimeyidog, akawe, gawaabamininim!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, by and by he came out upon a frozen lake. As he started forth on the ice, as nigh to the narrows of the lake he drew, he saw some one running past over the ice; then some more, four of them. Behold, (they were) Wolves! Then he called aloud to them: “O my little brothers! wait, I wish to see you.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningodingigo zaaga’igan omadaakon (once, a lake he comes out upon) ∙ Ezhi-maajii’aadagaakod (as he set out walking on the ice) ∙ waabigamaanig beshwaabandang (the narrows of the lake, coming near in sight of it) ∙ awiya owaabamaan bimaadagaa-gobadoonid (someone he sees running past over the ice) ∙ aazha minawaa, niiwiwag (and then again, there were four of them) ∙ Goniginin, ma’iinganag! (behold, wolves!) ∙ Ezhi-biibaagimaad (then he called out to them) ∙ “Nishiimeyidog, akawe, gawaabamininim!” (“my little brothers, wait, I will see you!”)</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Geget gii-bijiba’towag; eshi-wawenabinid aninaazikawaad. Ezhi-ganoonaad: “Niijisaziikizi, aandi ezhaayeg?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*To be sure, they came to a sudden halt; then they sat down, while he went up to where they were. Then he spoke to them, saying: “My old friend, whither are you going?”*</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) “Kā, o∙ō´∙witi, kīcika‘tikwạning, mī∙i∙witi äcāyāng. Nībinunk ki∙ạ∙sạntcigōbạnig ogō´ᵘ kitōcimạg, ki‘tci∙a∙yābän ugīnisāwâbạnīn. Mīdạc iwiti äcāyāng.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaa, o’oowidi, gizhigaatigwaning, mii-iwidi ezhaayaang. Niibinong gii-asanjigoobanig ogow gidoozhimag, gichi-ayaaben oginisaawaabaniin. Mii dash iwidi ezhaayaang.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Oh, over here, for the place of cedar boughs, is where we are bound. Last summer did these nephews of yours make a cache there, a great bull (moose) they killed then. Now, that is the place (for which) we are bound.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gaa, o’oowidi (oh, over here) ∙ gizhigaatigwaning (at the place of cedar boughs) ∙ mii-iwidi ezhaayaang (that is where we are going) ∙ Niibinong gii-asanjigoobanig (last summer they made a cache) ∙ ogow gidoozhimag (these your nephews) ∙ gichi-ayaaben oginisaawaabaniin (a great bull-moose they killed) ∙ Mii dash iwidi ezhaayaang (and that is where we are going)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) “Ä‘tiwä, mī gayä nīn iwiti äcāyān, kīcika‘tikwạning, — mīsa´ ‘iᵉi´ᵘ tci∙ạ∙niwâwītcīwinaguk.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Etiwe, mii gaye niin iwidi ezhaayaan, gizhigaatigwaning, — mii-sa ’i’iw ji-aniwaa-wiijiiwinagog.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Why, that is the place, too, for which I am bound, to the place of cedar boughs, so therefore it is my wish to go along with you.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Etiwe (why) ∙ mii gaye niin iwidi ezhaayaan (that too is where I am going) ∙ gizhigaatigwaning (to the place of cedar boughs) ∙ mii-sa ’i’iw ji-aniwaa-wiijiiwinagog (so it is that I will go along with you)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Ānīc, mīsa´ gägä‘t Nänabucu aninạnta∙u∙ninamāt. Kāwäsā ni‘tāwusäsī, ānuwītcīwāt ‘iᵉi´ᵘ ma∙ī∙ngạnaᵉ. Äjikạnōnint Nänabucu: “Ạmbäsinō, äntōtạmāngigu pimiba‘tōyāng, mī kayä kīn kätōtạmạn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aniish, mii-sa geget Nenabozho ani-nanda’oninamaad. Gaawesaa nitaawosesii, aano-wiijiiwaad ’i’iw ma’iingana. Ezhi-ganoonind Nenabozho: “Ambesino, endoodamaangigo bimiba’toyaang, mii gaye giin ge-doodaman.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So thereupon, truly did Nänabushu go to find a place to camp. Not at all was he familiar with (their way of) travelling, as he tried going along with the Wolves. Then was Nänabushu told: “Come, as we do when we run along, so in like manner should you do too.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aniish, mii-sa geget Nenabozho ani-nanda’oninamaad (so truly Nänabushu went to look for a camping place) ∙ Gaawesaa nitaawosesii (not at all was he skilled at it) ∙ aano-wiijiiwaad ’i’iw ma’iingana (though he tried to go along with the wolf) ∙ Ezhi-ganoonind Nenabozho (Nänabushu was told) ∙ “Ambesino, endoodamaangigo bimiba’toyaang (come, just as we do when we run along) ∙ mii gaye giin ge-doodaman (that too is what you should do)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Tāyā, kayä wīn tōtạm. Ānīc mīdạc iᵘ kāwīn kägō ‘iᵉi´ᵘ osō, mīdạc ‘iᵉi´ᵘ wīnạg ‘iᵉi´ᵘ wäsowāt. Kāwīn kạnagä nōmạg cigwa ạnimaskawā‘kwatininig.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Daayaa, gaye wiin doodam. Aniish mii dash iw gaawiin gego ’i’iw ozow, mii dash ’i’iw winag ’i’iw wezowad. Gaawiin ganage noomag zhigwa ani-mashkawaakwadininig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Ah, and he did the same. Naturally, there was nothing in the way of a tail, therefore his penis was what he used for a tail. It was but a very little while before it was frozen stiff.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Daayaa, gaye wiin doodam (ah, he too did so) ∙ Aniish mii dash iw gaawiin gego ’i’iw ozow (and there was nothing for a tail) ∙ mii dash ’i’iw winag ’i’iw wezowad (so his penis was what he used for a tail) ∙ Gaawiin ganage noomag zhigwa ani-mashkawaakwadininig (it was not even a little while before it froze stiff)</span>
