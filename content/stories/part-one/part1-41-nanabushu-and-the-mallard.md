<span class="l-fiero">**Nenabozho gaye Zhiishiib**</span>

Nänabushu and the Mallard (2)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by probably Wâsagunäckạnk (Michelson’s own hedge, carried) — Series 5 (Nos. 40–42), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Wäyābạninig äjimādcāt kīyusät, kāwäsā kägō uni‘tōsīn. Mōⁿcạg ānukī∙u∙sä, mīsōntcita kāwīn kägō uni‘tōsī. Mīsa´ ā‘pidci pa‘kadät. Cigwa wäyābạninig äjimādcāt Nänabucu, mīdạc ‘iᵉi´ᵘ pạbāạntakīyōtät. Ninguting utō‘kawi∙ā∙n ạnicinābäᵉ, omāta∙ạ∙nāt. Kumāgu a‘pī tägwicink, owâbạndān ändānit. Änijipīndigät ininiwạn nāmadapiwạn, kayä wīn i‘kwäwạn kayä ‘iᵉi´ᵘ unīdcānisini. Äjikạnōnint: “ ‘A‘a´ᵘ, nāmạdạpîn!” inā Nänabucu.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Waabaninig ezhi-maajaad giiyosed, gaawesa gegoo o-nita-oosin. Moozhag anokii-osed, miisozhtigo gaawiin gegoo o-nita-oosii. Mii sa aapidji bakaded. Zhigwa waabaninig ezhi-maajaad Nenabozho, mii dash iwedi babaa-andakiiyaad. Ningoding odo-okawi-aanan anishinaabe, omaadaanaad. Gomaa go apii dagwishing, owaabandaan endaanid. Ani-zhi-biindiged ininiwan namadabiwan, gaye wiin ikwewan gaye iniw oodaanisini. Ezhi-ganoonind: “Aaw, namadabin!” ina Nenabozho.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When the morrow was come, then off he went on a hunt for game, but not a thing did he kill. Continually without result did he hunt; and, in spite of all he could do, nothing did he kill. Thereupon very hungry did he become. Then on the morrow away went Nänabushu, it was to wander from place to place visiting (old friends). Once he came upon the footprints of some people, in whose trail he then followed. When some distance farther on he was come, he saw where they lived. On entering in, (he saw) a man that was seated there, likewise a woman and their children. He was addressed: “Welcome! be seated!” was told Nänabushu.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Waabaninig ezhi-maajaad giiyosed (when morning came, off he went hunting) ∙ gaawesa gegoo o-nita-oosin (not a thing did he manage to kill) ∙ Moozhag anokii-osed (continually as he hunted) ∙ miisozhtigo gaawiin gegoo o-nita-oosii (do all he might, nothing did he kill) ∙ Mii sa aapidji bakaded (then very hungry he became) ∙ Zhigwa waabaninig ezhi-maajaad Nenabozho (now next morning off went Nenabozho) ∙ mii dash iwedi babaa-andakiiyaad (and there he went about visiting) ∙ Ningoding odo-okawi-aanan anishinaabe (once he came on the tracks of people) ∙ omaadaanaad (he followed their trail) ∙ Gomaa go apii dagwishing (some way on when he arrived) ∙ owaabandaan endaanid (he saw where they lived) ∙ Ani-zhi-biindiged (going in he entered) ∙ ininiwan namadabiwan (a man who was seated) ∙ gaye wiin ikwewan gaye iniw oodaanisini (and also a woman and their children) ∙ Ezhi-ganoonind: “Aaw, namadabin!” (he was addressed: “All right, sit down!”) ∙ ina Nenabozho (was Nenabozho told).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Kīgitōwạn īni´ᵘ ininiwan: “Wägunäcinā kägīgä∙ạ∙nk aᵘ pīwitä? Tạga, mīnōtc nibi ạnạgōtōn,” inimāwạn īni´ᵘ i‘kwäwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Giigidowan iniw ininiwan: “Wegonezhinaa ge-giigaag a’aw biiwide? Daga, miinotch nibi anagotoon,” inimaawan iniw ikwewan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then up spoke the man: “What have we to offer the guest (to eat)? Well, anyhow, hang up (a kettle of) water!” he said to the woman.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Giigidowan iniw ininiwan (then spoke the man) ∙ “Wegonezhinaa ge-giigaag a’aw biiwide? (what is there for that visitor to have?) ∙ Daga, miinotch nibi anagotoon,” (well, anyhow, hang up some water) ∙ inimaawan iniw ikwewan (he said to the woman).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Kägä‘t, ạgi‘kwạn äciwanạgōnāt a∙i∙‘kwä. Mägwāgu nạmạdạpinit īni´ᵘ ininiwạn, undcipạsigu∙ō∙wạn, nōndāgusiwạn: “Kwänk, kwänk, kwänk,” inwäwän. Mīdạc imāⁿ midā‘kupitcigạning äcipōnīnint, nōndāgusint: “Kwänk, kwänk,” inwänit. Ä‘tawā, kuniginīn äcimīsinint īmāⁿ a‘kikunk, kīgitōwạn: “ ‘A‘a´ᵘ mīnōtc anä∙ạ∙n!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gegget, agiikwan ezhi-wanagoonaad a-ikwe. Megwaa go namadabinid iniw ininiwan, ondji-bazigwiiwan, noondaagosiwan: “Gwaank, gwaank, gwaank!” inwewan. Mii dash imaa midaakobijiganing ezhi-booninind, noondaagozid: “Gwaank, gwaank,” inwenid. Ah-tawe, goniginiin ezhi-miizinind imaa akikong, giigidowan: “Aaw miinotch ane-an!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Truly, then a kettle did the woman hang up. And while the man was seated, up he flew, and was heard to say, “Kwänk, kwänk, kwänk!” (such) was what he uttered. And then yonder upon the cross-pole (above the fire) he alighted, being heard to say, “Kwänk, kwänk!” (such) was the sound he uttered. Oh, how strange that when he muted into the kettle, he was saying, “Come on, pay no heed, but keep it stirring!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gegget, agiikwan ezhi-wanagoonaad a-ikwe (truly a kettle the woman hung up) ∙ Megwaa go namadabinid iniw ininiwan (while the man was sitting) ∙ ondji-bazigwiiwan (up he flew) ∙ noondaagosiwan: “Gwaank, gwaank, gwaank!” inwewan (heard, “Gwaank…”, such his cry) ∙ Mii dash imaa midaakobijiganing ezhi-booninind (then there on the cross-pole he lit) ∙ noondaagozid: “Gwaank, gwaank,” inwenid (heard saying, “Gwaank, gwaank”) ∙ Ah-tawe, goniginiin (oh, lo and behold) ∙ ezhi-miizinind imaa akikong (he let go into the kettle there) ∙ giigidowan: “Aaw miinotch ane-an!” (he said: “All right, never mind, keep stirring it!”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Kägä‘t ‘aᵉa´ᵘ udạnä∙ā∙n. Mägwāgu änä∙ạ∙nk, kuniginīn, mạnōmin ā‘pidci mōckinä i∙i∙māⁿ a‘ki‘kunk; kayä pänkutä. “ ‘Aᵘ, mī∙i´∙ᵘ ici∙ạ∙gwācîm.” Mīdạc ‘iᵉi´ᵘ äcinīsipōnīnint. “Mīsagunā iᵘ kayä nīn ä∙i∙cīyān wāwīsiniyānin.” Mīnangwạna īninciban wätisāt. Kāwunạbinit, “Ạmbäsa´, unagạnink a‘tōn,” īnimāwạn īni´ᵘ i‘kwäwạn, “ā‘pidcigu mōckinā‘tōn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gegget a’aw odane’aan. Megwaa go ene’ang, goniginiin, manoomin aapidji mooshkine imaa akikong; gaye bengode*. “Aaw, mii i’iw izhi-agwaashim.” Mii dash iniw ezhi-niisi-booninind. “Mii sa go naa iw gaye niin ezhi-ayaan waa-wiisiniyaanin.” Mii-nangwana ininishiban* wedisaad. Gaa-onabinid, “Ambesa, onaaganing atoon,” inimaawan iniw ikwewan, “aapidjigo mooshkinatoon.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Truly she stirred it. And while she was stirring it, lo, very full of rice was the kettle there; and it was cooked dry. “All right! now take it off the fire.” And then down he flew, alighting. “Now, this is only a way I have whenever I want to eat.” It happened to be a Mallard whom he had come to visit. After the Mallard was seated, “Come, into a vessel do you put it!” he said to the woman, “and very full do you fill it.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gegget a’aw odane’aan (truly she stirred it) ∙ Megwaa go ene’ang, goniginiin, manoomin aapidji mooshkine imaa akikong (while she was stirring it, lo, very full of rice was the kettle there) ∙ gaye bengode (and it was cooked dry) ∙ “Aaw, mii i’iw izhi-agwaashim.” (“All right, now take it off the fire.”) ∙ Mii dash iniw ezhi-niisi-booninind (and then down he flew, alighting) ∙ “Mii sa go naa iw gaye niin ezhi-ayaan waa-wiisiniyaanin.” (“Now, this is only a way I have whenever I want to eat.”) ∙ Mii-nangwana ininishiban wedisaad (it happened to be a Mallard whom he had come to visit) ∙ Gaa-onabinid, “Ambesa, onaaganing atoon,” inimaawan iniw ikwewan, “aapidjigo mooshkinatoon.” (after the Mallard was seated, “Come, into a vessel do you put it!” he said to the woman, “and very full do you fill it.”) — *flagged* bengode, ezhi-ayaan, ininishiban, wedisaad and Gaa-onabinid: hapax verbs with no lexicon or corpus witness, mechanically derived by character rule from Jones’s printed page (p.350 ll.18–24); everything else drafted from this record’s own established vocabulary (a’aw, mii, iniw, gegget, imaa, akikong, gaye, aapidji, goniginiin, manoomin, ikwewan, atoon, mooshkina’toon) or the shared root anaa’- (‘keep stirring’, used unflagged 4x elsewhere in this record) and agwaas- (§22’s agwaasidoog).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Kägä‘t i‘kwä omōckina‘tōn ‘iᵉi´ᵘ unāgạn. “ ‘Aa´ᵘ, Nänabucu, wīsinîn!” Nänabucu äjimātạntcigät. Ā‘pidci täwīsinit, äjānici‘tạnk.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gegget ikwe omooshkinatoon iniw onaagan. “Aaw, Nenabozho, wiisinin!” Nenabozho ezhi-maadanjiged. Aapidji dewiisinid, ezhi-aanizhitang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Truly, the woman filled up the bowl. “All right, Nänabushu, do you eat!” Nänabushu then began eating. When his desire for food was quite appeased, then he ceased (eating).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gegget ikwe omooshkinatoon iniw onaagan (truly the woman filled up the bowl) ∙ “Aaw, Nenabozho, wiisinin!” (“All right, Nenabozho, eat!”) ∙ Nenabozho ezhi-maadanjiged (Nenabozho then began to eat) ∙ Aapidji dewiisinid (when he had eaten his fill) ∙ ezhi-aanizhitang (then he left off).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) “Mīna´ mini‘k wāsinīyạn?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Miinaa minik we-wiisiniyan?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Is that all you are going to eat?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Miinaa minik we-wiisiniyan?” (“Is that the amount you will eat?”).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) “Ānīn dạc kä∙i∙cikagāntạckinēyān?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniin dash ge-izhi-gagaandashkineyaan?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“And how am I to force myself to eat (more)?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniin dash ge-izhi-…?” (“And how then am I to make myself eat more?”) — gagaandashkine- (to cram/force oneself full): resolved via character rule ạc→ash (185 evidence) and lexicon witness kä∙i∙cikagāntạckinēyän → ge-izhi-gagaandashkineyaan.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) “Nänabucu, mīgu∙i∙ᵘ icikīwäwic waᵉa´ᵘ tcībā‘kwān. Māgicā kinītcānisạg pītạmaᵘ.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Nenabozho, mii go iw izhi-giiwewish a’aw jiibaakwaan. Maagizhaa giniijaanisag biidamaw.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Nänabushu, therefore then do you take back to them at home the rest of the cooked food. Perhaps to your children do you take it home.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Nenabozho, mii go iw izhi-giiwewish a’aw jiibaakwaan (Nenabozho, so then carry home that cooked food) ∙ Maagizhaa giniijaanisag biidamaw” (perhaps bring it to your children) — giiwewish (take home, deprecatory/‘the leavings’): resolved, exact lexicon match.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Nänabucu äjikīgitut: “Mīcigwa wī‘kīwäyān.” Mīgu∙i∙māⁿ, wạnimō‘k, wäntcicägunāt ‘iᵉi´ᵘ ugīcō‘tä∙u∙naᵉ. Äjisāga∙ạ∙nk, päcu´ äni∙a∙yāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenabozho ezhi-giigidod: “Mii zhigwa wii-giiweyaan.” Mii go imaa, wanimook, wenjishegonaad* iniw ogiizhoode’onan. Ezhi-zaaga’ang, besho ani-ayaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Nänabushu then spoke, saying: “It is now time for me to go back home.” And so, when no one was looking, in under (the mat) he pushed his mittens. When he went outside, then near by did he tarry.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nenabozho ezhi-giigidod: “Mii zhigwa wii-giiweyaan.” (Nenabozho spoke: “Now I will go home.”) ∙ Mii go imaa, wanimook (and right there, when none were looking) ∙ wenjishegonaad iniw ogiizhoode’onan (he tucked away/hid his mittens) ∙ Ezhi-zaaga’ang (then he went outside) ∙ besho ani-ayaad (close by he stayed) — ogiizhoode’onan (‘mitten’ stem, Jones ugīcō‘tä∙u∙naᵉ): settled 2026-09-02 from the page’s c = zh, with the corpus behind it (story_p1_37 §5/§7, story_p1_40 §7/§9, story_p1_35 §24); wenjishegonaad (the hiding verb) *flagged* — no lexicon or corpus witness found; stays flagged, FOR M.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Mīwīni∙i´∙ᵘ ä‘kitut ‘aᵉa´ᵘ inini: “Ạmbäsinō, kägu iciwī‘tawā‘käg ‘iᵉi´ᵘ ugīcō‘tä∙u∙na ‘aᵉa´ᵘ Nänabucu.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii wini iw ekidod a’aw inini: “Ambesino, gego izhiwidawaakeg* iniw ogiizhoode’ona a’aw Nenabozho.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And this was what the man said: “Please do not carry to Nänabushu his mittens.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii wini iw ekidod a’aw inini (and this is what the man said) ∙ “Ambesino, gego izhiwidawaakeg iniw ogiizhoode’ona a’aw Nenabozho.” (“Please, do not (any of you) carry his mittens to Nenabozho.”) — resolved: ambesino by corpus-wide consensus (dozens of Ambesino/Ambe sa noo witnesses across the corpus); the ‘mitten’ stem settled as ogiizhoode’ona (see §9). izhiwidawaakeg* stays flagged: the page’s iciwī‘tawā‘käg gives -dawaa- (matching this record’s own §11 izhiwidawaasii), but the whole inflected form is attested nowhere.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Mīsa kägä‘t kāwīn iciwītawāsī. Cigwa Nänabucu kwīnawīpi∙u∙ tcibīciwītawint. Äcipīpāgit: “Nīwunitcigāgimä!” Kāwīn ānubisiskitawāsī. Äckạm änigu‘k äjipīpāgit. Gäga‘pī, “Mānū, iciwitawi‘k; wâsa undci∙a∙pagitawi‘k ‘iᵉi´ᵘ ugīcō‘tä∙u∙na.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa gegget gaawiin izhiwidawaasii. Zhigwa Nenabozho gwiinawii-bii’o ji-bi-izhiwidawind. Ezhi-biibaagid: “Niwanichigaagime!” Gaawiin aano-bizindawaasii. Eshkam enigok ezhi-biibaagid. Gegaapii, “Maanoo, izhiwidawik; waasa ondji-a-bagidawik iniw ogiizhoode’ona.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so, truly, he did not have them fetched to him. Already was Nänabushu becoming tired of waiting to have them brought to him. Then with a loud voice he called: “I have forgotten my mittens!” He was not harkened to. Then with a louder voice he called. At last, “Well, go take them to him; from afar do you throw him his mittens.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa gegget gaawiin izhiwidawaasii (and so truly they were not taken to him) ∙ Zhigwa Nenabozho gwiinawii-bii’o ji-bi-izhiwidawind (now Nenabozho grew tired of waiting to have them brought) ∙ Ezhi-biibaagid: “Niwanichigaagime!” (he called out loud: “I have forgotten my mittens!”) ∙ Gaawiin aano-bizindawaasii (he was not listened to) ∙ Eshkam enigok ezhi-biibaagid (louder and louder he called) ∙ Gegaapii, “Maanoo, izhiwidawik; (at last, “Well, take them to him;) ∙ waasa ondji-a-bagidawik iniw ogiizhoode’ona.” (from afar throw him his mittens.”) — Niwanichigaagime (‘I forgot’ verb): resolved by corpus witness story_p1_42 §29 (Kiwanitcigāgimä → Giwanichigaagime, a parallel telling with a different person prefix, same stem).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) Cigwa owâbạmā kwīwisänsaᵉ. “Ictä, päcu´ pīcāyu‘k! Mīnạngwạna iᵘ äcipa‘kadäyä‘k. Kāwīn nīmpạ‘kudäsī. Wâbạnk kōsiwā tạbīcā mämwätcigu nāwa‘kwänig.” Mīdạc ‘iᵉi´ᵘ änīcimādcāt Nänabucu. Kā‘tạgwicink äntāt, kägä‘t mōtcigisiwaᵉ unīdcānisaᵉ wīsininit, kayä īni´ᵘ wīwạn; ā‘pidci täwīsiniwạg. Wäyābạninig mīsa cigwa äjipī∙u∙t īni´ᵘ pīwitämạn, pī∙ā∙t nāwa‘kwänig. Cigwa tagwicinōn. Kāwunạbinit, “Wägunäcina kä∙ạ∙camạnk ‘aᵉa´ᵘ pīwitä? mīnōtc a‘ki‘k unạgōc.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa owaabamaa gwiiwizensan. “Ishte, besho biizhaayog! Mii-nangwana iw ezhi-bakadeyeg. Gaawiin nimbakodesii. Waabang goosiwaa dabiizhaa memwech igo naawakwenig.” Mii dash iniw ani-izhi-maajaad Nenabozho. Gaa-dagwishing endaad, gegget moojigiziwan oniijaanisan wiisininid, gaye iniw wiiwan; aapidji dewiisiniwag. Waabaninig mii sa zhigwa ezhi-bii’od iniw biiwideman, bii’aad naawakwenig. Zhigwa dagwishinoon. Gaa-onabinid, “Wegonezhinaa ge-ashamang a’aw biiwide? Miinotch akik onagoosh.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Presently he saw the boys. “Why, come you up close! And so it is a fact that you are without food. I am not hungry. To-morrow let your father come over exactly at noon.” Thereupon away then went Nänabushu. When he was come at where he lived, truly pleased were his children to have food to eat, so the same with his wife; thoroughly were they satisfied with food. On the morrow he then waited for his guest, he waited for him at noon. Soon he was come. When he was seated, “What have we to feed the guest? Anyway, hang up the kettle.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa owaabamaa gwiiwizensan (now he saw the little boys) ∙ “Ishte, besho biizhaayog! (“Hey, come close!”) ∙ Mii-nangwana iw ezhi-bakadeyeg (so it is that you are hungry) ∙ Gaawiin nimbakodesii (I am not hungry myself) ∙ Waabang goosiwaa dabiizhaa memwech igo naawakwenig” (to-morrow let your father come exactly at noon) ∙ Mii dash iniw ani-izhi-maajaad Nenabozho (thereupon away then went Nenabozho) ∙ Gaa-dagwishing endaad, gegget moojigiziwan oniijaanisan wiisininid, gaye iniw wiiwan (when he was come at where he lived, truly pleased were his children to have food to eat, so the same with his wife) ∙ aapidji dewiisiniwag (thoroughly were they satisfied with food) ∙ Waabaninig mii sa zhigwa ezhi-bii’od iniw biiwideman, bii’aad naawakwenig (on the morrow he then went for his guest, arriving at noon) ∙ Zhigwa dagwishinoon (soon he was come) ∙ Gaa-onabinid, “Wegonezhinaa ge-ashamang a’aw biiwide? <span class="l-orig">(13) Kägä‘t, Nänabucu wīwạn utōnạgōtōn ‘iᵉi´ᵘ nipi.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gegget, Nenabozho wiiwan odoonagotoon iniw nibi.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*To be sure, the wife of Nänabushu hung up the (kettle of) water.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gegget, Nenabozho wiiwan odoonagotoon iniw nibi (to be sure, Nenabozho’s wife hung up the water).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) “Änigu‘k pạgitînisän, wäwīp tạ´wanso aᵘ a‘kik.” Tayā, mägwāgu nạmạdạpit Nänabucu undcīpạsingutcisä nōndāgusit: “Kwänk, kwänk, kwänk!” inwät Nänabucu. Kistciwī‘kā äjikạcki∙u∙t imāⁿ mitā‘kupitcigạning Nänabucu. Kāwänạgōsit imāⁿ mi‘tā‘kupitciganing, änigu‘k kintcitạnāmu ānuwī‘kwatcimīsīt; ubwānawi‘tōn. Ningutingigu umisā‘kutcān äjipạngisinini‘t i∙i∙māⁿ a‘ki‘kunk, ugạnōnān wīwạn: “Mīnōtc! anä∙ạ∙n.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Enigok bagidinisen, wewiib daa-wanzo a’aw akik,” Tayaa… megwaa go namadabid Nenabozho, onji-bazigonjise noondaagosid: “Gwaank, gwaank, gwaank!” inwed Nenabozho.
