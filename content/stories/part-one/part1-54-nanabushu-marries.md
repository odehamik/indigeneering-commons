54\. NÄNABUSHU MARRIES.

Nanabushu Marries

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Penessi — Series 7 (Nos. 44–56), Fort William (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ānīc, ningudingsa kīⁿwäⁿ Nänabucu kībabimusä pạbāmādisit, wīnä‘tagu nici‘kä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish, ningoding sa giiwenh Nenaboozhoo gii-babimose babaamaadizid, wiin eta go nishike.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Well, once on a time they say Nänabushu went walking along, travelling from place to place, and all alone.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish, ningoding sa giiwenh (well, once upon a time, they say) Nenaboozhoo gii-babimose (Nenaboozhoo went walking along) babaamaadizid (travelling about from place to place), wiin eta go nishike (he all alone by himself).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Käga‘pī ninguding ạnicinābäᵉ odōtisān; imā dạc ayāwāt īgi’ᵘ anicinābäᵉg u‘pimäya∙ī∙ dạc imā owâbandān wīgiwāmäns ayānik.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gegapii ningoding anishinaaben odoodisaan; imaa dash ayaawaad igiw anishinaabeg opimeya’ii dash imaa owaabandaan wiigiwaamens ayaanig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then in due course of time to where some people were he came; now, off at one side of where the people were, he saw a small wigwam standing. (1)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gegapii ningoding (then at last, one time) anishinaaben odoodisaan (he came upon some people); imaa dash ayaawaad igiw anishinaabeg (and there where those people were) opimeya’ii dash imaa (off at one side there) owaabandaan wiigiwaamens ayaanig (he saw a little wigwam standing).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Mīdạc kā∙i∙jināⁿzi‘kạng, kīta‘pābi dạc ickwāndänk; i‘kwäwạn owâbamān nạmadạbinit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-naazikang, gii-dapaabi dash ishkwaandeng; ikwewan owaabamaan namadabinid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Accordingly, when he went up to it, he peeped in at the entry-way; a woman he saw seated there.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-naazikang (and so, when he went up to it), gii-dapaabi dash ishkwaandeng (he peeped in at the doorway); ikwewan owaabamaan namadabinid (a woman he saw sitting there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Kāwīn kạnagä pīnābisīwạn; a‘pī∙i∙dạc kä‘känimigut mīkạnōnigut: “Kägu intawā pīndikä‘kän,” udigōn; “undcita omāⁿ nijikä nindaiyā,” udigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin ganage bi-inaabisiiwan; apii idash gekenimigod mii ganoonigod: “Gego indawaa biindigeken,” odigoon; “onjida omaa nishike nindayaa,” odigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Not even did she glance up at him; and when his presence became known, then was he spoken to (in these words): “Do not enter in, I pray,” he was told; “especially since I am here alone,” (2) he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin ganage bi-inaabisiiwan (not at all did she even glance up); apii idash gekenimigod (and when she became aware of him) mii ganoonigod (then she spoke to him): “Gego indawaa biindigeken,” (“do not come in, please,”) odigoon (she said to him); “onjida omaa nishike nindayaa,” (“especially since I am here alone,”) odigoon (she told him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) “Mānu, ningapīndigä!” udinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Maanoo, ninga-piindige!” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Please let me come in!” he said to her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Maanoo, ninga-piindige!” (“please, let me come in!”) odinaan (he said to her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) “Kägo pīndikä‘kän,” udigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gego biindigeken,” odigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Do not come in,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gego biindigeken,” (“do not come in,”) odigoon (she told him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) “Kāwīn awiya omāⁿ tapīndikäsī.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaawiin awiya omaa da-piindigesii.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Nobody is allowed to enter here.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gaawiin awiya omaa da-piindigesii.” (“no one may come in here.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Kāwīn kạnagä pī∙u∙ndci∙i∙nābisīwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin ganage bi-onji-inaabisiiwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Not even did she look up (at him) from where she was.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin ganage bi-onji-inaabisiiwan (not at all did she even look up from where she was).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Nänabucū dạc ā‘pidci inändạm wī‘pīndigät, mīdạc kā∙i∙jipīndigät; mī kīⁿwäⁿ awi‘kwä äji∙ā∙‘pidcinawạgi‘kwänit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenaboozhoo dash aapiji inendam wii-piindiged, mii dash gaa-izhi-piindiged; mii giiwenh aw ikwe ezhi-aapiji-nawagikwenid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, Nänabushu was very keen to enter, whereupon he then went in; then they say the woman bowed her head, holding it very low.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nenaboozhoo dash aapiji inendam wii-piindiged (now Nenaboozhoo was very set on going in), mii dash gaa-izhi-piindiged (and so he then went in); mii giiwenh aw ikwe (then, they say, that woman) ezhi-aapiji-nawagikwenid (bowed her head down very low).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Pānimā dạc wayābạnk kigīcäp pitạgwicinōn ogīn ‘aᵉa’wi‘kwä pi∙ạ∙cạmigut; pā‘täniwīyās pātōd aᵘ mindimōyä, mī∙i’∙ᵘ kā∙ạ∙camāt udānisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Baanimaa dash wayaabang gigizheb bi-dagwishinoon ogiin a’aw ikwe bi-ashamigod; baateni wiiyaas baadood aw mindimooye, mii iw gaa-ashamaad odaanisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, by and by on the morrow, during the morning, hither came the mother of the woman, bringing food to feed her (daughter); dried meat was what the old woman fetched, and with that she fed her daughter.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Baanimaa dash wayaabang gigizheb (then later, the next day in the morning) bi-dagwishinoon ogiin a’aw ikwe (the woman’s mother came hither) bi-ashamigod (to feed her); baateni wiiyaas baadood aw mindimooye (plenty of dried meat the old woman fetched), mii iw gaa-ashamaad odaanisan (and with that she fed her daughter).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Nänabucū dạc ogạnōnān īni’ᵘ mindimōyäyạn: “Kāwīnina nintāwīdigämāsī ‘aᵉa’ᵘ kitānis?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenaboozhoo dash oganoonaan iniw mindimooyeyan: “Gaawiin ina nindaa-wiidigemaasii a’aw gi-daanis?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, Nänabushu spoke to the old woman, saying: “May I not marry your daughter?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nenaboozhoo dash oganoonaan iniw mindimooyeyan (now Nenaboozhoo spoke to that old woman): “Gaawiin ina nindaa-wiidigemaasii a’aw gi-daanis?” (“may I not marry that daughter of yours?”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) “Nîyā,” i‘kitu ‘aᵘ mindimoyä; “mägwā kuca mạnidō∙u∙i!</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Niyaa,” ikido aw mindimoye; “megwaa gosha manidoo’o!**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Dear me!” said the old woman; “why, she is now in the condition of a manitou! (3)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Niyaa,” ikido aw mindimoye (“dear me!” said the old woman); “megwaa gosha manidoo’o! (“why, she is right now manidoo’o!)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) Kayä dạc kāwīn tibänindisusī, ōsạn udibänimigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaye dash gaawiin dibenindizosii, oosan odibenimigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And she is not at liberty yet to act for herself, under her father’s control is she still.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaye dash gaawiin dibenindizosii (and besides, she is not her own master), oosan odibenimigoon (by her father is she still ruled).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) Ningawīndamawā ‘aᵘ a‘kiwäⁿzī.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ninga-wiindamawaa aw akiwenzii.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*I will tell the old man about it.” (4)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ninga-wiindamawaa aw akiwenzii.” (“I will tell the old man about it.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) “Ānīc, mānōsa’. Mīnawā na‘kawä ningabạbāmādi’s.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniish, maanoo sa. Miinawaa na kawe ninga-babaamaadis.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Well, all right. For another while will I wander about.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniish, maanoo sa. (“well, all right then.) Miinawaa na kawe ninga-babaamaadis. (and for a while yet I will wander about.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) Kīnicwāsugunaga‘k ningatạgwicin, mī∙i’∙ᵘ tcibinandu ki‘kändạmān kädi‘kitogwän aᵘ a‘kiwäⁿzi.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gii-niizhwaasogonagak ninga-dagwishin, mii iw ji-bi-nando-gikendamaan ged-ikidogwen aw akiwenzi.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*At the end of seven days I will return, then will I come to learn what the old man shall say.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gii-niizhwaasogonagak ninga-dagwishin (when seven days have passed I will return), mii iw ji-bi-nando-gikendamaan (then I will come to find out) ged-ikidogwen aw akiwenzi.” (whatever the old man may say.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) Mīdạc kā∙i∙jimādcāt Nänabucū kīpabāmādisit nō‘piming; ạnōdci kägōn ubabāni‘tōn mādcit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-maajaad Nenaboozhoo gii-pabaamaadizid noopiming; anooji gegoon obabaa-nitoon maajid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon departed Nänabushu, travelling from place to place inland; all sorts of things he killed to eat during his wandering.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-maajaad Nenaboozhoo (and so Nenaboozhoo set out) gii-pabaamaadizid noopiming (wandering about inland); anooji gegoon obabaa-nitoon maajid (all sorts of things he killed along his way).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) A‘pī∙i∙dạc äninicwāsugunagạtinig mī∙i’∙ᵘ cigwa icāt, ạnōtc awäsīyänsaᵉ udạnimādcīnāᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii idash eni-niizhwaasogonagadinig mii iw zhigwa izhaad, anooj awesiiyensan odani-maajiinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when the seventh day was drawing on, then thither he went.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii idash eni-niizhwaasogonagadinig (and when the seventh day was coming on) mii iw zhigwa izhaad (then now he went), anooj awesiiyensan odani-maajiinaan (all kinds of little game animals he took along).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(19) A‘pī∙i∙dạc äni∙u∙di‘tạng iwä wīgiwāmäns owâbạndān, wäwäni pīnictcigādänig.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii idash eni-oditang iwe wiigiwaamens owaabandaan, weweni biinichjigaadenig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when he got up to the small wigwam (5), he saw that it was all set in neat order.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii idash eni-oditang iwe wiigiwaamens (and when he reached that little wigwam) owaabandaan (he saw it), weweni biinichjigaadenig (all neatly tidied up).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(20) A‘pī∙i∙dac tä‘pābạndạnk iwä wīgiwāmäns, ubiganawābạmigōn īni’ᵘ i‘kwäwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii idash depaabandang iwe wiigiwaamens, obi-ganawaabamigoon iniw ikwewan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when he peeped into the small wigwam, he was met with an expectant look from the woman.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii idash depaabandang iwe wiigiwaamens (and when he peeped into that little wigwam), obi-ganawaabamigoon iniw ikwewan (he was looked upon by that woman).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(21) “Ānīc, kitāpīndigä,” udigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniish, gidaa-piindige,” odigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Well, you may come in,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniish, gidaa-piindige,” (“well, you may come in,”) odigoon (she told him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(22) Mī∙i∙dạc ‘aᵉa’wi‘kwä agwạtcing kī∙i∙jā; mīdạc wâbạndạnk Nänabucō ubimiwạnān, wīyās ä‘tänig, mizisäᵉ kayä pinäwaᵉ kayä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash a’aw ikwe agwajing gii-izhaa; mii dash waabandang Nenaboozhoo obimiwanaan, wiiyaas etenig, mizise’ gaye binewa’ gaye.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon the woman went out of doors; and so, when she saw Nänabushu’s pack, meat was therein, besides turkeys and ruffed grouse.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash a’aw ikwe agwajing gii-izhaa (then that woman went outside); mii dash waabandang Nenaboozhoo obimiwanaan (and when she saw Nenaboozhoo’s pack), wiiyaas etenig (meat was in it), mizise’ gaye binewa’ gaye (turkeys too and ruffed grouse too).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(23) Mīdạc awi‘kwä kā∙i∙jitcībā‘kwät, mīdạc ä‘kitut: “Ningatawinạndumāk ninīngī∙i∙gōg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash awi kwe gaa-izhi-jiibaakwed, mii dash ekidod: “Ningad-awi-nandomaag ni-niigi’igoog.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so when the woman had cooked a meal, she then said: “I will go ask my parents to come.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash awi kwe gaa-izhi-jiibaakwed (and so when that woman had cooked), mii dash ekidod (then she said): “Ningad-awi-nandomaag ni-niigi’igoog.” (“I will go invite my parents.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(24) Mī kā∙i∙jimādcāt ‘awi‘kwä, ugī∙a∙winạndumān ōsạn ugīn kayä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii gaa-izhi-maajaad awi kwe, ogii-awi-nandomaan oosan ogiin gaye.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Accordingly then departed the woman; she went to invite her father and mother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii gaa-izhi-maajaad awi kwe (and so that woman set out), ogii-awi-nandomaan oosan ogiin gaye (she went to invite her father and her mother too).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(25) Nīngānidạc kī‘pitạgwicin awi‘kwä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ninga anidash gii-pi-dagwishin aw ikwe.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Before (their arrival), back home had come the woman. (6)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ninga anidash gii-pi-dagwishin aw ikwe (and beforehand that woman had come back home).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(26) A‘pī∙i∙dac tägucinuwāt īgiwä ki‘tci∙ạ∙nicinābäᵉg, owâbạmāwān Nänabucōn nạmạdạbinit agāmindäsīng.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii idash degoshinowaad igiw gichi-anishinaabeg, owaabamaawaan Nenaboozhoon namadabinid agaamindesing.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, when the old folks arrived, they saw Nänabushu seated in the space behind the fire. (7)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii idash degoshinowaad igiw gichi-anishinaabeg (and when those elders arrived), owaabamaawaan Nenaboozhoon namadabinid agaamindesing (they saw Nenaboozhoo sitting in the place behind the fire).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(27) Mīdạc ä‘kitut awä a‘kiwäⁿzi: “Ānīc, Nänabucū, ningīwīndạmāgō ‘iᵉiᵘ kā∙i∙‘kituwạnän änänimāwạtän ‘aᵉa’wi nindānisinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash ekidod awe akiwenzi: “Aaniish, Nenaboozhoo, ningii-wiindamaagoo i’iw gaa-ikidowanen e-nenimaawaaden a’awi nin-daanisinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon said the old man: “Well, Nänabushu, I have been told what you said concerning the way you feel about this daughter of ours.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash ekidod awe akiwenzi (then that old man said): “Aaniish, Nenaboozhoo, (“well, Nenaboozhoo,) ningii-wiindamaagoo i’iw gaa-ikidowanen (I have been told what you said) e-nenimaawaaden a’awi nin-daanisinaan. (about how you feel toward this daughter of ours.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(28) Kāwīn ā‘pidci kägōn uni‘tāwitōsīn, nōndäsi.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin aapiji gegoon onitaawidoosiin noondezi,”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*She is not very smart at doing things, she is dull.” (8)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin aapiji gegoon onitaawidoosiin (she is not very skilled at doing things) noondezi,” (she falls short,”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(29) Wäwäni ugīwīndạmawān īni’ᵘ Nänabucōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Weweni ogii-wiindamawaan iniw Nenaboozhoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He was careful to tell Nänabushu about her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Weweni ogii-wiindamawaan iniw Nenaboozhoon (carefully he told Nenaboozhoo about her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(30) “Kīcpîn dạc ānawänimāsiwạt, mānōsa’ intawā kitāwīdcīwā.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Giishpin dash aanawenimaasiwad, maanoo sa indawaa gidaa-wiijiiwaa.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“So if you are not disinclined to taking her, why, you may then marry her.” (9)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Giishpin dash aanawenimaasiwad (“and so if you do not think her unworthy), maanoo sa indawaa gidaa-wiijiiwaa.” (well then, you may take her to live with you.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(31) Mīdạc kā∙i∙cipạsigwīt ‘aᵉa’ᵘ a‘kiwäⁿzi ōgīsạgini‘känān udānisạn, Nänabucōndạc namadạpinit ogī∙u∙nạbi∙ā∙n.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-pazigwiid a’aw akiwenzii ogii-saginikenaan odaanisan, Nenaboozhoon dash namadabinid ogii-onabi’aan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon rising to his feet, the old man took his daughter by the hand, and where Nänabushu was seated he had her sit beside him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-pazigwiid a’aw akiwenzii (and so, rising to his feet, that old man) ogii-saginikenaan odaanisan (took his daughter by the arm), Nenaboozhoon dash namadabinid ogii-onabi’aan (and where Nenaboozhoo sat he seated her beside him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(32) Mī∙i∙dac kīkagī‘kạmāwāt īni’ᵘ udānisiwān wäwäni tciwī‘pimādisinit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gii-kagiikamaawaad iniw odaanisiwaan weweni ji-wii-bimaadizinid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then he charged his daughter that she live an upright life.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gii-kagiikamaawaad iniw odaanisiwaan (and then they counselled their daughter) weweni ji-wii-bimaadizinid (that she should live well and uprightly).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(33) Mīdạc kā∙i∙jiwīsiniwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-wiisiniwaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon they ate.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-wiisiniwaad (and so then they ate).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(34) Kā∙i∙ckwāwīsiniwāt kī‘kanōnā Nänabucō: “Ạmbä, pījāyu‘k ändāyāng, kayä kīnawā tcipi∙a∙yāyäg imā ōdä‘tōwād anicinābäg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaa-ishkwaa-wiisiniwaad gii-kanoonaa Nenaboozhoo: “Ambe, bi-izhaayog endaayaang, gaye giinawaa ji-pi-ayaayeg imaa oodetoowaad anishinaabeg.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*After they had eaten, then Nänabushu was told: “Now, do you come to where we live, so that you also may dwell yonder where the people have a town.” (10)*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaa-ishkwaa-wiisiniwaad (after they had finished eating) gii-kanoonaa Nenaboozhoo (Nenaboozhoo was spoken to): “Ambe, bi-izhaayog endaayaang, (“come, come to where we live,) gaye giinawaa ji-pi-ayaayeg imaa (that you too may stay there) oodetoowaad anishinaabeg.” (where the people have a town.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(35) Mīdạc imā kīna∙ā∙ngạbit; mōjạgidạc kīnạndawändcigä, anōdc kägō uni‘tōn awäsīyaᵉ.</span>

(54) Nanabushu Marries

(1) Reference is to the menstrual lodge.

(2) Man is forbidden to enter a menstrual lodge; usually an old woman is about, who looks after the wants of the woman.

(3) A woman menstruating is to be avoided for the evil power she then is said to have.

(4) These excuses by the mother are only a formality. She really has more to say than the father, and could have given the answer then. It is a point not to appear too anxious, however willing one may be.

(5) This is given as the same little wigwam, but as a matter of fact it would be another into which the woman would go after her illness.

(6) All this is according to custom.

(7) At the back of the lodge, the proper place for a male visitor to sit where there is no male owner of the lodge.

(8) These words are purely formal, and have no meaning. a parent uses them, no matter how capable his daughter is, but he does not permit any one else to say the same thing of her.

(9) Another formal statement which serves to put the responsibility on the man.

(10) As a rule, a man and his wife do not go away at once to live by themselves; they live a while either with his parents or else with hers.

(11) The ceremony is always after the wedding.
