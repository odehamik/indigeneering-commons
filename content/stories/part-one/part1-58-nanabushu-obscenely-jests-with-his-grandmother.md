<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīdạc kīwäⁿ ninguting Nänabucō pạbimiba‘tōd, kāwīn wī‘kā kibisi‘kāsī; ningutingdạc kīwäⁿ ạnipạbimiba‘tōd Nänabucu ogạnōnigōn ō‘kumisạn: “Nōjic, nīwīsāga∙ạ∙m,” udigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash giiwenh ningoding Nenabozho babimibatood, gaawiin wiikaa gibizhi’gaazii; ningodingidash giiwenh ani-babimibatood Nenabozho oganoonigoon o’gomisan: “Noozhish, niwii-zaaga’am,” odigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And now they say that once while Nänabushu was travelling about on the run, never did he come to a halt; and once they say that when he was running along, Nänabushu was addressed by his grandmother saying: “My grandson, I wish to go out,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">mii dash giiwenh — and now they say ∙ ningoding — once ∙ Nenabozho babimibatood — Nanabushu running about ∙ gaawiin wiikaa gibizhi’gaazii — never did he stop ∙ ningodingidash giiwenh — and once they say ∙ ani-babimibatood — as he was running along ∙ oganoonigoon o’gomisan — he was addressed by his grandmother ∙ Noozhish — my grandson ∙ niwii-zaaga’am — I wish to go out (to relieve myself) ∙ odigoon — he was told by her.</span>

Also Nänabucō with the macron. ⚠️ 58-F1 (for M.): the Fiero reads o’gomisan here and at §2, §4, §6, §8 — five times — where 131 tokens across the rest of Part I read ookomisan. <span class="l-orig">(2) Nänabuc kayä wīn i‘kito: “Pä‘kädiyänin,” udinān ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenabozh gaye wiin ikido: “*bekadiyanin*,” odinaan o’gomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And Nänabushu in reply said: “Simply lean aside with your buttocks,” he said to his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nenabozh gaye wiin ikido — and Nanabushu for his part says ∙ *bekadiyanin* — simply lean aside with your buttocks (*flagged*: form not in lexicon, sense per Jones’s English) ∙ odinaan o’gomisan — he says it to his grandmother.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) “Kāwīn, nōjis,” udinān, “niwīmīsī,” udinān ōjisạn.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) “Mīgu imā mīsīn,” udinān o‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii go imaa miizin,” odinaan o’gomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Ānīc, mī wīn kägä‘t mindimōyä äjimīzīgubạnän imā. Mīdạc minawā aᵘ mindimōyä änāt ujicäⁿyạn: “Nōjis!” udinān, “tcīgā‘kwā ạni∙a∙yạba‘tōn. Nīwī∙ạ∙niudā‘pinān wâkisīndimä∙o∙yān,” udigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Anish, mii wiin geget mindimooye ezhi-miizigubanen imaa. Mii dash minawaa a’aw mindimooye enaad ojisheyan: “Noozhis!” odinan, “jiigaakwaa ani-aya-batoon. Niwii-ani-odaapinaan waa-giisindimaa’oyaan,” odigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Well, it was so that the old woman relieved herself at the time in that position. And so again the old woman said to her grandson: “O my grandson!” she said to him, “by the edge of the woods do pass along as you run. I wish to get hold of something on the way to wipe myself at the anus,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Anish, mii wiin geget — well, so it was indeed ∙ mindimooye ezhi-miizigubanen imaa — the old woman relieved herself there as related ∙ mii dash minawaa a’aw mindimooye enaad ojisheyan — and so again the old woman said to her grandson ∙ Noozhis — my grandson ∙ odinan — she says to him ∙ jiigaakwaa ani-aya-batoon — by the edge of the woods run along ∙ niwii-ani-odaapinaan — I wish to get hold of something along the way ∙ waa-giisindimaa’oyaan — with which to wipe myself at the anus ∙ odigoon — he was told by her.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget a’aw mindimooye ezhi-giisindimaa’od odooskon. Mii dash minawaa a’aw mindimooye enaad ojisan: “Zaaga’akwaang ani-izhaan; niwii-ani-giiziiya’kwizitoon nindooskon,” odinan ojisheyan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And it was true that the old woman wiped herself at the anus with her elbow. Thereupon again the old woman said to her grandson: “Into the thick woods do you go; for, as I go I wish to clean my elbow with the limbs,” she said to her grandson.*</span>
