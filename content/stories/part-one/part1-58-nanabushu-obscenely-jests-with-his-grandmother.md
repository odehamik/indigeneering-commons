58\. NÄNABUSHU OBSCENELY JESTS WITH HIS GRANDMOTHER.

<span class="l-fiero">**58. Nenabozho Obscenely Jests with His Grandmother.**</span>

58\. Nanabushu Obscenely Jests with His Grandmother.

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Mrs. Syrette — Series 8 (Nos. 57–63), Fort William (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīdạc kīwäⁿ ninguting Nänabucō pạbimiba‘tōd, kāwīn wī‘kā kibisi‘kāsī; ningutingdạc kīwäⁿ ạnipạbimiba‘tōd Nänabucu ogạnōnigōn ō‘kumisạn: “Nōjic, nīwīsāga∙ạ∙m,” udigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash giiwenh ningoding Nenabozho babimibatood, gaawiin wiikaa gibisikaasii*; ningoding dash giiwenh ani-babimibatood Nenabozho oganoonigoon ookomisan: “Noozhish, niwii-zaaga’am,” odigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And now they say that once while Nänabushu was travelling about on the run, never did he come to a halt; and once they say that when he was running along, Nänabushu was addressed by his grandmother saying: “My grandson, I wish to go out,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">mii dash giiwenh — and now they say ∙ ningoding — once ∙ Nenabozho babimibatood — Nanabushu running about ∙ gaawiin wiikaa gibisikaasii* — never did he stop ∙ ningoding dash giiwenh — and once they say ∙ ani-babimibatood — as he was running along ∙ oganoonigoon ookomisan — he was addressed by his grandmother ∙ Noozhish — my grandson ∙ niwii-zaaga’am (I wish to go out; to relieve myself) ∙ odigoon — he was told by her.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Nänabuc kayä wīn i‘kito: “Pä‘kädiyänin,” udinān ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenabozh gaye wiin ikido: “bekediyenin*,” odinaan ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And Nänabushu in reply said: “Simply lean aside with your buttocks,” he said to his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nenabozh gaye wiin ikido — and Nanabushu for his part says ∙ bekediyenin* (simply lean aside with your buttocks; *flagged*: form not in lexicon, sense per Jones’s English) ∙ odinaan ookomisan — he says it to his grandmother.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) “Kāwīn, nōjis,” udinān, “niwīmīsī,” udinān ōjisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaawiin, noozhis,” odinaan, “niwii-miizii,” odinaan oozhisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Nay, my grandson,” she said to him, “I have need of relief,” she said to her grandson.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin — no ∙ noozhis — my grandson ∙ odinaan — she says to him ∙ niwii-miizii (I have need of relief; I wish to defecate) ∙ odinaan oozhisan — she says it to her grandson.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) “Mīgu imā mīsīn,” udinān o‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii go imaa miizin,” odinaan ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Then do it there,” he said to his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">mii go imaa — then right there ∙ miizin (defecate; imperative) ∙ odinaan ookomisan — he says it to his grandmother.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Ānīc, mī wīn kägä‘t mindimōyä äjimīzīgubạnän imā. Mīdạc minawā aᵘ mindimōyä änāt ujicäⁿyạn: “Nōjis!” udinān, “tcīgā‘kwā ạni∙a∙yạba‘tōn. Nīwī∙ạ∙niudā‘pinān wâkisīndimä∙o∙yān,” udigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish, mii wiin geget mindimooye ezhi-miiziigobanen imaa. Mii dash minawaa a’aw mindimooye enaad ozhishenyan*: “Noozhis!” odinaan, “jiigaakwaa ani-aya-batoon. Niwii-ani-odaapinaan waa-gisiindime’oyaan*,” odigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Well, it was so that the old woman relieved herself at the time in that position. And so again the old woman said to her grandson: “O my grandson!” she said to him, “by the edge of the woods do pass along as you run. I wish to get hold of something on the way to wipe myself at the anus,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish, mii wiin geget — well, so it was indeed ∙ mindimooye ezhi-miiziigobanen imaa — the old woman relieved herself there as related ∙ mii dash minawaa a’aw mindimooye enaad ozhishenyan* — and so again the old woman said to her grandson ∙ Noozhis — my grandson ∙ odinaan — she says to him ∙ jiigaakwaa ani-aya-batoon — by the edge of the woods run along ∙ niwii-ani-odaapinaan — I wish to get hold of something along the way ∙ waa-gisiindime’oyaan* — with which to wipe myself at the anus ∙ odigoon — he was told by her.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) “Kitōskun kisīndimä∙u∙n,” udinān ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gidooskon gisiindime’on*,” odinaan ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“With your elbow wipe your anus,” he said to his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">gidooskon — your elbow ∙ gisiindime’on* (wipe yourself at the anus; imperative) ∙ odinaan ookomisan — he says it to his grandmother.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Mīdạc kägä‘t aᵘ mindimōyä äjikisindimä∙u∙t odōskun. Mīdạc minawā aᵘ mindimōyä änāt ōcisạn: “Sạgā‘kwāng ạni∙i∙cān; niwī∙ạ∙nikisīyā‘kwisitōn nintōskun,” udinān ōcîcäⁿyạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget a’aw mindimooye ezhi-gisiindime’od* odooskon. Mii dash minawaa a’aw mindimooye enaad oozhisan: “Zaagaakwaang* ani-izhaan; niwii-ani-giziiyaakwizitoon* nindooskon,” odinaan ozhishenyan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And it was true that the old woman wiped herself at the anus with her elbow. Thereupon again the old woman said to her grandson: “Into the thick woods do you go; for, as I go I wish to clean my elbow with the limbs,” she said to her grandson.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">mii dash geget a’aw mindimooye ezhi-gisiindime’od* odooskon — and it was true the old woman wiped herself at the anus with her elbow ∙ mii dash minawaa a’aw mindimooye enaad oozhisan — thereupon again the old woman said to her grandson ∙ zaagaakwaang* ani-izhaan — into the thick woods go along ∙ niwii-ani-giziiyaakwizitoon* nindooskon (I wish to clean my elbow as I go; with the branches) ∙ odinaan ozhishenyan — she says it to her grandson.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Mīdạc minawā Nänabucu änāt ō‘kumisạn: “Sōbạndạn,” udinän.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash minawaa Nenabozho enaad ookomisan: “soobandan*,” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Whereupon again Nänabushu said to his grandmother: “Lick it with your tongue,” he said to her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">mii dash minawaa Nenabozho enaad ookomisan — whereupon again Nanabushu said to his grandmother ∙ soobandan* (lick it, with your tongue; *flagged*: Jones Sōbạndạn; sense per English) ∙ odinaan — he says it to her.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Ānīc, mī wīn minawā äjisōbạndạnk ōtōskun, mīdạc aᵘ mindimōyä änāt ōcicäⁿyạn: “Nōjis! nawạtc pạngī i‘ku‘kwänîn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish, mii wiin minawaa ezhi-soobandang* odooskon, mii dash a’aw mindimooye enaad ozhishenyan: “Noozhis! nawaj bangii ikokwenin*.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Well, it was the same again, for she licked her elbow with her tongue, whereupon the old woman said to her grandson: “O my grandson! just raise your head a little.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish, mii wiin minawaa ezhi-soobandang* odooskon — well, it was the same again, she licked her elbow ∙ mii dash a’aw mindimooye enaad ozhishenyan — whereupon the old woman said to her grandson ∙ Noozhis — my grandson ∙ nawaj bangii ikokwenin* (just raise your head a little; *flagged*: ikokwenin — form not in lexicon, sense per Jones’s English).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) “Ānic?” i‘kito Nänabucu.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniish?” ikido Nenabozho.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Why?” said Nänabushu.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">aaniish — why ∙ ikido Nenabozho — says Nanabushu.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) “Nīwīskwātcigä,” i‘kito mindimōyä. Nänabucu i‘kito: “Kuntạn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Niwii-zikwaachige*,” ikido mindimooye. Nenabozho ikido: “Goondan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“I want to spit,” said the old woman. Nänabushu said: “Swallow it.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">niwii-zikwaachige* — I want to spit ∙ ikido mindimooye — says the old woman ∙ Nenabozho ikido — Nanabushu says ∙ goondan (swallow it; imperative).</span>
