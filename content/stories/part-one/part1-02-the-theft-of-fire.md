2\. THE THEFT OF FIRE.

<span class="l-fiero">**Gii-gimoodid ishkode Nenabosho**</span>

The Theft of Fire

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Wâsagunäckạnk — Series 1 (Nos. 1–18), Bois Fort (per Michelson’s roster, Part I Preface p. XX; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1917).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ānīc mīsa iᵘ cīgwa kīni‘tawigi∙a∙t ‘aᵉa’ᵘ mindimōyä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nenabosho Steals Fire  
Aaniish mii sa iw zhigwa gii-nitaawigi’aad a’aw mindimooyenh.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So accordingly then did the old woman bring him up.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish mii sa iw zhigwa (well, and so by this time) gii-nitaawigi’aad (she had raised him) a’aw mindimooyenh (that old woman).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Mīsa iᵘ cayīgwa ugī∙i∙nān ini’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn: “Kāwīnîna ningutci anicinābäg kiki‘känimāsīg tci∙ạ∙yāwāt?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa iw zhayiigwai ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan: “Gaawiin ina ningoji anishinaabeg gi-kikenimaasiig ji-ayaawaad?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so by and by he said to his grandmother: “Don’t you know of a place where there are some people.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa iw zhayiigwa (and so by and by) ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan (he said to his grandmother): “Gaawiin ina ningoji anishinaabeg (is there nowhere any people) gi-kikenimaasiig (that you know of) ji-ayaawaad? (who are about?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) “Äyeᵉ,” ugī∙i∙gōn īni ō‘kumisạn. “O∙o∙widi’ agāmiki‘tcigạmi ayāwag īgi’ᵘ anīcinābäg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Eye,” ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan. “O’owidi agaami-kichigami ayaawag igiw anishinaabeg.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Yes,” he was told by his grandmother. “In yonder direction on the farther shore of the sea are some people.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Eye,” (“yes,”) ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan (he was told by his grandmother). “O’owidi agaami-kichigami (over yonder, on the far shore of the great sea) ayaawag igiw anishinaabeg (there are people).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) “Kunagä‘kā udayāsīnawātug ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ickudä?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gonage gaa odayaasiinaawaadog i’iw ishkode?”ii**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“I am curious to know if they do not possess fire.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gonage (I wonder whether) gaa odayaasiinaawaadog i’iw ishkode? (they might not have any fire?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) “Äyeᵉ,” ugī∙i∙gōn īniᵘ ō‘kumisạn; “Kägä‘t utāiyānāwa ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ickudä.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Eye,” ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan; “Geget odayaanaawaa i’iw ishkode.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Yes,” he was told by his grandmother; “truly, they do possess some fire.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Eye,” (“yes,”) ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan (he was told by his grandmother); “Geget odayaanaawaa i’iw ishkode (truly they do have fire).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) O∙o∙widac ugī∙i∙nān īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn: “Ạmbäsanō, ninganāsi‘kān ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ickudä,” ugi∙i∙nān īni’ᵘ</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**O’owidi dash ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan: “Ambe sa noo, ninga-naazikaaniii i’iw ishkode,” ogii-inaan ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, this was what he said to his grandmother: “Please let me go fetch the fire,” he said to his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">O’owidi dash ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan (and this he said to his grandmother): “Ambe sa noo (please now), ninga-naazikaan i’iw ishkode (let me go to that fire),” ogii-inaan ookomisan (he said to his grandmother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) ō‘kumisạn. O∙ō∙widạc ugī∙i∙gōn īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn: “Kāwīn kidākạckitōsīn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**O’owidi dash ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan: “Gaawiin gidaa-kashkitoosiin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And this was what he was told by his grandmother: “Not will you be able to succeed.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">O’owidi dash ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan (and this he was told by his grandmother): “Gaawiin gidaa-kashkitoosiin (you will not be able to do it).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Kägä‘t ā‘pidci ogạnawändānāwā i∙i∙wisa ändāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Geget aapiji oganawendaanaawaa i’iw isa endaawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Truly, a very careful watch do they keep over it there where they dwell.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Geget aapiji oganawendaanaawaa (truly very closely they guard it) i’iw isa endaawaad (there where they live).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) A‘kiwäⁿzī i∙i∙mā ayā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Akiwenzii i’imaa ayaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*An old man at the place abides.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Akiwenzii i’imaa ayaa (an old man stays there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Mīgu a‘pạnä tạsing kījigạtinig asạbin uji∙a∙t.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii go apane dasingiv giizhigadinig asabiin ozhi’aad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And all the while, as often as the day comes round, upon a net he works.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii go apane dasing giizhigadinig (and all the time, every day that comes) asabiin ozhi’aad (he works at a net).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Kāwīn wī‘ka ningutci∙i∙jāsī, mīgu a‘pạnä imā pīndik ayāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin wiikaa ningoji izhaasii, mii go apane imaa biindig ayaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Never anywhere does he go, but always there indoors he remains.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin wiikaa ningoji izhaasii (never does he go anywhere), mii go apane imaa biindig ayaad (but always he stays there inside).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) Nīⁿjiwaᵉidạc ‘iᵉi’ᵘ ōtānisaᵉ, mīyä‘ta iᵘ mōjạg ạgwạtcing äyānit.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Niizhiwag idash i’iw odaanisa’, mii eta moozhag agwajing eyaanid.”v**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, two are those daughters of his, and only they are continually out of doors.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Niizhiwag idash i’iw odaanisa’ (and two are his daughters), mii eta moozhag agwajing eyaanid (and only they are always out of doors).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) O∙o∙widạc ugī∙i∙nān īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisan: “Mā’nū ninga∙i∙jā,” ogī∙i∙nān īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**O’ow idash ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan: “Maanoo ninga-izhaa,” ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And this he said to his grandmother: “Nevertheless I will go,” he said to his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">O’ow idash ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan (and this he said to his grandmother): “Maanoo ninga-izhaa (never mind, I will go),” ogii-inaan iniw ookomisan (he said to his grandmother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) “Awawa,” ugī∙i∙gōn īni’ᵘ ō‘kumisạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Awawa,” ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Very well,” he was told by his grandmother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Awawa,” (“Awawa,”) ogii-igoon iniw ookomisan (he was told by his grandmother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) Ānīc mīsa a‘pī cigwa kā∙i∙‘kidut: “Ạmbäsạnō tạgackatin ‘oᵉō ki‘tcigạmi, wīgwāsabakwāng tawīapī‘tadin o∙o∙ ki‘tcigạmi.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish mii sa apii zhigwa gaa-ikidod: “Ambe sa noo da-gashkadin o’ow gichigami, wiigwaasabakwaang da-wii-apiitadin o’ow gichigami.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, this was what he then said afterwards: “I will that the sea shall freeze, as thick as the birch-bark covering of the lodge so let this sea freeze.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish mii sa apii zhigwa gaa-ikidod (well, and so then this is what he said): “Ambe sa noo da-gashkadin o’ow gichigami (let this sea now freeze over), wiigwaasabakwaang da-wii-apiitadin o’ow gichigami (as thick as a birch-bark lodge-covering let this sea freeze).”</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“O’ow idash ninga-izhinaagos (and this is how I will look),” kii-ikido (he said); “nindawaa ninga-waaboosoonsiw (I will turn into a little hare instead).”</span>
