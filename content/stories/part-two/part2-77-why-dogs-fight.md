WHY DOGS FIGHT, AND WHY PEOPLE ARE ENVIOUS.

Told by Maajiigaaboo (Mādcīgābō) of Leech Lake.

<span class="l-src">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1919).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Päcig aᵘ inini māminunäntạm: “Taga, ningasạgaswä∙ā∙g anicinābäg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Bezhig aw inini maaminonendam: “Daga, ninga-zagaswe’aag anishinaabeg.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*A certain man was revolving matters over in his mind: “Now, I am going to invite the people for a smoke.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Bezhig aw inini (a certain man) maaminonendam (was turning things over in his mind): “Daga (now then), ninga-zagaswe’aag (I will invite to a smoke) anishinaabeg (the people).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Mīsa kā∙i∙cinạntumāt īni’ᵘ ininiwạn, o∙ō∙widac udinān: “Wäntcisagaswä∙i∙nagu‘k kāwīn kīnawint kitōgimāwisīmin. Ka‘kina kītcipimādisīnān ugimāwi. Agatạga, unā‘kunititā ạgi∙u∙gimāwit. O∙ō∙widac kigaca‘kamōnānān ạgi∙u∙gimāwit, o∙o∙ ä‘täg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa gaa-izhi-nandomaad iniw ininiwan, o’ow idash odinaan: “Wenji-zagaswe’inagog gaawiin giinawind gidoogimaawisiimin. Gakina giiji-bimaadiziinaan ogimaawi. Aga daga, onaakonidiidaa ge-ogimaawid. O’ow idash giga-zhakamoonaanaan ge-ogimaawid, o’o eteg.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Whereupon he sent for the men, and this he said to them: “The cause of my inviting you for a smoke is that you and I have no chief. All that live (on earth) with you and me have a chief. Therefore let us decree among ourselves who shall be chief. And this we will give him to swallow who is to be the chief, this thing that is here.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa gaa-izhi-nandomaad (and so he summoned) iniw ininiwan (the men), o’ow idash odinaan (and this he said to them): “Wenji-zagaswe’inagog (the reason I invite you to smoke is) gaawiin giinawind gidoogimaawisiimin (you and I have no chief). Gakina giiji-bimaadiziinaan (all our fellow living beings) ogimaawi (have a chief). Aga daga (come then), onaakonidiidaa (let us decree among ourselves) ge-ogimaawid (who shall be chief). O’ow idash (and this thing) giga-zhakamoonaanaan (we shall put into the mouth of) ge-ogimaawid (the one who is to be chief), o’o eteg (this that lies here).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) “Ānīc, waᵍa’wisa ta∙u∙gimāwi.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniish, wa’aw isa da-ogimaawi.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Why, let that one yonder be chief\!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniish (why), wa’aw isa (that one there) da-ogimaawi (let him be chief)\!”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Päcig mīnawa inini kāgīgitut: “Kikīpādisi, kāwīn tā∙u∙gimāwisī aᵍa’ᵘ. Ānīc, waᵍawisa ta∙u∙gimāwi, mī waᵍa’ᵘ näbwā‘kāt.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Bezhig miinawaa inini gaagiigidod: “Gigiibaadizi, gaawiin daa-ogimaawisii a’aw. Aaniish, wa’aw isa da-ogimaawi, mii wa’aw nebwaakaad.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Another man spoke, saying: “He is a fool, a chief that one could not be. Why, let that one yonder be chief, he that is wise\!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Bezhig miinawaa inini (another man) gaagiigidod (spoke, saying): “Gigiibaadizi (he is foolish), gaawiin daa-ogimaawisii (he could not be chief) a’aw (that one). Aaniish, wa’aw isa da-ogimaawi (why, let that one be chief), mii wa’aw nebwaakaad (the one who is wise).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Pānimā∙u∙gu päjig inini wändcipạsingutcisät äjinawandạndạn i’ᵘiᵘ waca‘kạmūnitiwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Baanimaa go bezhig inini wenji-bazigonjised ezhi-nawadandang i’iw wezhakamoonidiwaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then of a sudden one of the men, leaping up from his place, seized in his mouth that which they had to be swallowed.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Baanimaa go (then suddenly) bezhig inini (one man) wenji-bazigonjised (leaping up from his place) ezhi-nawadandang (seized in his mouth) i’iw wezhakamoonidiwaad (the thing they were to give one another to swallow).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Äjinawantamint kīwäⁿ, aᵍa’ᵘ kānawandandạnk.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ezhi-nawadamind giiwenh, a’aw gaa-nawadandang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then it is said some one fastened teeth on him, on him that had swallowed it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ezhi-nawadamind (then teeth were fastened on him) giiwenh (so they say), a’aw gaa-nawadandang (the one who had seized it in his mouth).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Mīsa kīwä kā∙i∙cikidiskanusät ‘i’ᵘi’ᵘ näwundandangibạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa giiwenh gaa-izhi-gidiskanozed i’iw newandandangiban.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon he quickly dropped from his mouth that which he was going to swallow;*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa giiwenh (and so, they say) gaa-izhi-gidiskanozed (he let drop from his mouth) i’iw newandandangiban (that which he had been about to swallow; *preterite*).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Ningutci kīwäⁿ kībimi∙a∙‘täni ‘i’ᵘi’ᵘ näwandandangibạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoji giiwenh gii-bimi-ateni i’iw newandandangiban.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*aside then, they say, lay that which he was going to swallow.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoji giiwenh (off to one side, they say) gii-bimi-ateni (there it lay) i’iw newandandangiban (the thing he would have swallowed; *preterite*).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Mīsa’ kīwäⁿ kā∙i∙jimīgātiwāt äjiga‘kinaku imā äyāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa giiwenh gaa-izhi-miigaadiwaad ezhi-gakina go imaa eyaawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Accordingly, so the story goes, one with another began fighting, all they that were there at the time.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa giiwenh (and so, the story goes) gaa-izhi-miigaadiwaad (they fought one another) ezhi-gakina go (all of them) imaa eyaawaad (who were there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Mīsa kā∙i∙jipōni∙i∙tiwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa gaa-izhi-booni’idiwaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then they left one another alone.*</span>

Mii sa gaa-izhi-booni’idiwaad (and then they let one another be).

<span class="l-orig">(11) “Mīsa kinawint i’ᵘi’ᵘ tcikakībātisiyạng, mīsa’ kīnawint ạnimuc kīgatigōmin. Mīsa’ kīnawint tcimāmawikībātisiyạnk. Mīdạc igu nä‘kwä‘kutātiyạngin tcimīkātiyạng. Kāwīn wīn awiya tatōtātisī. Mīdạc i’ᵘ∙i∙’ᵘ kīnawint kīkakībātisiwininān ändōtāguyạng.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii sa giinawind i’iw ji-gagiibaadiziyang, mii sa giinawind animosh gigad-igoomin. Mii sa giinawind ji-maamawi-giibaadiziyang. Mii dash igo nekwekodaadiyangin ji-miigaadiyang. Gaawiin wiin awiya da-doodaadisii. Mii dash i’iw giinawind gigagiibaadiziwininaan endoodaagoyang.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Therefore shall you and I be fools, whereupon you and I shall be called dogs. And now you and I shall be fools above all fools. So accordingly, whenever we meet together, then shall we begin fighting one with another. No other beings shall treat one another so. And this is what we have brought upon ourselves because of our foolishness.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Mii sa giinawind (therefore you and I) i’iw ji-gagiibaadiziyang (shall be fools), mii sa giinawind (and you and I) animosh gigad-igoomin (shall be called dogs). Mii sa giinawind ji-maamawi-giibaadiziyang (and you and I shall be fools above all). Mii dash igo nekwekodaadiyangin (so whenever we meet one another) ji-miigaadiyang (we shall fight). Gaawiin wiin awiya da-doodaadisii (no other beings shall do so to one another). Mii dash i’iw (and this is) giinawind gigagiibaadiziwininaan (our own foolishness) endoodaagoyang (that is done to us; *what we have brought on ourselves*).”</span>
