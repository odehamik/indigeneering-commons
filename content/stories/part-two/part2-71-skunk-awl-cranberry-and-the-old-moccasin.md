<span class="l-fiero">**ZHIGAAG, MIGOOS, MASHKIIGIMIN, GAYE MAKIZINISH.**</span>

SKUNK, AWL, AND CRANBERRY, AND THE OLD MOCCASIN.

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by either Wāsāgunäckạnk, or Midāsugaⁿj, or both (the roster’s own wording) — Series 4 (Nos. 62–78), Bois Fort (per Jones Part II Introduction p. ix; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1919).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mīdạc iᵉi’ᵘ a∙ī∙ntāwạg cigāg, kayä mackīgimin, mīnawā migōs, kayä wīn ma‘kicinic; mīsa‘ iᵘ äjinīwiwāt wīpibōniciwāt. Cigwadạc wī‘pipōnini, mīsa´ iᵘ cigwa nạntawäntcigät aᵉa´ᵘ cigāg.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash i’iw ayi-andaawag zhigaag, gaye mashkiigimin, miinawaa migoos, gaye wiin makizinish; mii sa iw ezhi-niiwiwaad wii-biboonishiwaad. Zhigwa dash wii-biboonini, mii sa iw zhigwa nandawenjiged a’aw zhigaag.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And now there lived a Skunk, and a Cranberry, and an Awl, and an old miserable Moccasin; and thus four was the number of them that planned to go into camp for the winter. And in a while the winter came, whereupon then began Skunk to hunt for game.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash i’iw ayi-andaawag (and now there dwelt) zhigaag (Skunk), gaye mashkiigimin (and Cranberry), miinawaa migoos (and Awl), gaye wiin makizinish (and the old Moccasin; *-ish pejorative*); mii sa iw ezhi-niiwiwaad (and so four were they) wii-biboonishiwaad (who would winter together). Zhigwa dash wii-biboonini (and as winter was coming on), mii sa iw zhigwa nandawenjiged a’aw zhigaag (then Skunk began to hunt for game).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Kägä‘t ninguting mạmītāwäntạm mackīgimin ā‘kusit; ā‘pidci wīpāckitcīckā. Ninguting äcipāckitcīckāt, mīsā‘pan.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Geget ningoding mamiitawendam mashkiigimin aakozid; aapiji wii-baashkijiishkaa. Ningoding ezhi-baashkijiishkaad, mii sa apane.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*In truth, disturbed in mind was Cranberry once, because of being sick; on the very point of bursting open at the belly was she. By and by, when she burst open at the belly, then she was gone.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Geget ningoding mamiitawendam mashkiigimin aakozid (truly once Cranberry was troubled in mind, being sick); aapiji wii-baashkijiishkaa (she was about to burst open). Ningoding ezhi-baashkijiishkaad (once when she burst open), mii sa apane (then she was gone for good).</span>

*¹ All four are represented as old women. — Jones’s note, foot of p. 703. mamiitawendam “troubled in mind”; baashkijiishkaa- “burst open” — drafted; the comic bursting of Cranberry. apane “gone for good / forever.”*

<span class="l-orig">(3) Ningutingidạc kistci∙ā∙bawāni; aᵘ cigāg äjimādcāt kīyusät. Ninguting otō‘kawi∙ā∙n īni´ᵘ piciwạn, mīsa´ äji∙u∙cimut äntāt. Ningutingidạc cigwa tạgwicinōn īni’ᵘ pici’wạn mīsa‘ wīwīwimigut; misa´ käga‘pi kīwīwimigut. Mīdạc īni´ᵘ äntawäntcigänit, kägä‘t kāwīn kägō uni‘tōsin; ā‘pidci kō‘pạtisi. Cigwa pa‘kadäwạg. Ānīc mī∙a∙´ᵘ cigāg ạnicā odayān iᵉi´ᵘ gämīdciwāt. Käga‘pi kāwīn wī‘kā ningutạno wī∙i∙nạno‘kīsī aᵉa’ᵘ pīci’ᵘ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding idash gichi-aabawani; a’aw zhigaag ezhi-maajaad giiyosed. Ningoding odookawi’aan iniw bizhiwan, mii sa ezhi-oojimod endaad. Ningoding idash zhigwa dagwishinon iniw bizhiwan mii sa wii-wiiwimigod; mii sa gegapii gii-wiiwimigod. Mii dash iniw endawenjigenid, geget gaawiin gegoo onitoosiin; aapiji goopadizi. Zhigwa bakadewag. Aaniish mii a’aw zhigaag anishaa odayaan i’iw ge-miijiwaad. Gegapii gaawiin wiikaa ningodino wii-inanokiisii a’aw bizhiw.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, once there was a great thaw; Skunk then set out to hunt for game. Once she came upon the trail of a Lynx, whereupon she fled to where she lived. And once when Lynx came, accordingly then was she desired for a wife by him; and so at last she was taken to wife by him. And now it was he that hunted for game, but nothing did he kill; for utterly worthless was he. In time they were in want of food. So it was due to Skunk that they had food to eat. Finally never a thing did Lynx care to do.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding idash gichi-aabawani (now once there was a great thaw); a’aw zhigaag ezhi-maajaad giiyosed (Skunk set off to hunt). Ningoding odookawi’aan iniw bizhiwan (once she came on the trail of a Lynx), mii sa ezhi-oojimod endaad (and she fled home). Ningoding idash zhigwa dagwishinon iniw bizhiwan (and once when the Lynx arrived), mii sa wii-wiiwimigod (she was wanted by him for a wife); mii sa gegapii gii-wiiwimigod (and at last he took her to wife). Mii dash iniw endawenjigenid (and now it was he that hunted), geget gaawiin gegoo onitoosiin (but truly he killed nothing); aapiji goopadizi (utterly worthless was he). Zhigwa bakadewag (now they were hungry). Aaniish mii a’aw zhigaag anishaa odayaan i’iw ge-miijiwaad (so it was Skunk alone who had what they would eat). Gegapii gaawiin wiikaa ningodino wii-inanokiisii a’aw bizhiw (at last never at all would the Lynx do a thing).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Ninguting unāgucininig āwasut owāwâbandānạn upwāmạn. Kīgito: “Ạmạntcigic ä‘pugisiwānän! Ạmạntcsa pa‘kwäcạmān iᵉi’ᵘ nimpwām!” Kägä‘t äcipa‘kwäcạnk iyupwāmạn, unāⁿsitīwạgōn. Mīdạc iᵉi´ᵘ kāpa‘kwäcạnk, ickutänk uda‘tōn. Äjikīcitänig; kā‘kīcitänig, utạgwāsitōn. Äcimīdcit, kīgito: “Kägä‘t niminu‘pugus.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding onaagoshininig awasod owaawaabandaanan obwaaman. Giigido: “Amanjigizh e-bagozhiwaanen! Amanj sa bakwezhamaan i’iw nimbwaam!” Geget ezhi-bakwezhang iyo-obwaaman, onaanzidiiwagon. Mii dash i’iw gaa-bakwezhang, ishkodeng odatoon. Ezhi-giizhidenig; gaa-giizhidenig, odagwaasitoon. Ezhi-miijid, giigido: “Geget nimino-bagoz.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When one evening he was warming himself, he was looking (with admiration) at his hips. He spoke, saying: “Wonder how I should taste! Suppose that I slice (a portion) off my hip!” Of a truth, he then sliced off (a piece) from his hip, from the back of his thigh. And then, after he had sliced off a portion, into the fire he placed it. Then it was cooked; after it was cooked, he took it off the fire. Then, eating it, he spoke, saying: “Of a truth, I am good to eat.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding onaagoshininig awasod (one evening as he warmed himself) owaawaabandaanan obwaaman (he gazed at his hips/thigh). Giigido (he spoke): “Amanjigizh e-bagozhiwaanen! (wonder how I would taste!) Amanj sa bakwezhamaan i’iw nimbwaam! (suppose I slice off a piece of my thigh!)” Geget ezhi-bakwezhang iyo-obwaaman (truly he sliced off his thigh), onaanzidiiwagon (from the back of it). Mii dash i’iw gaa-bakwezhang (and what he sliced off), ishkodeng odatoon (he set in the fire). Ezhi-giizhidenig (when it was cooked); gaa-giizhidenig, odagwaasitoon (when cooked, he took it off). Ezhi-miijid, giigido (eating it, he said): “Geget nimino-bagoz (truly I taste good).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Cigāg ogạnōnān: “Nackänā acạmicin!” Mīsa´ kāwīn utacạmigusīn. Mīsanā kīwīsinit pici´ᵘ. “Kägä‘t niminu‘pugus.” Ninguting mīnawā, ānīc, mī ā‘pidci cigwạ pa‘kadäwāt. Ninguting mīnawā äji∙ā∙wạsut, “Ạmạntcigic iᵉi´ᵘ ninạgic ä‘pugwatugwän! Tạga ningapạgutcīnitis.” Kägä‘t äcipagutcīnitisut äcimạmōt ābi‘ta iᵉi´ᵘ unạgic. Kāmạmōt, ickutänk uta‘pagitōn. Äji∙a∙‘ka‘kacäbwät, “Tciⁿ, tciⁿī tciⁿ, tciⁿ,” inwäwätäni iᵉi´ᵘ unạgic. Äji∙ạ∙gwāsitōt, mīsa´ mīnawā äcimīdcit. Kā‘kidāng, ā‘pidci minu‘pugwạt inändạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigaag oganoonaan: “Nashke naa ashamishin!” Mii sa gaawiin odashamigosiin. Mii sa naa gii-wiisinid bizhiw. “Geget nimino-bagoz.” Ningoding miinawaa, aaniish, mii aapiji zhigwa bakadewaad. Ningoding miinawaa ezhi-aawasod, “Amanjigizh i’iw ninagizh ebagwadogwen! Daga ningabagojiinidis*.” Geget ezhi-bagojiinidisod* ezhi-mamood aabita i’iw onagizh. Gaa-mamood, ishkodeng odapagitoon. Ezhi-akakanzhebwed, “Chin, chinii chin, chin,” inwewedeni* i’iw onagizh. Ezhi-agwaasitood, mii sa miinawaa ezhi-miijid. Gaa-gidaang*, aapiji mino-bagwad* inendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Skunk spoke to him, saying: “Come, now, and give me some to eat!” But she was not fed. And so then Lynx ate. “Of a truth, I am good to eat.” One other time, well, it was when they were very much in want of food. Another time, when he was warming himself, “Wonder how my entrails would taste! Now, I will open myself at the belly.” In truth, when he opened himself at the belly, he then removed a part of his entrails. After removing them, into the fire he put them. When he roasted them on hot coals, “Tciⁿ, tciⁿ, tciⁿ, tciⁿ!” was the sound his entrails made. When he took them off the fire, he then ate again. After eating them up, very good was their taste, he fancied.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigaag oganoonaan (Skunk spoke to him): “Nashke naa ashamishin! (come now, feed me!)” Mii sa gaawiin odashamigosiin (but she was not fed by him). Mii sa naa gii-wiisinid bizhiw (and so the Lynx ate). “Geget nimino-bagoz (truly I taste good).” Ningoding miinawaa, aaniish (and one more time, well), mii aapiji zhigwa bakadewaad (then very much were they in want of food). Ningoding miinawaa ezhi-aawasod (another time, when he was warming himself), “Amanjigizh i’iw ninagizh ebagwadogwen! (wonder how my entrails would taste!) Daga ningabagojiinidis*. (now, I will open myself at the belly.)” Geget ezhi-bagojiinidisod* (in truth, when he opened himself at the belly) ezhi-mamood aabita i’iw onagizh (he then removed a part of his entrails). Gaa-mamood, ishkodeng odapagitoon (after removing them, into the fire he put them). Ezhi-akakanzhebwed (when he roasted them on hot coals), “Chin, chinii chin, chin,” (*vocable, carried as printed*) inwewedeni* i’iw onagizh (was the sound his entrails made). Ezhi-agwaasitood (when he took them off the fire), mii sa miinawaa ezhi-miijid (he then ate again). Gaa-gidaang*, aapiji mino-bagwad* inendam (after eating them up, very good was their taste, he fancied).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Mīsa´ iᵉi´ᵘ mīdạc iᵉi´ᵘ cigāg nickī∙i∙gut. “Kägä‘t māma‘kātc ạcamisiwānk. Intawā wābạnk mādcān, asām kāwīn kidạcamisīmin.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa i’iw mii dash i’iw zhigaag nishki’igod. “Geget maamakaaj ashamisiwaang. Indawaa waabang maajaan, asam gaawiin gidashamisiimin.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon was Skunk made angry: “Truly strange it is that you do not feed us. Therefore to-morrow you leave, for too often do you fail to feed us.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa i’iw mii dash i’iw zhigaag nishki’igod (thereupon Skunk was angered by it). “Geget maamakaaj ashamisiwaang (truly strange that you do not feed us). Indawaa waabang maajaan (therefore tomorrow leave), asam gaawiin gidashamisiimin (for too often you fail to feed us).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) A‘pī wayābạninig kägä‘t kisināni; mīgu iᵘ mīnōtc äcimādcāt pici´ᵘ kigicäp. Wayäntcī‘kä kī‘kạtci. Ningutingiku mạtāpīckusiwä sībīns; änimạdāpīckusiwät ā‘pidci kī‘kạtci. Nāwaya∙i∙ agāwa ugacki‘tōn i∙i∙māⁿ nā∙ạ∙ckusi iᵘ icāt. Kīwätinnuk äji∙i∙nābit, ä‘tawā! mī∙i∙´ᵘ mackawā‘kwạtcit, pạnāgu niskīngwänit; mīgu i´ᵘ änā-‘kwạtcit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii wayaabaninig geget gisinaani; miigo iw miinooj ezhi-maajaad bizhiw gigizheb. Wayenji-kaa gii-gashkadji. Ningoding igo madaabiishkozhiwe-ziibiins; ani-madaabiishkozhiwed aapiji gii-gashkadji. Naawayi’ii agaawaa ogashkitoon i’imaa naa-ashkozid iw izhaad. Giiwedinong ezhi-inaabid, e-tawaa! mii iw mashkawaakwajid, banaa go niskiingwenid; miigo iw enaakwajid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When the morrow came, truly cold it was; but nevertheless away went Lynx in the morning. From the very beginning he was cold. And by and by he came out upon a meadow (where there was) a brook; while coming out upon the meadow, very cold he became. Hardly was he able to get as far as the middle of the meadow. When towards the north he then looked, poor fellow! he was then freezing, and all the while he made an ugly face; whereupon that was the way he was frozen.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii wayaabaninig geget gisinaani (when morning came it was truly cold); miigo iw miinooj ezhi-maajaad bizhiw gigizheb (yet even so the Lynx set off at dawn). Wayenji-kaa gii-gashkadji (from the very start he was cold). Ningoding igo madaabiishkozhiwe-ziibiins (by and by he came out on a brook-meadow); ani-madaabiishkozhiwed aapiji gii-gashkadji (coming out onto it he grew very cold). Naawayi’ii agaawaa ogashkitoon i’imaa naa-ashkozid iw izhaad (hardly could he get to the middle of the meadow he was going to). Giiwedinong ezhi-inaabid (when he looked to the north), e-tawaa! (poor fellow!) mii iw mashkawaakwajid (then he froze stiff), banaa go niskiingwenid (making an ugly face the while); miigo iw enaakwajid (and that is how he froze).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Mīsa´ piciwicīgạn cigāg pimādcā pipima∙a∙nāt. Ä‘tawā! ninguting ạni∙i∙nābit unāpämạn kicingicinōn kī‘kawạtcinit. Mīsa imāⁿ äjimawit; kā‘tämawit intawā äjikīwät. Mīdạc imāⁿ äntāwāt tibātcimut: “Mī gī‘kawạtcit aᵉa´ᵘ pici´ᵘ.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa bizhiwishigan zhigaag bimaajaa bi-bima’anaad. E-tawaa! ningoding ani-inaabid onaabeman gizhingishinon gii-gawajinid. Mii sa imaa ezhi-mawid; gaa-demawid indawaa ezhi-giiwed. Mii dash imaa endaawaad dibaajimod: “Mii gii-gawajid a’aw bizhiw.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And now behind Lynx came Skunk following in his tracks. Alas! by and by, while going along she was looking about, there lay her husband, who had frozen to death. Thereupon then she wept; after she had had a surfeit of crying, then back home she went. And then at the place where they lived she told the news: “Therefore now has Lynx frozen to death.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa zhigaag bimaajaa bi-bima’anaad (and now Skunk came along on his tracks). E-tawaa! ningoding ani-inaabid (alas! by and by as she looked about) onaabeman gizhingishinon gii-gawajinid (there lay her husband, frozen to death). Mii sa imaa ezhi-mawid (thereupon she wept there); gaa-demawid indawaa ezhi-giiwed (when she had wept her fill she went home). Mii dash imaa endaawaad dibaajimod (and there where they lived she told it): “Mii gii-gawajid a’aw bizhiw (now the Lynx has frozen to death).”</span>

*¹ It is not brought out clearly here, but this is one account of the origin of the squinting look of the lynx. — Jones’s note, foot of p. 705. gawaji- “freeze to death”; bima’anaad “follow his trail”; the surfeit-of-weeping gaa-demawid drafted. bizhiwishigan §-init word uncertain, flagged.*

<span class="l-orig">(9) Ā‘pidci mānäntạm aᵉa´ᵘ ma‘kicinic, kayä aᵉa´ᵘ migōs. “Ānīc kätīyạnk?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aapiji maanendam a’aw makizinish, gaye a’aw migoos. “Aaniish ge-dodamang”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Very sad was the miserable old Moccasin, so too the Awl. “What are we going to do?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aapiji maanendam a’aw makizinish (very downcast was the old Moccasin), gaye a’aw migoos (and the Awl too). “Aaniish ge-doodamang? (what shall we do?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) “Mānūⁿ! ō∙o∙māⁿ ayātā.” Mīsa cigwa mādci‘tāt cigāg nạntawäntcigät, mīsa´ iᵉi´ᵘ kāwīn käyābi pa‘kạtäsiwạg. Ningutingiku wâbōsōn udōtisiguwān äjikạgwätcimiguwāt: “Kāna umāⁿ nintā∙a∙yāsī?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Maanoo! o’omaa ayaadaa.” Mii sa zhigwa maajiitaad zhigaag nandawenjiged, mii sa i’iw gaawiin geyaabi bakadesiiwag. Ningoding igo waaboozoon odoodisigowaan ezhi-gagwejimigowaad: “Gaa na omaa nindaa-ayaasii?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Never you mind! here let us remain.” Accordingly then to work set Skunk hunting for game, and then no longer did they lack for food. Now, once by a Hare they were visited, when by him they were asked: “May I not continue here?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Maanoo! o’omaa ayaadaa. (never mind! here let us stay.)” Mii sa zhigwa maajiitaad zhigaag nandawenjiged (so then Skunk set to hunting), mii sa i’iw gaawiin geyaabi bakadesiiwag (so they were no longer hungry). Ningoding igo waaboozoon odoodisigowaan (once a Hare came to them) ezhi-gagwejimigowaad (and was asking them): “Gaa na omaa nindaa-ayaasii? (might I not stay here?)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) “Wä∙i∙bạnạnā, omāⁿ wītạcipibōniciyạn.” Ā‘pidci kägä‘t ni‘tānạntāwäntcigäwạn. Kägä‘t ā‘pidci anōtcigu kägō ubītōni wīsiniwīn. Ningutingidạc kī∙o∙sänit pītcinicimōwạn. “Ningī∙o∙‘kawi∙ā∙g īgi’ᵘ pigic kanitiskäsiwạg.” Mīdạc iᵉi´ᵘ cigāg äjigạnōnāt: “Awänänạg idạc iᵉi’ᵘ äcini‘kānạtwā pigickanitiskäsi?” “Kā, nạngwạna piciwạg, mīwagigi äcini‘kāsowāt. Kīcpin u‘kawī∙i∙wāt mīwạgigi käniciwāt; ningapītānigōg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Wewiib inanaa, omaa wii-dazhi-biboonishiyan.” Aapiji geget nitaa-nandawenjigewan. Geget aapiji anooji go gegoo obiidoonan wiisiniwin. Ningoding idash gii-osenid biijinizhimowan. “Ningii-ookawi’aag igiw bigizh-ganidiskesiwag.” Mii dash i’iw zhigaag ezhi-ganoonaad: “Awenenag idash i’iw ezhi-nikaanadwaa* bigizh-ganidiskesii?” “Gaa*, nangwana bizhiwag, miiwagigi* ezhi-nikaasowaad. Giishpin okawi’iwaad* miiwagigi genizhiwaad; ningabiitaanigoog*.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“(You are) welcome, in this place you may pass the winter.” A thoroughly fine hunter of game he truly was. In truth, just about every sort of thing in the way of food he fetched home. And once, while out on the hunt, he came home in speedy flight. “I got on trail of those Putrid-Navels.” Thereupon Skunk spoke to him, saying: “And who may they be whom you call by the name of Putrid-Navels?” “Why, it is the Lynxes, for such is what they are called. If they come upon my trail, then it is they that will slay me; by them shall I be followed into where I am.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Wewiib inanaa, omaa wii-dazhi-biboonishiyan. (do come, here you may pass the winter.)” Aapiji geget nitaa-nandawenjigewan (a truly skilled hunter he was). Geget aapiji anooji go gegoo obiidoonan wiisiniwin (truly all kinds of food he brought home). Ningoding idash gii-osenid biijinizhimowan (and once, while hunting, he came fleeing home). “Ningii-ookawi’aag igiw bigizh-ganidiskesiwag. (I struck the trail of those Putrid-Navels.)” Mii dash i’iw zhigaag ezhi-ganoonaad (thereupon Skunk spoke to him): “Awenenag idash i’iw ezhi-nikaanadwaa* bigizh-ganidiskesii? (and who may they be whom you call by the name of Putrid-Navels?)” “Gaa*, nangwana bizhiwag (why, it is the Lynxes), miiwagigi* ezhi-nikaasowaad (for such is what they are called).
