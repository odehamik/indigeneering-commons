A MOOSE AND HIS OFFSPRING.

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Wāsāgunäckạnk — Series 3 (Nos. 44–61), Bois Fort (per Jones Part II Introduction p. ix; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1919).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Mōⁿs wī‘pibōnici, kayä īni´ᵘ wīwạn. Nīciwa unitcānisaᵉ, kayä päcigowạn īni’ᵘ udōckinawämạn; mī∙i∙’ᵘ nānạniwāt. Mīsa’ äjiku‘tātciwāt, ini´ᵘ anicinābän kusāwāt. Ā‘pidcisa wâwâsa icā aᵘ uckinawä, mīsā‘panā ānutạcimāt a‘kiwäⁿzī. “Kigạmicā∙ạ∙nik ạnicinābä.” Kāwīn ānicī‘tạnzī. Ninguting pimi‘kawäwạn pa‘kān mōⁿsōn; ugi‘känimān i‘kwäwinit. Mīsa´ mādā∙ạ∙nāt, mīsa´ wâbạmāt mī∙i∙´ᵘ kīwīwit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mooz wii-biboonizhi, gaye iniw wiiwan. Niizhiwa onijaanisag, gaye bezhigowan iniw odoshkinaweman; mii i’iw nananiwaad. Mii sa ezhi-godatchiwaad, iniw anishinaaben gosaawaad. Aapijisa waawaasa izhaa aw oshkinawe, mii sa-ban enootachimaad akiwenzii. “Gigamizhaa-anik anishinaabe.” Gaawiin anishitanzii. Ningoding bimikawewan bakaan moozoon; ogiikenimaan ikwewinid. Mii sa madaa’anaad, mii sa waabamaad iniw giiwiwid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*The Moose was about to go into camp for the winter, and also his wife. Two (in number) were their children, and there was a youth among them; therefore they were five. It was so that they were in fear, of people they were in fear. On very long journeys frequently went the youth, whereupon continually was the old man trying to dissuade him (not to go so far). “Upon your trail might come the people.” But (the youth) paid no heed. Once (he saw) the tracks of another Moose; he knew it was a cow. Accordingly he followed after her, whereat, on seeing her, he took her to wife.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mooz wii-biboonizhi (the Moose was about to winter — *stem flagged*), gaye iniw wiiwan (and his wife too). Niizhiwa onijaanisag (two were their children), gaye bezhigowan iniw odoshkinaweman (and one of them was a youth); mii i’iw nananiwaad (so they were five — *naanan ‘five’; stem flagged*). Mii sa ezhi-godatchiwaad (and so they were fearful), iniw anishinaaben gosaawaad (of people they were afraid). Aapijisa waawaasa izhaa aw oshkinawe (very far away the youth would go), mii sa-ban enootachimaad akiwenzii (whereupon the old man kept trying to dissuade him — *anood-/aanawendam-? stem flagged*). “Gigamizhaa-anik anishinaabe.” (“the people might come upon your trail.”) Gaawiin anishitanzii (but he paid no heed — *stem flagged*). Ningoding bimikawewan bakaan moozoon (once he came on the tracks of another moose); ogiikenimaan ikwewinid (he knew it was a cow). Mii sa madaa’anaad (and so he followed her trail), mii sa waabamaad iniw giiwiwid (and so, on seeing her, he took her to wife — *wiidigemaa-; stem flagged*).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Cigwagu māgu mini‘k wāwit, pa‘kān awīya īni´ᵘ mōⁿzōn udōdisikuwān; nāsāb wāwītigämigut; kägä‘t wītigämāt. Mīdac iᵉi´ᵘ nīⁿc ayāwāt i‘kwäwaᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa go maa* go minik waawid, bakaan awiya iniw moozoon odoodisigowaan; naasaab wawiidigemigod; geget wiidigemaad. Mii dash i’iw niizh ayaawaad ikwewag.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*During this time that he had her for wife, by another Moose were they visited; and by her, as by the other, was he desired for a husband; to be sure, he married her. Therefore two were the wives he had.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa go maa* go minik waawid (now during the while he had her to wife), bakaan awiya iniw moozoon odoodisigowaan (by another moose they were visited); naasaab wawiidigemigod (likewise was he desired for a husband by her — *stem flagged*); geget wiidigemaad (sure enough, he married her). Mii dash i’iw niizh ayaawaad ikwewag (and so two were the wives he had).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Kägä‘t wawība mīgātiwaᵉ. Ningutingigu mādcā, ōsạn icāt. Kā‘tạgwicing äjikanōnāt īni´ᵘ ōsạn: “Kägä‘t, nōs, nīⁿc nintayāwāg īgi´ᵘ i‘kwäwạg.” Ugạnōnigōn: “Ningwisis, kägu´ icictcigä‘kän tcinīciwāt tci∙a∙yāwạtwā īgi’ᵘ i‘kwäwạg. Kạnạbạtc tạbā‘tā∙i∙tiwạg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Geget wawiiba miigaadiwag. Ningoding igo maajaa, oosan izhaad. Gaa-dagwishing ezhi-ganoonaad iniw oosan: “Geget, noos, niizh nindayaawaag igiw ikwewag.” Oganoonigoon: “Ningwisis, gego izhichigeken ji-niizhiwaad ji-ayaawadwaa igiw ikwewag. Ganabaj daa-baataa’idiwag.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*In truth, very frequently did they fight. And once he went away, to his father he went. After he was come, he spoke to his father, saying: “Verily, my father, two (are) the women I have.” He was addressed by him saying: “My son, do not bring it about that there be two women for you to have. Perhaps they might do harm to each other.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Geget wawiiba miigaadiwag (truly, very often they fought). Ningoding igo maajaa (and once he set off), oosan izhaad (going to his father). Gaa-dagwishing (after he arrived) ezhi-ganoonaad iniw oosan (he spoke to his father): “Geget, noos, niizh nindayaawaag igiw ikwewag.” (“truly, father, two are the women I have.”) Oganoonigoon (he was addressed by him): “Ningwisis, gego izhichigeken (“my son, do not bring it about) ji-niizhiwaad ji-ayaawadwaa igiw ikwewag (that you have two women). Ganabaj daa-baataa’idiwag.” (perhaps they might harm one another — *baataa’idi- ‘harm each other’ stem flagged*.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) “Äyeᵉ,” udinān ōsạn. Mīdạc iᵘ wäyābạninig äjikīwät; cigwa tạgwicin ändāwāt. Mīsa´ kägä‘t kīnisîmint päjig ini´ᵘ wīwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aye’,” odinaan oosan. Mii dash iw wayaabaninig ezhi-giiwed; zhigwa dagwishin endaawaad. Mii sa geget gii-nisimind bezhig iniw wiiwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Ay,” he said to his father. And then on the morrow he went back home; in a while he arrived at where they dwelt. Whereupon, sure enough, (he found) that one of his wives had been killed.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aye’,” odinaan oosan (“ay,” he said to his father). Mii dash iw wayaabaninig (and then on the morrow) ezhi-giiwed (he went home); zhigwa dagwishin endaawaad (in a while he arrived where they lived). Mii sa geget gii-nisimind bezhig iniw wiiwan (and sure enough, one of his wives had been killed).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Ningutingigu tạgwicinuᵉ mōⁿzūᵉ nīc. Cigwa kạnōnā: “Ānīc wäntcinīcō‘kwäwäyạn? Kāwin kitāgīcictcigäsī.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding igo dagwishinow moozoog niizh. Zhigwa ganoonaa: “Aaniish wenji-niizhookwaweyan? Gaawiin gidaa-gii-izhichigesii.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And once there arrived two other Moose. Presently they spoke to him, saying: “Why did you have two wives? You should not have done so.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding igo dagwishinow moozoog niizh (and once two other moose arrived). Zhigwa ganoonaa (presently he was spoken to): “Aaniish wenji-niizhookwaweyan? (“why did you have two wives? — *niizhookwe- ‘have two women’ stem flagged*) Gaawiin gidaa-gii-izhichigesii.” (you should not have done so.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Ānīc kīmōtc waᵉa´ᵘ uckī∙i∙nini äjipa‘kwạtcibināt iᵉi´ᵘ uniciwaᵉ, kā∙i∙ji∙a∙‘pagināt ningābī∙ạ∙nung iji gwaya‘k.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish giimooj wa’aw oshki-inini ezhi-bakwajibinaad i’iw onizhiwan, gaa-izhi-apaginaad ningaabii’anong izhi gwayak.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, in secret the youth had plucked out his testes, afterwards he flung them straight toward the west.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish giimooj wa’aw oshki-inini (now, in secret the youth) ezhi-bakwajibinaad i’iw onizhiwan (plucked out his testes — *bakwaji-bin- ‘pluck/tear out by hand’*), gaa-izhi-apaginaad (then he flung them) ningaabii’anong izhi gwayak (straight toward the west).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Mīdạc ä‘kitowāt īgi´ᵘ i‘kwäwạg: “Indawā, ninō‘pinạnānānig īgi´ᵘ kiniciwạg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash ekidowaad igiw ikwewag: “Indawaa, ninopinananaanig igiw ginizhiwag.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then said the women: “Therefore we will follow after your testes.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash ekidowaad igiw ikwewag (and then said the women): “Indawaa, ninopinananaanig igiw ginizhiwag.” (“so then, we will follow after your testes.” — *noopinan- ‘follow after’; possessor reading of ginizhiwag flagged*)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Mīdac iᵉi´ᵘ ā‘pidci ā‘kusi, agāwa ugạcki‘tōn. Kīwät ijāt ōsạn. Cigwa pīndigä, mīsa cigwa māsumigut īniyōsạn. “Mī∙i∙´ᵘ ānugī∙u∙ndci∙i∙ninān. Wâwiyạg kītōntci∙ā∙‘kus. Mī intawā pîsān ici∙ā∙yān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash i’iw aapiji aakozi, agaawaa ogashkitoon. Giiwed izhaad oosan. Zhigwa biindige, mii sa zhigwa maazomigod iniyoosan. “Mii i’iw aano-gii-onji-i’ininaan. Waawiyazh gii-doonji-aakos. Mii indawaa biisaan izhi-ayaan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon he became exceedingly ill, hardly was he able to go back to his father. In time he arrived within (the wigwam), whereupon then he began to undergo treatment from his father. “Such was the reason why I tried to dissuade you from your purpose. Because of this disobedience you became sick. Therefore now you should remain quietly by.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash i’iw aapiji aakozi (thereupon he became very ill), agaawaa ogashkitoon (he could hardly manage it). Giiwed izhaad oosan (he went home to his father). Zhigwa biindige (in time he came inside), mii sa zhigwa maazomigod iniyoosan (whereupon his father began to treat him — *maazom-/mashkiki- treatment stem flagged*). “Mii i’iw aano-gii-onji-i’ininaan (“that is why in vain I told you so — *fused; flagged*). Waawiyazh gii-doonji-aakos (because of (this) wrongdoing you became sick — *stem flagged*). Mii indawaa biisaan izhi-ayaan.” (so now you should stay quietly by.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Cigwa ā‘pitapibōnini. Ningutci pạbā‘tacī‘käwạg aya∙āⁿ∙sạg. Sōgi‘poninig nạgạmōwạg īgi´ᵘ ạbinōtcīⁿyạg mōzōⁿsạg. Kägä‘t minwäntạmōg zōgi‘pōninig:—</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa aabitabiboonini. Ningoji babaa-dazhi-gewag aya-aanhsag. Zoogiponinig nagamowag igiw abinoojiinyag moozoonsag. Geget minwendamog zoogiponinig:—**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*By this time the winter was halfway gone. In certain places roundabout where they lived wandered the calves. When it snowed, (then) sang the young Moose. Truly happy they were when it snowed:—*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigwa aabitabiboonini (by now the winter was half gone — *aabita-biboon- ‘mid-winter’*). Ningoji babaa-dazhi-gewag aya-aanhsag (here and there roundabout wandered the calves — *stem flagged*). Zoogiponinig (when it snowed) nagamowag igiw abinoojiinyag moozoonsag (the young moose-calves sang). Geget minwendamog zoogiponinig (truly happy they were when it snowed):—</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Pūnīwa, pūnīwa!  
Pūnīwa, pūnīwa!  
Pūnīwa, pūnīwa!  
Pūnīwa, pūnīwa!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“May more snow fall, may some more snow fall! May more snow fall, may some more snow fall! May more snow fall, may some more snow fall! May more snow fall, may some more snow fall!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Pūnīwa, pūnīwa! …” — the snow-song of the young moose, carried VERBATIM and unconverted (merge-excluded). Jones glosses it “May more snow fall, may some more snow fall!” The form rests on boon-/poon- ‘snow falls’; not normalized.</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Mī∙i∙‘ᵘ äna∙ạ∙mawāt īgi’ᵘ mōⁿzōnsạg. Unōntāguwān ugiwān äjigạnōniguwāt: “Kägu’ ina∙ạ∙ngägun, kạnạbạtc kagwatcinīwana∙a∙‘gō unābạna‘k kīcpîn nībiwa zōgi‘puk.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii ena’amawaad igiw moozoonsag. Onoondagowaan ogiwaan ezhi-ganoonigowaad: “Gego ina’angegon, ganabaj gagwajiniiwanagowaa-ban onaabanak giishpin niibiwa zoogipog.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thus sang the young Moose. They were heard by their mother, by whom they were then addressed: “Do not sing such a song, lest perhaps you be laid low with a club on the hardened crust, if much snow falls.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii ena’amawaad igiw moozoonsag (thus sang the young moose — *ina’amaw- ‘sing thus’ stem flagged*). Onoondagowaan ogiwaan (they were heard by their mother) ezhi-ganoonigowaad (and were addressed by her): “Gego ina’angegon (“do not sing such a song), ganabaj gagwajiniiwanagowaa-ban onaabanak (lest you be clubbed down on the hard snow-crust — *long fused verb; stem flagged*) giishpin niibiwa zoogipog.” (if much snow falls.”)</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa i’iw ezhi-ishkwaataawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon they ceased.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa i’iw ezhi-ishkwaataawaad (thereupon they ceased — *ishkwaa- ‘stop, cease’*).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) Ningutingigu ā‘pitcigu ạniminu∙a∙ya aᵘ uckînawä. Mī∙i∙cigwa mādcāt ạbāgagwätusät; ā‘pitcigu minōpạpimusä. Ningutingigu owâbạndān ayagawā‘täyāsininig iᵉi’ᵘ āna‘kwạt; kägä‘t umānäntān tcinạgạtạngibạn. Mīdạc iᵉi’ᵘ äcigạgwätcikạtạnk ā‘pidci tciwâsa unạgạtān. Kägä‘t minwäntạm nạgạtạnk āna‘kwạt. Äni∙i∙cigīwät; kā pīndigät ändāwāt, ugạnōnān ōsạn: “Nōs, kägä‘t kikīwạnîm ä‘kidoyạn kicī‘kā aᵉa´ᵘ anicinābä kī∙i∙‘kitoyạn. Nōngum kī‘kīciga‘k ningīgạgwätcikạtān iᵉi´ᵘ āna‘kwạt, wâsa ningīnạgạtān. Mādcīna tā∙a∙pīsi‘kā waᵉaᵘ anicinābä.” Mīdac i∙i∙ᵘ äcikạnōnigut ōsạn: “Ningwisis, kägä‘t kigitcikidimāgis wimänänimāt aᵉa´ᵘ ạnicinābä. Mạnidōwⁱ anicinābä‘k. Nōngum kigạgi‘kändān kīcpîn wawâsa wi∙i∙cāyạn wäntcimanitōwit anicinābä. Udawāᵉ kägä‘kwaᵉ kayä wäᵉwäᵉ, mī∙i∙´ᵘ wändcigicī‘kād aᵉaᵘ ạnicinābä.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding igo aapiji ani-mino-ayaa aw oshkinawe. Mii i’iw zhigwa maajaad abaagagwedosed; aapiji go mino-babimose. Ningoding igo owaabandaan ayagawe-deyaasininig i’iw aanakwad; geget omaanendaan ji-nagadang-iban. Mii dash i’iw ezhi-gagwejigadang aapiji ji-waasa onagadaan. Geget minwendam nagadang aanakwad. Ani-izhi-giiwed; gaa-biindiged endaawaad, oganoonaan oosan: “Noos, geget gigiiwanim ekidoyan gizhiikaa a’aw anishinaabe gii-ikidoyan. Noongom gii-giizhigak ningii-gagwejigadaan i’iw aanakwad, waasa ningii-nagadaan. Maajiina daa-bisikaa wa’aw anishinaabe.” Mii dash i’iw ezhi-ganoonigod oosan: “Ningwisis, geget gigichi-gidimaagiz wii-menenimaad a’aw anishinaabe. Manidoowi anishinaabeg. Noongom giga-gikendaan giishpin waawaasa wii-izhaayan wenji-manidoowid anishinaabe. Odawaag gegekwag gaye weweg, mii i’iw wenji-gizhiikaad a’aw anishinaabe.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And in course of time to very much better health was the youth restored. Therefore then he started off, trying to see how he could travel; and very comfortably did he walk along. And once he saw where the cloud had cast a shadow; in truth, he believed that he could outstrip it. Accordingly, when he ran it a race, a very great distance behind he left it. Truly pleased was he to have outrun the cloud. Then on his homeward way he went. When he entered into where they lived, he spoke to his father, saying: “My father, of a truth, you deceived me when you said that speedy is a human being. On this day now past I raced with the cloud, far behind I outran it. Not so swift as that would a human being be.” Thereupon he was addressed by his father saying: “My dear son, of a truth, you are greatly to be pitied for regarding with contempt a human being. Of the nature of a manitou is a human being. To-day you shall learn, if very far you intend to go, how it is that a human being is of the nature of a manitou. He makes use of bird-hawks and swans, and on that account speedy is a human being.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding igo aapiji ani-mino-ayaa aw oshkinawe (in time the youth grew much better). Mii i’iw zhigwa maajaad abaagagwedosed (then he set off, trying out his walking — *stem flagged*); aapiji go mino-babimose (and walked along very comfortably). Ningoding igo owaabandaan ayagawe-deyaasininig i’iw aanakwad (once he saw where the cloud cast its shadow — *stem flagged*); geget omaanendaan ji-nagadang-iban (truly he thought he could leave it behind). Mii dash i’iw ezhi-gagwejigadang (so he raced it) aapiji ji-waasa onagadaan (and left it far behind). Geget minwendam nagadang aanakwad (truly pleased to have outrun the cloud). Ani-izhi-giiwed (then he went home); gaa-biindiged endaawaad (when he came inside) oganoonaan oosan (he spoke to his father): “Noos, geget gigiiwanim (“father, truly you deceived me) ekidoyan gizhiikaa a’aw anishinaabe gii-ikidoyan (when you said the human being is swift). Noongom gii-giizhigak ningii-gagwejigadaan i’iw aanakwad (today I raced the cloud), waasa ningii-nagadaan (far behind I left it). Maajiina daa-bisikaa wa’aw anishinaabe.” (not even that swift would a human being be — *bizikaa-? stem flagged*) Mii dash i’iw ezhi-ganoonigod oosan (then his father addressed him): “Ningwisis, geget gigichi-gidimaagiz (“my son, you are greatly to be pitied) wii-menenimaad a’aw anishinaabe (for holding the human being in contempt — *meneni m- ‘scorn’ stem flagged*). Manidoowi anishinaabeg (of manitou-nature is the human being). Noongom giga-gikendaan (today you shall learn) giishpin waawaasa wii-izhaayan (if you mean to go very far) wenji-manidoowid anishinaabe (why the human being is of manitou-nature). Odawaag gegekwag gaye weweg (he makes use of bird-hawks and swans), mii i’iw wenji-gizhiikaad a’aw anishinaabe.” (that is why the human being is swift.”)</span>

Ningoding igo babimosed owaabandaan awiya bimikawenid; indigwa mitigoon naazhwaakodaabaadaminid, mii i’iw ezhikawenid. “Memaawiina-a’aw anishinaabe gaa-izhikawed,” inendam. Ani-izhi-mataa’anaad.
