WÂSĀGUNÄCKẠNK UTATIBĀTCIMUWIN.

<span class="l-fiero">**WAASAAGONESHKANG ODADIBAAJIMOWIN.**</span>

THE NARRATIVE OF HE-THAT-LEAVES-THE-IMPRINT-OF-HIS-FOOT-IN-THE-SNOW-GLISTENING.

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Wāsāgunäckạnk — Series 3 (Nos. 44–61), Bois Fort (per Jones Part II Introduction p. ix; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1919).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ānīnt i‘kitō ạnicinābä — kä‘tä ạnicinābä, kīcitäbwäyäntạm — mīdac ‘iᵉi´ᵘ ōguwäti tibickō‘kạmig kā∙i∙nā‘kunigäwāt ki∙tcimạnitōg. A‘pīsa´ kā‘kici‘tōt Nänabucu a‘ki´ mī cigwa kītibä‘kunigäwāt īgi´ᵘ ki∙tcimạnitōg. “Ạmbäsa, uci∙ā∙tānig īgi‘ᵘ kä∙ạ∙nicinābäwiwāt,” i‘kitōwaᵉ ‘iᵉi´ᵘ mạnitōᵉ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniind ikido anishinaabe — gete-anishinaabe, giizhi-debweyendam — mii dash i’iw ogowedi dibishkookamig gaa-izhi-naakonigewaad gichi-manidoog. Apii sa gaa-giizhitood Nenabozho aki, mii zhigwa gii-dibaakonigewaad igiw gichi-manidoog. “Ambe sa, ozhi-aataanig igiw ge-anishinaabewiwaad,” ikidowag i’iw manidoog.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Some people say — that is, the old-time people, (and they) believed in it — that it was these great manitous, that dwell directly opposite from us on the other side of the world, who decreed what should be. After Nänabushu had created the earth, then it was that the great manitous decreed what should be. “Behold, let us create them that are to be human beings,” (thus) said the manitous.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniind ikido anishinaabe (some of the people say), gete-anishinaabe (the old-time people), giizhi-debweyendam (and they fully believed it); mii dash i’iw (and so it is) ogowedi dibishkookamig (these ones directly opposite, on the other side of the world — *dibishkoo-akamig flagged*) gaa-izhi-naakonigewaad gichi-manidoog (the way the great manitous decreed it). Apii sa gaa-giizhitood Nenabozho aki (after Nenabozho had finished making the earth), mii zhigwa gii-dibaakonigewaad igiw gichi-manidoog (then the great manitous laid down the law). “Ambe sa, ozhi-aataanig (“come now, let us make them — *ozhi-aataanig: oshi- ‘make’ + imperative; flagged*) igiw ge-anishinaabewiwaad,” (those that are to be human beings,”) ikidowag i’iw manidoog (say the manitous).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Pājigidac äjikīgigitut ‘aᵉa´ᵘ ki‘tcimạnidō: “O‘ōwisa ta∙i∙ciwäbạt, tagagwatạgi‘tō aᵘ anicinābä. Māmwä‘tcigu uskabinōtciwiwit mī aᵘ käcawänimäk. Ta udci∙tcāgudạc aᵘ abinōtcī. Kīnawādạc, manitōtug! ninguting kīgakawạtisāwa aᵉa´ᵘ anicinābä. A‘pī wīmawạtisäg i‘kwädạc kapīndigäckawāwa; mīdạc aᵉ´aᵘ kä∙u∙gīyäg. ‘I‘i´widạc kä∙a∙‘kwānig ạnicinābä äciwīndamawik mini‘k käbimādisit; wawīngä kīwâpi‘kwät tcipikīwät.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Bezhig idash ezhi-giigidod a’aw gichi-manidoo: “O’oo sa da-izhiwebad, da-gagwaadagi’ido aw anishinaabe. Maamwe go go oshki-abinoojiiwiwid mii aw ge-zhawenimeg. Da-ojichaago dash aw abinoojii. Giinawaa dash, manidootog! ningoding giga-gawaadizaawaa a’aw anishinaabe. Apii wii-mawadiseg ikwe dash ga-biindigeshkawaawaa; mii dash a’aw ge-oogiyeg. I’iwi dash ge-aakwaanig anishinaabe ezhi-wiindamawig minik ge-bimaadizid; waawiinge gii-waabikwed ji-bigiwed.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then up spoke one of the great manitous, saying: “This is the way it shall be, let human beings undergo trials. And in particular, while they are yet in early infancy, that is when you shall bless them. And souls shall the infants have. And you, O manitous! at times shall you visit the people. When you desire to go visit the people, then into woman shall you enter; and of that one shall you make a mother. And knowledge shall you impart to the people of the length of time that they shall live; that when their hair is entirely white, then shall they return home.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Bezhig idash ezhi-giigidod a’aw gichi-manidoo (then one of the great manitous spoke): “O’oo sa da-izhiwebad (“this is how it shall be), da-gagwaadagi’ido aw anishinaabe (the human being shall undergo hardship). Maamwe go go oshki-abinoojiiwiwid (especially while still a new infant — *oshki-abinoojiiwiwid flagged*) mii aw ge-zhawenimeg (that is the one you shall bless). Da-ojichaago dash aw abinoojii (and the infant shall have a soul). Giinawaa dash, manidootog! (and you, O manitous! — *vocative; flagged*) ningoding giga-gawaadizaawaa a’aw anishinaabe (at times you shall visit the human being — *gawaadizaawaa: ‘visit’, stem flagged*). Apii wii-mawadiseg (when you wish to go visit) ikwe dash ga-biindigeshkawaawaa (you shall enter into a woman); mii dash a’aw ge-oogiyeg (and of that one you shall make a mother). I’iwi dash ge-aakwaanig anishinaabe (and that — the length the person will live —) ezhi-wiindamawig (you shall make known to him) minik ge-bimaadizid (how long he is to live); waawiinge gii-waabikwed (when his hair is fully white) ji-bigiwed.” (then he shall return home.”)</span>

*English footnote 1 (p.515), attaching to “return home.”: “This may mean one or both of two things: it may mean the return of the soul to the place whence it came; or it may be the idiom, so common among the aged, which means the close of this life and the departure for the spirit-world.” Restored 2026-09-02 from the page.*

<span class="l-orig">(3) Mī’sa cigwa mạnitō omawadisān anicinābän; ā´pidcidạc mạnitōwi o∙o∙mā a‘kīng. Mini‘kidạc kayä mänitōwisik ạnicinābä, kämāgayä ạtcina tapimādisi. Mī wīn i∙i∙‘ᵘ äjitäbwāyäntạnk aᵉa´ᵘ ạnicinābä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa zhigwa manidoo omawadisaan anishinaaben; aapiji dash manidoowi o’omaa akiing. Minik idash gaye menidoowisig anishinaabe, gemaa gaye achina da-bimaadizi. Mii wiin i’iw ezhi-debweyendang a’aw anishinaabe.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so now the manitous visit the people; and with very much supernatural power are they endued (while) here on earth. And of the number of people that are not endued with a sufficient amount of supernatural power, only a short while will they live. This is what the people believe.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii sa zhigwa manidoo omawadisaan anishinaaben (and so now the manidoo visits the person); aapiji dash manidoowi o’omaa akiing (and very full of spirit-power is he here on earth). Minik idash gaye menidoowisig anishinaabe (and the person who is not endowed with enough spirit-power), gemaa gaye achina da-bimaadizi (perhaps will live but a short while). Mii wiin i’iw ezhi-debweyendang a’aw anishinaabe (this is what the person believes).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Mīdạc ōwiti äcānit u∙tci∙tcāgwạn. Pōtc dạc kayä wīnawā mạnitōn ugwä‘kā‘kunigowān, tibickō mạnitōng āwiwạg. Mī wīn i∙i∙‘ᵘ äcitäbwäyäntạnk aᵉa´ᵘ anicinābä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash oowidi ezhaanid ojichaagwan. Booch idash gaye wiinawaa manidoon ogwekaakonigowaan, dibishkoo manidoong aawiwag. Mii wiin i’iw ezhi-debweyendang a’aw anishinaabe.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And it is off in this direction that the soul goes. And according as they desire, so are they transformed by the manitous, like manitous do they become. This, therefore, is what the people believe.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash oowidi ezhaanid ojichaagwan (and it is off in this direction that the soul goes — ojichaagwan ‘his/her soul’). Booch idash gaye wiinawaa manidoon ogwekaakonigowaan (and of necessity, too, by the manidoo they are turned/transformed — *gwekaakonige- ‘turn, decree-over’, stem flagged*), dibishkoo manidoong aawiwag (just like manidoos they become). Mii wiin i’iw ezhi-debweyendang a’aw anishinaabe (this is what the person believes).</span>

*English footnote 2 (p.515), attaching to “off in this direction”: “South, or west, or to the other side of the world, or to the heavens among the stars and suns.” Restored 2026-09-02 from the page.*

<span class="l-orig">(5) Naskä dạc o‘ō‘ᵘ nạnīsānạt,—nibōwin, iᵉi´ᵘ ā‘pitcinibuwin. Awägwän ä‘ta ā‘pidci käminopimādisigwän mī yä‘ta aᵘ kä∙u∙di‘tạnk iᵉi´ᵘ tạna‘kīwin. Naskäkuta ändasingin nạnīsānisiwinạn! Ā‘pidci yä‘ta mänupimādisit ayā iwiti tana‘kīwining. Kāwīndạc wī‘kā nibusī. Mī wīn i∙i∙‘ᵘ äcitäbwäyäntạnk aᵉa´ᵘ ạnicinābä, mī a‘panä gāginik ayānit utci∙tcāgwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Naske dash o’ow naniizaanad,—nibowin, i’iw aapiji-nibowin. Awegwen eta aapiji ge-mino-bimaadizigwen mii yeta aw ge-odidang i’iw danakiiwin. Naske gida endasingin naniizaanisiwinan! Aapiji yeta meno-bimaadizid ayaa iwidi danakiiwining. Gaawiin idash wiikaa niboosii. Mii wiin i’iw ezhi-debweyendang a’aw anishinaabe, mii apane gaaginiig ayaanid ojichaagwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Behold, this is the thing to be feared, — death, the death (that is) everlasting. Only they that live an upright life will come to that abiding-place. Why, behold the number of things to be feared! Only they that have lived an exceedingly upright life are at yonder habitation. And never will they die. This therefore is what the people believe, that forever do their souls continue there.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Naske dash o’ow naniizaanad (now behold, this is the fearsome thing),—nibowin, i’iw aapiji-nibowin (death, the everlasting death). Awegwen eta aapiji ge-mino-bimaadizigwen (whoever lives an exceedingly upright life — *dubitative participle*) mii yeta aw ge-odidang i’iw danakiiwin (that is the only one to reach that dwelling-place). Naske gida endasingin naniizaanisiwinan! (behold how many are the things to be feared! — *endasingin flagged*) Aapiji yeta meno-bimaadizid (only the one who lives exceedingly well) ayaa iwidi danakiiwining (is at yonder habitation). Gaawiin idash wiikaa niboosii (and never does he die). Mii wiin i’iw ezhi-debweyendang a’aw anishinaabe (this is what the person believes), mii apane gaaginiig ayaanid ojichaagwan (that forever, eternally, his soul continues there).</span>

*English footnote 3 (p.514→515), attaching to “upright life”: “The way to the spirit-world is beset with obstacles of various sorts. The ease or the difficulty of overcoming them depends on the character of the past life of the individual. If he has been careful in all religious observances, the passage of his soul will be easier than the soul of one who has not been so careful. It is conformity to religious observance that makes up an upright life. Ethics may or may not play a prominent part.” Restored 2026-09-02 from the page.*

<span class="l-orig">(6) Mạtcimạnitō ayā, pīg icini‘kāsuwitug, kīyōn kayä kānō kayä, tạnäs kayä. Ningī∙i∙gidạc nimicōmisiban: “Nōjis, kägu’ wī‘kā tacī‘kawā‘kän. Pitcīnạg kīgawâbạmāg, a‘tātiwinạg ta∙i∙cini‘kāsuwag; mī aᵘ wayābickīwät kätacī‘kawāt, kayä ‘iᵉi´ᵘ tci∙ō∙ci∙ā∙t.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Maji-manidoo ayaa, biig izhinikaazowidog, giyoon gaye gaano gaye, danes gaye. Ningii-igo idash nimishoomisiban: “Noozhis, gego wiikaa dazhi-gawa’aaken. Bijiinag giga-waabamaag, adaadiwinag da-izhinikaazowag; mii aw wayaabishkiwed ge-dazhi-gawaad, gaye i’iw ji-oozhi’aad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*An evil manitou exists; a spade must his name be, and a diamond, and a heart, and a club. Now, I was told by my grandfather who is no more: “My grandson, never have anything to do with them. After a while you shall see them, objects for gambling is what they shall be called; for that is what the white (people) shall occupy themselves with, and they shall make them.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Maji-manidoo ayaa (there is an evil manidoo), biig izhinikaazowidog (he must be named ‘Spade’ — *biig = Eng. ‘spade/pique’ loan), giyoon gaye (and ‘Diamond’ — *Eng. ‘queen’? carried as named in body; flagged*) gaano gaye (and ‘Heart’ — *Eng. ‘knave’? carried; flagged*), danes gaye (and ‘Club’ — *Eng. ‘deuce’? carried; flagged*). Ningii-igo idash nimishoomisiban (and I was told by my late grandfather): “Noozhis, gego wiikaa dazhi-gawa’aaken (“my grandson, never busy yourself with them — *dazhi-gawa’- ‘occupy oneself / strike at’, stem flagged*). Bijiinag giga-waabamaag (by and by you will see them), adaadiwinag da-izhinikaazowag (‘gambling-objects’ they will be called); mii aw wayaabishkiwed ge-dazhi-gawaad (it is the white one who will occupy himself with them), gaye i’iw ji-oozhi’aad (and so he will make them).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Mīdac īni‘ᵘ käki‘kino∙ạ∙māgut mạtcimạnitōn. Kīcpīn täbwä‘tawisiwạn äninān, kabäya∙ī∙ tacī‘kawạtwā, kīgawâbạmā ‘aᵉa´ᵘ mạtcimạnitō.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash iniw ge-gikino’amaagod maji-manidoon. Giishpin debwetawisiwan eninaan, gabe-ayi’ii dazhi-gawaadwaa, giga-waabamaa a’aw maji-manidoo.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon by the evil manitou shall one be taught. If you do not heed what I tell you then, if for a long time you occupy yourself with them, you shall see that evil manitou.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash iniw ge-gikino’amaagod maji-manidoon (and then he will be taught by the evil manidoo). Giishpin debwetawisiwan eninaan (if you do not believe what I tell you), gabe-ayi’ii dazhi-gawaadwaa (if for a long time you busy yourself with them), giga-waabamaa a’aw maji-manidoo.” (you will see that evil manidoo.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Nīnidạc Wâsāgunäckạnk ägōyān. Kägabäya∙ī∙ nintacī‘kawāg īgi´ᵘ a‘tātiwinạg. Nīcugun tacī‘kawạgwā mī∙i∙´ᵘ äjikīwạnātisiyān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Niin idash Waasaagoneshkang egooyaan. Ge-gabe-ayi’ii nindazhi-gawaag igiw adaadiwinag. Niizho-gon dazhi-gawagwaa mii i’iw ezhi-giiwanaadiziyaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, I myself am named He-that-leaves-the-Imprint-of-his-Foot-in-the-Snow-glistening. For long periods at a time do I occupy myself with those gambling-objects. If for two days I am busied with them, then I am out of my wits.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Niin idash Waasaagoneshkang egooyaan (now I myself am the one called Waasaagoneshkang). Ge-gabe-ayi’ii nindazhi-gawaag igiw adaadiwinag (for long stretches I busy myself with those gambling-objects). Niizho-gon dazhi-gawagwaa (when I occupy myself with them for two days) mii i’iw ezhi-giiwanaadiziyaan (then it is that I go out of my wits — giiwanaadizi ‘be deranged/crazed’).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Kayä nīn wäntcinibwā‘kāyān ‘aᵉa´ᵘ nōs mī∙o∙wä kāmāmawinigānisit. ‘Aᵉa´ᵘ nōs Wämbwäyāc, mī∙i∙‘ᵘ kā∙i∙cini‘kāsut. Mīdạc īgi‘ᵘ kā‘pī‘kitowāt: “Kägu’ wī‘kā nisā‘kägun weyāpickīwät. Kayägu iᵘ wī‘kā ninguting ki∙tcimīgātink tciwimīgānäk. Kīcpin mīgānint mīgu∙i∙ᵘ tcī∙i∙ckwā∙a∙nicinābä‘kāk.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaye niin wenji-nibwaakaayaan a’aw noos mii iwe gaa-maamawinigaanizid. A’aw noos Wembweyaazh, mii i’iw gaa-izhinikaazod. Miinawaa gii-oozisid noosiban, Zhezhoonginik gii-izhinikaazo nimishoomisiban. Miinawaa gii-oozisid Zhezhoonginik, Awaasigiwenzhi; gii-gichi-midewi. Awaasigiwenzhi Esiban gii-izhinimaawan oosan. <span class="l-eng">*Now, the reason why I am wise is that my father was the leader of all at this place. This father of mine (was) Snow-blown-by-the-Wind, that was what he was called. Furthermore, my father, who is no more, had a father; Big-Strong-Arm was the name of my grandfather who is no more. Again, Big-Strong-Arm had a father, (he was) Old-Bull-Head; he was versed in the mystic rite. Old-Bull-Head had a father by the name of Raccoon. And this is what they have handed down (one to another): “Never slay the white (people). And if ever in the future there is a big war, don’t fight them. If they are warred against, then that shall be the end of the people.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaye niin wenji-nibwaakaayaan a’aw noos (and the reason I am wise is my father) mii iwe gaa-maamawinigaanizid (he was the one who led all here — *maamawinigaanizi flagged*). A’aw noos Wembweyaazh (this father of mine was Snow-blown-by-the-Wind), mii i’iw gaa-izhinikaazod (that is what he was called). Miinawaa gii-oozisid noosiban (again, my late father had a father), Zhezhoonginik gii-izhinikaazo nimishoomisiban (Big-Strong-Arm my late grandfather was named). Miinawaa gii-oozisid Zhezhoonginik (again, Big-Strong-Arm had a father), Awaasigiwenzhi (Old-Bull-Head); gii-gichi-midewi (he was deeply versed in the Midewiwin — *gichi-midewi: the mystic rite*). Awaasigiwenzhi Esiban gii-izhinimaawan oosan (Old-Bull-Head’s father was named Raccoon). Mii dash igiw gaa-bi-gidowaad (and this is what they handed down): “Gego wiikaa nisaakegon weyaabishkiiwed (“never slay the white man). Gaye go iw wiikaa ningoding gichi-miigaading ji-wii-miigaaneg (and never, if some day there is a great war, are you to fight them). Giishpin miigaanind mii go i’iw ji-ishkwaa-anishinaabekaag.” (if they are fought, then that is the end of the people’s being.”)</span>
