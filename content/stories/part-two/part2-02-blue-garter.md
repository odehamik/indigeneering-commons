<span class="l-fiero">**WOZHAAWAASKOKASKITAASEPASON.**</span>

BLUE-GARTER.

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Mrs. Marie Syrette — Series I (Nos. 1–3), Fort William. Credited on the series heading, Part II p.3: “(Told by Mrs. Marie Syrette.)” — orchestrator crop `v2_p3_0` 2026-09-13; the given name and the page credit were missing from the earlier fill (Jones Part II Introduction p. ix).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1919).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ninguting kīwäⁿ aiyāwạg nīj ạpinōtcīⁿyạg; päjik kwīwisäns, päjik kwäsäns aiyāwạg.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoting giiwenh aiyaawag niizh abinoochiinyag*; bezhik gwiiwizens, bezhik kwesens aiyaawag.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Once upon a time, so the story goes, there lived two children; a boy (and) a girl there lived.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoting giiwenh (once, so the story goes) aiyaawag (there were) niizh abinoochiinyag* (two children); bezhik gwiiwizens (one a boy), bezhik kwesens (one a girl) aiyaawag (there were).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Mīdạc äckạm ạniminditōwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash eshkam ani-minditoowaat.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And larger they grew as time went on.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash (and then) eshkam (more and more) ani-minditoowaat (they grew bigger as time went on).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Mīdạc kīwäⁿ kwīwisäns nạntawäntcigät, nisāt wâbōzōn; kagä wīn dạc i‘kwäsäns, mạnisä tcībākwä kayä ändāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash giiwenh gwiiwizens nantawenchiget, nisaat waaboozoon; gegaa wiin dash ikwesens, manise chiibaakwe gaye endaawaat.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then, according to the story, the boy began to hunt for game (and) killed rabbits; and as for the girl, she gathered firewood and did the cooking at where they lived.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash giiwenh (and then, so the story goes) gwiiwizens (the boy) nantawenchiget (went hunting for game), nisaat (killing) waaboozoon (rabbits); gegaa wiin dash (and as for the other one) ikwesens (the girl), manise (she gathered firewood) chiibaakwe (and cooked) gaye endaawaat (there at where they lived).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Mīdạc kiwä äckạm ạniminditōwāt; aᵘ dac kīwäⁿ kwiwisäns äckạm ạniki‘tci∙u∙ckinawäwi, ki‘tci awäsīⁿyạn kayä onisān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash giwe eshkam ani-minditoowaat; aw dash giiwenh kwiwisens eshkam ani-gichi-oshkinawewi, gichi-awesiinyan gaye onisaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then, as the story goes, they continued getting larger; and then they say that the boy grew to be somewhat of a young man, and then big game he killed.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash giiwenh (and then, so the story goes) eshkam ani-minditoowaat (they kept growing bigger); aw dash giiwenh kwiwisens (and that boy, they say) eshkam ani-gichi-oshkinawewi (gradually became a grown young man), gichi-awesiinyan gaye onisaan (and big game animals he killed).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Äckạm ā‘pitci mino∙a∙iyāwạg kīwäⁿ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Eshkam aapichi mino-aiyaawag giiwenh.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*In a very much more comfortable way then they lived, so it is said.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Eshkam aapichi mino-aiyaawag giiwenh (more and more comfortably they lived, so it is said).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Nīngudingidac kīwäⁿ, aiyayāwād, udinān umisäⁿyạn:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Niingoding idash giiwenh, aiyayaawaad, odinaan omisenyan:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so once, according to the story, while they were yet living at the place, said (the youth) to his elder sister:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Niingoding idash giiwenh (and once, so the story goes), aiyayaawaad (while they were still living there), odinaan omisenyan (he said to his elder sister):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) “Nimisäⁿ! misa´ cayīgwa tcipa‘kädiyạnk.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Nimisenh! mi sa shayiigwa chi-pakediyank.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“O my elder sister! the time is soon at hand when we shall part from each other.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Nimisenh! (“my elder sister!) mi sa shayiigwa (now soon) chi-pakediyank.” (we shall part from each other.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) “Ānīn nạgwana, nicīm,” udigōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aaniin nangwana, nishiim,” odigoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Very well, so let it come, my little brother,” he was told.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aaniin nangwana, nishiim,” (“very well then, my little brother,”) odigoon (he was told).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) “Aiyāngwāmisînsa kayä kīn wī‘pîmādisuyạn!</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Aiyaangwaamisin sa gaye giin wii-pimaadisoyan!**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“And do be very careful of yourself if you wish to live!*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Aiyaangwaamisin sa gaye giin (“do be careful of yourself too) wii-pimaadisoyan! (if you want to live!)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Pōtc, inạngä mōjạg kīgawīdcīndimin.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Booch, inange moozhag giiga-wiijiindimin.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*However, it is impossible that we should always live together.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Booch, inange (however, of course) moozhag (always) giiga-wiijiindimin.” (we cannot keep living together.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Mīdạc kīwäⁿ aᵘ uskinawäⁿ kā∙i∙jimādcāt, mīnawā udigōn umisäⁿyạn:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash giiwenh aw oshkinawenh gaa-izhi-maajaat, miinawaa odigoon omisenyan:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon, so it is said, as the youth started to go, once more was he told by his elder sister:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash giiwenh (and so, they say) aw oshkinawenh (that youth) gaa-izhi-maajaat (as he set off), miinawaa odigoon omisenyan (again he was told by his elder sister):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) “Nicîm, mi‘kwänimicîn ninguting kīcpin kägō ā‘pitci sạnạgisiyạn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Nishim, mikwenimishin ningoting giishpin gegoo aapichi sanagisiyan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“My little brother, think of me if ever at any time you are in deep trouble over something.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Nishim (“my little brother), mikwenimishin ningoting (think of me sometime) giishpin gegoo aapichi sanagisiyan.” (if you are ever in deep trouble over something.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) “Äyeᵉ,” udinān, “nimisäⁿ.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Eye’,” odinaan, “nimisenh.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“I will, my elder sister,” he said to her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Eye’,” (“I will,”) odinaan, (he said to her,) “nimisenh.” (“my elder sister.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) Mīdạc mādcāt aᵘ uckinawä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash maajaat aw oshkinawe.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then went the youth away.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash maajaat aw oshkinawe (and then away went the youth).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) Ningutingidạc ạnipapimosät, ā‘pitci aiyä‘kusi.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoting idash ani-papimoset aapichi aiyekosi.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And once, as he was walking along, very weary he became,*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoting idash (and once), ani-papimoset (as he walked along), aapichi aiyekosi (he grew very weary),</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) Mīdạc änändạnk, “Mī omāⁿ tcinibāyān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash enendank, “Mii omaanh chi-nibaayaan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*whereupon he thought, “Now in this place will I sleep.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash enendank (whereupon he thought), “Mii omaanh chi-nibaayaan.” (“now here I will sleep.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) Mīdạc kägät imā gī∙a∙nipạgidînạnk upimiwạnānäns.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget imaa gii-ani-pagidinank opimiwanaanens.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so truly there laid he down his little pack.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget imaa (and so truly there) gii-ani-pagidinank (he laid down) opimiwanaanens (his little pack).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) Pạngīdac nīgān ạnī∙i∙jā, nạndawâbandạnk ānindi wâbạninig kädạni∙i∙jimādcāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Pangii dash niigaan anii-izhaa, nandawaabandank aanindi waabaninig ged-ani-izhi-maajaat.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now a little farther on he went, taking a look whither on the morrow he would go.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Pangii dash niigaan anii-izhaa (a little farther ahead he went), nandawaabandank (looking over) aanindi waabaninig ged-ani-izhi-maajaat (where on the morrow he would go).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(19) Wayībadac näyāp ajäkīwä kī∙a∙‘tōt ubimiwạnān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Wayiiba dash neyaap azhekiiwe gii-atoot obimiwanaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And in a little while back he came to where he had put his pack.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Wayiiba dash neyaap azhekiiwe (and in a little while back he returned) gii-atoot obimiwanaan (to where he had put his pack).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(20) Änitạgwicingidạc ōwâbandān āja apicimōni‘kātänik, kayä āca kī∙u∙citcikātänik tcîwīsinit tcîmibāt kayä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Eni-tagwishing idash oowaabandaan aazha apishimoonikaatenik, gaye aazha gii-oshichikaatenik chi-wiisinit chi-mibaat gaye.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when he was come, he saw that a bed had already been made, and that there was also prepared some food for him to eat, and a place for him to sleep.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Eni-tagwishing idash (and when he arrived) oowaabandaan (he saw) aazha apishimoonikaatenik (that a bed was already made), gaye aazha gii-oshichikaatenik (and that there was already prepared) chi-wiisinit (for him to eat) […chi-nibaat gaye (and for him to sleep).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(21) Aᵘ widac ckinawä mama‘kādäntạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aw widash shkinawe mamakaadendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon the youth wondered.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aw widash shkinawe mamakaadendam (and that youth was astonished).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(22) “Mīsa kanabạtc nimisäⁿ kā∙i∙citcigät,” inändạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii sa ganabach nimisenh gaa-ishichiget,” inendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Now, perhaps my elder sister did it,” he thought.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Mii sa ganabach nimisenh (“now, perhaps my elder sister) gaa-ishichiget,” (is the one who did it,”) inendam (he thought).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(23) Mīdạc äjimādcīwīsinit nibāt kayä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash ezhi-maajii-wiisinit nibaat gaye.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon he began to eat, and then he went to sleep.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash ezhi-maajii-wiisinit (thereupon he began to eat) nibaat gaye (and then he went to sleep).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(24) Wayābaninigidac mīnawā mādcā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Wayaabaninig idash miinawaa maajaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And on the following day he started on.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Wayaabaninig idash miinawaa maajaa (and on the next day again he set off).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(25) Mīnawā wänāgucininig mīgu mīnawā nāsāb äjiwäpisit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Miinawaa wenaagoshininig mii go miinawaa naasaab ezhiwepisit.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When again it came evening, once more the same thing happened to him.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Miinawaa wenaagoshininig (and again when evening came) mii go miinawaa naasaab ezhiwepisit (the same thing happened to him again).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(26) Wänāgucininig kīnibā mīnawā, mīdạc mīnawā mādcā wayābạninig.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Wenaagoshininig gii-nibaa miinawaa, mii dash miinawaa maajaa wayaabaninig.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*In the evening he went to sleep again, and then he went on when the morrow came again.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Wenaagoshininig gii-nibaa miinawaa (in the evening he slept again), mii dash miinawaa maajaa wayaabaninig (and then again he set off when morning came).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(27) Ningutingidạc kīwäⁿ ạnipapimusät, omi‘kān ā‘pitci unicicininik; minunāgwạtinig; ā‘pitcidạc kīsisōn sa‘kāsigänit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoting idash giiwenh ani-papimoset, omikaan aapichi onishishininik; minonaagwatinig; aapichi dash giisisoon sakaasigenit.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now once, so it is said, as he went walking along, he found a place that was very pleasing; it offered a beautiful view; and very bright did the sun shine there.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoting idash giiwenh (and once, so the story goes), ani-papimoset (as he walked along), omikaan aapichi onishishininik (he came upon a very beautiful place); minonaagwatinig (it looked good); aapichi dash giisisoon sakaasigenit (and very brightly the sun shone there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(28) Kīwawänạpi, inändạng:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gii-wawenapi, inendang:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He sat down (and) began thinking:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gii-wawenapi (he sat down), inendang (thinking):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(29) “Wāgutugwän kā∙u∙ndcinạganāwạgän nimisäⁿ?”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Waagotogwen kaa-onji-naganaawagen nimisenh?”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Why did I ever leave my elder sister?”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Waagotogwen kaa-onji-naganaawagen nimisenh?” (“why ever did I leave my elder sister?”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(30) Kạskändạnk; bä‘kic mägwādạc kīⁿwäⁿ nānagatawändạnk, pānimāgu udūntciki‘känimān ạwiya nāsi‘kāgut pi‘kwanāng ina‘ka‘käyā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Kashkendank; bekish megwaa dash giinwenh naanagatawendank, baanimaa go odoonchi-kikenimaan awiya naasikaagot bikwanaang inakakeyaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He was sad; at the same time, they say, while he was in deep thought, suddenly he felt that somebody was coming towards him from behind.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Kashkendank (he was sad); bekish megwaa dash giinwenh (and meanwhile, they say) naanagatawendank (as he was deep in thought), baanimaa go odoonchi-kikenimaan awiya (suddenly he sensed someone) naasikaagot bikwanaang inakakeyaa (coming toward him from behind).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(31) Mīdạc pikanōnikut:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash bi-kanoonikot:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And he was addressed by the one who came saying:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash bi-kanoonikot (and then he was addressed by the one who came):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(32) “Wägunän mā wäci‘tōyạn?</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Wegonen maa weshitooyan?**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“What are you doing there?*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Wegonen maa weshitooyan? (“what are you doing there?</span>

<span class="l-orig">(33) Wägunän wändcikitcinānạgatawäntạmạn, kayä kaskändạmạn?” udigōn īni´ᵘ; i‘kwäwan nạgwana īni´ᵘ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Wegonen wenji-gichi-naanagatawentaman, gaye gashkendaman?” odigoon iiniw; ikwewan nangwana iiniw.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*For what are you in such deep thought, and why are you sad?” (thus) was he told by that one; a woman did that one turn out to be.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Wegonen wenji-gichi-naanagatawentaman (why are you in such deep thought), gaye gashkendaman?” (and why are you sad?”) odigoon iiniw (he was told by that one); ikwewan nangwana iiniw (a woman that one turned out to be).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(34) Ā‘pitcidạc minwändạm aᵘ ckînawä wâbamāt awiya käkakanōnāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aapichi dash minwendam aw shkinawe waabamaat awiya ge-kakanoonaat.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And very pleased was the youth when he saw some one with whom to talk.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aapichi dash minwendam aw shkinawe (and very pleased was the youth) waabamaat awiya ge-kakanoonaat (when he saw someone to talk with).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(35) Mīdec kīwäⁿ äjikākīkitōwāt, pīnic tci∙u∙ji‘tōwāt sisāgi∙i∙tiwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii desh giiwenh ezhi-kaakiikitoowaat, biinish chi-ozhitoowaat sisaagi’itiwaat.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And thereupon, so it is said, they began to converse, keeping it up till they felt mutual love for each other.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii desh giiwenh (and then, so the story goes) ezhi-kaakiikitoowaat (they began to converse), biinish (until) chi-ozhitoowaat sisaagi’itiwaat (they came to love each other).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(36) Mīdec kägä‘t sāgi∙i∙tiwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii desh geget saagi’itiwaat.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And really did they love each other.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii desh geget saagi’itiwaat (and truly they loved each other).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(37) Mīdec aᵘ i‘kwä ä‘kidot:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii desh aw ikwe ekidot:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then the woman said:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii desh aw ikwe ekidot (and then the woman said):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(38) “Päcu īma nindāmin; kanạbạtc kâgā neguti tepā∙i∙gạn a‘pīnindāmin,” i‘kito aᵘ i‘kwä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Besho iima nindaamin; ganabach gegaa negoti depaa’igan apii nindaamin,” ikito aw ikwe.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Close by yonder place is our home; perhaps almost a measure is the distance where we live,” said the woman.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Besho iima nindaamin (“close by yonder is our home); ganabach gegaa negoti depaa’igan apii nindaamin,” (perhaps about a measure away is where we live,”) ikito aw ikwe (said the woman).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(39) Mīdec kīwäⁿ kayä wīn uckinawä äjikagwätcimāt tciwīdigämāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii desh giiwenh gaye wiin oshkinawe ezhi-kagwechimaat chi-wiidigemaat.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And they say that the youth also progressed so far as to ask her to be his wife.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii desh giiwenh gaye wiin oshkinawe (and then, they say, the youth too) ezhi-kagwechimaat chi-wiidigemaat (asked her to marry him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(40) Mīdec kīwäⁿ awä‘kwä äjina‘kwumāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii desh giiwenh aw ekwe ezhi-nakwomaat.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And they say that the woman gave him her consent.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii desh giiwenh aw ekwe ezhi-nakwomaat (and then, they say, the woman consented to him).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(41) “Äyeᵉ,” udinān, “mīgu iᵘ kā∙i∙jinạntutūnān tci∙i∙jikagwätcimiyạn.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Eye’,” odinaan, “mii go iw kaa-izhi-nantotoonaan chi-izhi-gagwetchimiyan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Yes,” she said to him, “that is precisely what I expected you would ask me.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Eye’,” (“yes,”) odinaan, (she said to him,) “mii go iw kaa-izhi-nantotoonaan (“that is just what I expected) chi-izhi-gagwetchimiyan.” (you would ask me.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(42) Mīdec kīⁿwäⁿ äjiwīndamawāt untci ōsạn ugīn kayä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii desh giinwenh ezhi-wiindamawaat onchi oosan ogiin gaye.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And then they say that she told him about her father and her mother.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii desh giinwenh ezhi-wiindamawaat (and then, they say, she told him) onchi oosan ogiin gaye (about her father and her mother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(43) Udinān:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Odinaan:**</span>
