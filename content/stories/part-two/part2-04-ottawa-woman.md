UDĀWĀ‘KWÄG.

<span class="l-fiero">**ODAAWAAKWE.**</span>

OTTAWA-WOMAN.

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Penesi — Series 2 (Nos. 4–43), Fort William (per Jones Part II Introduction p. ix; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1919).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ningudingsa kīnwän kī∙a∙i∙ndā udāwā‘kwä, niji‘käwizi.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding sa giiwen’ gii-ayindaa odaawaakwe, nizhikewizi.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, once on a time, it is said, there was an Ottawa-Woman; she was alone.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding sa giiwen’ (now, once it is said) gii-ayindaa odaawaakwe (there lived an Ottawa-Woman), nizhikewizi (she was alone).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Kāwīn ininiwạn udayāwāsīn ạnōdc kägō udōji‘tōn, a’picimunạn ka‘kina gägōn ka∙i∙jitcigäwād i‘kwäwạg, mạckimudạn kayä; mī ∙i∙’u äniwä‘k kāwin ā‘pidci kạckändạnzi nicikäwizit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin ininiwan odayaawaasiin anooj gegoo odoozhitoon, apishimonan gakina gegoon gaa-izhichigewaad ikwewag, mashkimodan gaye; mii iw eniweg gaawiin aapiji gashkendanzii nizhikewizid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*She did not have a husband, yet various things she made, - mats and all the things that women are wont to make, likewise bags; for that very reason was she not so very sad that she was alone.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin ininiwan odayaawaasiin (she did not have a husband) anooj gegoo odoozhitoon (yet she made all sorts of things), apishimonan (mats) gakina gegoon gaa-izhichigewaad ikwewag (all the things that women make), mashkimodan gaye (and bags too); mii iw eniweg (and for that reason) gaawiin aapiji gashkendanzii (she was not very sad) nizhikewizid (that she was alone).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Apa‘kwaiyạn gayä udōj∙i∙ān pạ‘kibōdcigä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apakwayan gaye odoozhi’aan bakiboojige.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Reed mats she also made, and she spun twine.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apakwayan gaye odoozhi’aan (reed mats she also made) bakiboojige (and she spun twine).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) Ningudingdạc ugi‘kändān abinōdcīyạn ayāwāt wīyawing.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding dash ogikendaan abinoojiiyan odayaawaad wiiyawing.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, once she felt that there was a babe within herself.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding dash ogikendaan (now once she felt) abinoojiiyan odayaawaad (that she had a child) wiiyawing (within her body).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) A‘pī ∙i∙dạc cayīgwa wānīgi∙ā∙wạsut, pînäwạn nî‘tạm kīnīgiwạn ga‘kina tạc ạnōdc päbāmisätcig ändạswäwānagiziwāt - pînäwạg ga‘kina gayä pinäsiwạg.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii dash zhayiigwa waa-niigi’aawasod, binewan nitam gii-niigiwaan gakina dash anooj bebaamisejig endaswewaanagiziwaad - binewag gaye binesiwag.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when the time came for her to be delivered, there was born first of all a ruffed grouse, and then all the various creatures of the air, as many as there were, - ruffed grouse and all the birds.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii dash zhayiigwa waa-niigi’aawasod (and when the time came for her to give birth), binewan nitam gii-niigiwaan (a ruffed grouse was born first) gakina dash anooj bebaamisejig (and then all the various flying creatures) endaswewaanagiziwaad (as many as there were) - binewag gaye binesiwag (ruffed grouse and all the birds).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Mīwạni’u kānīgi∙ā∙t ga‘kina ogī∙a∙ninạgạnigō, mīyä‘ta pînäwạn, kāwin ugīnạgạniguzīn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Miiw aniw gii-niigi’aad gakina ogii-ani-naganigoo, miiy eta binewan, gaawiin ogii-naganigoosiin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, by all those to whom she had given birth was she forsaken as fast as they came, save only by the ruffed grouse, by it was she not abandoned.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Miiw aniw gii-niigi’aad gakina (now all those she had given birth to) ogii-ani-naganigoo (forsook her one after another), miiy eta binewan (save only the ruffed grouse), gaawiin ogii-naganigoosiin (by it she was not abandoned).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) ‘Aga’u bînä gī‘kito:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**A’aw bine gii-kido:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*The Ruffed Grouse spoke, saying:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">A’aw bine gii-kido (the Ruffed Grouse spoke).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) “Kāwīn nīn wī‘kā ninganạgạnāsī waga’u kigạnān; a‘pạnägu mōnjạg pä’cu ningạtayā tibitci∙a∙yāgwän kigạnān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaawiin niin wiikaa ninga-naganaasii wa’aw giganaan; apane go moozhag besho ningad-ayaa dibi ji-ayaagwen giganaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Never will I leave this mother of ours; for always will I be near by, no matter at what place our mother may continue.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gaawiin niin wiikaa ninga-naganaasii (“never will I leave) wa’aw giganaan (this mother of ours); apane go moozhag besho ningad-ayaa (always will I be near by) dibi ji-ayaagwen giganaan (no matter where our mother may be).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Mīdạc ‘igi’u ā‘pidci wändciwângawizit ‘aga’u pînä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash i’iw aapiji wenji-waangawizid a’aw bine.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Such is the reason why so very gentle a ruffed grouse is.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash i’iw (and this is the reason) aapiji wenji-waangawizid (why so very gentle is) a’aw bine (the ruffed grouse).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Minawā ninguding ugi‘kändān abinōdcīyạn ayāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Miinawaa ningoding ogikendaan abinoojiiyan ayaawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Another time she felt that she was with child.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Miinawaa ningoding ogikendaan (another time she felt) abinoojiiyan ayaawaad (that she was with child).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) A‘pī ∙i∙dạc minawā wädcidcisänig tcînīgi∙ā∙wạsut, minawā ugīnigi∙ā∙n ga‘kina ändaswäwānạgisinit awänsīyạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii dash miinawaa wejijisenig ji-niigi’aawasod, miinawaa ogii-niigi’aan gakina endaswewaanagizinid awesiiyan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when the time was come again for her to be delivered, again she gave birth to all the game-folk, as many as there were.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii dash miinawaa wejijisenig ji-niigi’aawasod (and when the time came again for her to give birth), miinawaa ogii-niigi’aan (again she gave birth to) gakina endaswewaanagizinid awesiiyan (all the game-folk, as many as there were).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) Mīgō∙i∙’u mīnawā kā ∙ạ∙nījinạgạnigut ka‘kina, mīyä‘ta wâbōsōn, kāwīn uginạgạnigusīn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii go iw miinawaa gaa-ani-izhinaganigod gakina, miiy eta waaboozoon, gaawiin ogi--naganigoosiin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, by them all, too, was she forsaken as fast as they came, save only by the hare, she was not deserted by it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii go iw miinawaa gaa-ani-izhinaganigod gakina (now by them all she was forsaken in turn), miiy eta waaboozoon (save only the hare), gaawiin ogii-naganigoosiin (by it she was not deserted).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) Kī ∙i∙‘kitō ‘aga’u wâbōs:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gii-ikido a’aw waabooz:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Said the Hare:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gii-ikido a’aw waabooz (said the Hare).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) “Kāwīn wī‘kā nīn ningạngạnāsī waga’u kigạnān,” kī∙i∙‘kidō.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaawiin wiikaa niin ninga-naganaasii wa’aw giganaan,” gii-ikido.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Never will I leave this mother of ours,” he said.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gaawiin wiikaa niin ninga-naganaasii (“never will I leave) wa’aw giganaan,” (this mother of ours,”) gii-ikido (he said).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) “Mī∙o∙mān pạnä tcînạmạdabiyān”.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii omaa pane ji-namadabiyaan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Here in this place will I always sit.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Mii omaa pane ji-namadabiyaan.” (“here in this place will I always sit.”)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) Ạsîn idạc wâbōzunk ijināguzitug; mī∙i∙’u äjini’kādägwän ī∙i∙mān wâbōs nạmạdabit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Asin idash waaboozoong izhinaagozidog; mii iw ezhinikaadegwen i’imaa waabooz namadabid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*There was a rock, probably in the likeness of a hare; accordingly it may have been called by the name of A-Hare-that-is-seated-here.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Asin idash waaboozoong izhinaagozidog (there was a rock, probably in the likeness of a hare); mii iw ezhinikaadegwen (accordingly it may have been so named) i’imaa waabooz namadabid (A-Hare-that-is-seated-there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) Mōnjạg īgi’u anicinābäeg ugītîbādotānāwa ‘igi’u wâbōs nạmạdabit ijini‘kātäg.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Moozhag igiw anishinaabeg ogii-dibaadodaanaawaa i’iw waabooz namadabid izhinikaadeg.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Always have the people referred to what was called The-hare-that-is-seated.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Moozhag igiw anishinaabeg ogii-dibaadodaanaawaa (always have the people spoken of) i’iw waabooz namadabid izhinikaadeg (what was called The-hare-that-is-seated).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) Mī∙i∙dạc ‘aga’u wâbōs mōnjạg wändcipimādisiwād anicinābäeg; usāgi∙ā∙n īni’u ugīn, ‘aga’u wâbōs.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii idash a’aw waabooz moozhag wenji-bimaadiziwaad anishinaabeg; ozaagi’aan iniw ogiin, a’aw waabooz.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Therefore such is why the hare is always around where dwell the people; he loved his mother, the hare (did).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii idash a’aw waabooz moozhag wenji-bimaadiziwaad anishinaabeg (and this is why the hare is always about where the people live); ozaagi’aan iniw ogiin (he loved his mother), a’aw waabooz (the hare did).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(19) Mīdạc mīnawā kī∙a∙i∙ndāt ‘aga’u udāwā‘kwä.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash miinawaa gii-ayindad a’aw odaawaakwe.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so there continued Ottawa-Woman.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash miinawaa gii-ayindad (and so on lived) a’aw odaawaakwe (Ottawa-Woman).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(20) Ninguding mīnawā ugi‘kändān ayāwāt abinōndcīyan.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding miinawaa ogikendaan ayaawaad abinoojiiyan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Another time she felt that she was with child.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding miinawaa ogikendaan (another time she felt) ayaawaad abinoojiiyan (that she was with child).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(21) A‘pī mīnawā nāgi∙ā∙wạsut kīngōnyag ugīnīgi∙ā∙g, ga‘kinagu ändaswäwānạgisiwād kīngōnyag.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii miinawaa naagi’aawasod giigoonya’ ogii-niigi’aa’, gakina go endaswewaanagiziwaad giigoonyag.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*When the time was come for her to be delivered, to fishes gave she birth, to all kinds of fishes, as many as there were.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii miinawaa naagi’aawasod (when the time came for her to give birth) giigoonya’ ogii-niigi’aa’ (to fishes she gave birth), gakina go endaswewaanagiziwaad giigoonyag (to all kinds of fishes, as many as there were).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(22) Mīgu menawā gakina gī∙a∙ninạgạnigut unīdcānisag mīyä‘ta ạdi‘kamägwạn kāwīn ugīnạgạnigusīn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii go miinawaa gakina gii-ani’naganigod oniijaanisa’, miiy eta adikamegwan, gaawiin ogii-naganigoosiin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So again by all her children was she forsaken as fast as they came, save only by the whitefish, she was not left by it.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii go miinawaa gakina gii-ani-naganigod oniijaanisa’ (so again by all her children she was forsaken in turn), miiy eta adikamegwan (save only the whitefish), gaawiin ogii-naganigoosiin (by it she was not left).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(23) Mī∙i∙’u kā∙i∙‘kitot ‘aga’u adi‘kamäg:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii iw gaa-ikidod a’aw adikameg:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*For this was what Whitefish said:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii iw gaa-ikidod a’aw adikameg (for this was what the Whitefish said).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(24) “Kāwīn nīn wī‘kā ninganạgạnāsī ‘aga’u kîgạnān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaawiin niin wiikaa ninga-naganaasii a’aw giganaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Never will I leave this mother of ours.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gaawiin niin wiikaa ninga-naganaasii (“never will I leave) a’aw giganaan (this mother of ours).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(25) Ka‘kina miziwä a‘kīng tîbisāga∙i∙gạn tcî∙a∙yāgwän, pō‘tcîmān ningạtayā,” kī∙i∙’kidō.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gakina miziwe akiing dibi zaaga’igan ji-ayaagwen, boozh imaa ningad-ayaa,” gii-ikido.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*In every place upon earth wherever a lake may be, even there will I be,” he said.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gakina miziwe akiing (in every place upon earth) dibi zaaga’igan ji-ayaagwen (wherever a lake may be), boozh imaa ningad-ayaa (even there will I be),” gii-ikido (he said).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(26) Mīdạc iu kaega‘t äjiwäbạ‘k, miziwa ayāwāt kīngōnyạg ka‘kinagu sāga∙i∙gạnīng kīngōni‘kāwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash geget ezhiwebak, miziwe ayaawaad giigoonyag gakina go zaaga’iganing giigoonyikaawan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And that, sure enough, has come to pass, everywhere are there fishes, and in all the lakes are there fishes.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash geget ezhiwebak (and that, sure enough, has come to pass), miziwe ayaawaad giigoonyag (everywhere there are fishes) gakina go zaaga’iganing giigoonyikaawan (and in all the lakes there are fishes).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(27) Mīdạc minawā ạnōdc kägō inạnu‘kīgubạnän.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash miinawaa anooj gegoo inanokiigobanen.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon she took up her work again, making all sorts of things.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash miinawaa anooj gegoo (thereupon all sorts of things) inanokiigobanen (she set herself to working at again).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(28) ‘Aga’u odāwā‘kwä änāwindubạnän ku‘kumisinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**A’aw odaawaakwe enaawindobanen gokoomisinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Ottawa-Woman was the name that our grandmother was called.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">A’aw odaawaakwe enaawindobanen (Ottawa-Woman was she called) gokoomisinaan (our grandmother).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(29) Ānīc mī ä‘kosit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aaniish mii ekoozid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Well, that is as far as (the story) goes.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aaniish mii ekoozid (well, that is as far as the story goes).</span>
