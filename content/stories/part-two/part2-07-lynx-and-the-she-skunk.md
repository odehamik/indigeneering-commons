<span class="l-fiero">**BIZHIW ZHIGAAGOKWE GAYE.**</span>

LYNX AND THE SHE-SKUNK.

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by Penesi — Series 2 (Nos. 4–43), Fort William (per Jones Part II Introduction p. ix; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1919).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) Ninguding kīwä, kī∙ā∙indā pījyᵘ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding giiwenh gii-aayindaa bizhiw.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Once on a time, they say, there lived a Lynx.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding giiwenh (once on a time, they say) gii-aayindaa bizhiw (there lived a Lynx).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Jigāgu‘kwän uwīdigämān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigaagokwen owiidigemaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*A She-Skunk he had for a wife.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Zhigaagokwen owiidigemaan (a She-Skunk he had for a wife).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Mīdạc imāⁿ ändạcinạndawändcigät ‘aᵘ pījyᵘ; ạnōdcigägō oni‘tōn—wâbōzōn, pinäwạn, cīcībạn—wā∙ạ∙cạmāt wīwạn; nānīngutinūⁿ kayä āmi‘kwạn unỉsān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash imaanh endazhi-nandawenjiged aw bizhiw; anooji gegoo onitoon - waaboozoon, pinewan, zhiishiiban - waa-ashamaad wiiwan; naningodinong gaye amikwan onisaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*It was there that Lynx hunted for game; every kind of thing he killed, — rabbits, ruffed grouse, ducks, — what he wanted to feed his wife on; sometimes a beaver too he killed.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash imaa (it was there) endazhi-nandawenjiged aw bizhiw (that this Lynx hunted for game); anooji gegoo onitoon (every kind of thing he killed) — waaboozoon, pinewan, zhiishiiban (rabbits, ruffed grouse, ducks) — waa-ashamaad (whatever he wanted to feed).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) A‘pī∙i∙dạc änipibōnk onōdci∙ā∙n ami‘kwạn ā‘pidci wīninūwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Api idash eni-biboong onooji’aan amikwan aapiji wiininowan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when winter was coming on, he obtained some beavers that were very fat.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Api idash eni-biboong (and when winter was coming on) onooji’aan amikwan (he obtained some beavers) aapiji wiininowan (that were very fat).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Ā‘pidci∙u∙misawänimān wī∙ạ∙mwāt wīwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aapiji omisawenimaan wii-amwaad wiiwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He very much desired to eat his wife.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aapiji omisawenimaan (he very much desired) wii-amwaad wiiwan (to eat his wife).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Kāwīn ugīnisāsīn ami‘kwạn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaawiin ogii-nisaasiin amikwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He had not killed a beaver (for some time).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaawiin ogii-nisaasiin amikwan (he had not killed a beaver for some time).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Ningudingidạc udinān wīwạn:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding idash odinaan wiiwan:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So once he said to his wife:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding idash odinaan wiiwan (so once he said to his wife):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) “Kidānipā kuca‘ku kājiga‘kin,” udinān.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gidaa-nibaa gosha go gaazhigakin,” odinaan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“You should really sometimes sleep during the day,” he said to her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Gidaa-nibaa gosha go gaazhigakin (you should really sometimes sleep during the day),” odinaan (he said to her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) ‘Aᵉa’widac i‘kwä ugi‘känimān wīnisigut unābäman.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**A’aw idash ikwe ogikenimaan wii-nisigod onaabeman.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, the woman knew that it was the desire of her husband to kill her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">A’aw idash ikwe (now, the woman) ogikenimaan wii-nisigod onaabeman (knew that it was the desire of her husband to kill her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Kīmūdc ubināsi‘kān ändāwāt nạndawâbạmād wīwạn tcinỉbānit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Giimooj obi-naazikaan endaawaad nandawaabamaad wiiwan ji-nibaanid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*In secret would he approach where they lived, to see if his wife was asleep.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Giimooj obi-naazikaan endaawaad (in secret would he approach where they lived) nandawaabamaad wiiwan ji-nibaanid (to see if his wife was asleep).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(11) Ningudingidạc kājiga‘k nibinādit ‘aᵉa’ᵘ i‘kwä, owābạmān unābämạn pạbāmusänit, ạgāming pạbāmusänit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ningoding idash gaazhigak nibinaadid a’aw ikwe, owaabamaan onaabeman babaamosenid, agaaming babaamosenid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, once in the day-time, when the woman went to get some water, she saw her husband walking from place to place, roundabout on the other shore was he walking.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ningoding idash gaazhigak (now, once in the day-time) nibinaadid a’aw ikwe (when the woman went to get some water), owaabamaan onaabeman babaamosenid (she saw her husband walking from place to place), agaaming babaamosenid (walking about along the shore).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(12) Awidạc i‘kwä āmi‘kwạn kīmōckamowạn imāⁿ unda∙i∙bāning.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aw idash ikwe amikwan gii-mooshkamowan imaanh onda’ibaaning.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And while the woman (was there), a beaver came up out of the water at the hole in the ice.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aw idash ikwe (and while the woman was there) amikwan gii-mooshkamowan (a beaver came up out of the water) imaa onda’ibaaning (there at the water-hole in the ice).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(13) Ugīnawadinān uzidāning īni‘ᵘ āmi‘kwạn, umindciminān, mī∙i∙‘ᵘ äjipīpāgimād unābämạn:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ogii-nawadinaan ozidaaning iniw amikwan, ominjiminaan, mii iw ezhi-biibaagimaad onaabeman:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*She seized the beaver by the feet, she held it tight, and then she called aloud to her husband:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ogii-nawadinaan ozidaaning iniw amikwan (she seized the beaver by the feet), ominjiminaan (she held it tight), mii iw ezhi-biibaagimaad onaabeman (and then she called aloud to her husband):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(14) “Pījē’ᵘ, undās! ami‘kwa awä! nimindciminā!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Biizhiw, ondaas! amikwa awe! minimjiminaa!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“O Lynx, hither! Here is a beaver! I have hold of him!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Biizhiw, ondaas! (O Lynx, hither!) amikwa awe! (here is a beaver!) minimjiminaa! (I have hold of him!)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(15) Pījyᵘ idạc pīnābi.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Bizhiw idash bi-inaabi.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, Lynx looked over to where she was.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Bizhiw idash bi-inaabi (now, Lynx looked over to where she was).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(16) “Pạgidin mägwā ninōdci∙ā∙!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Bagidin megwaa ninooji’aa!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Let it go, for I am hunting it!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Bagidin (let it go) megwaa ninooji’aa! (for I am hunting it!)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(17) Mīdạc kā∙i∙jipạgidināt, kī‘kīwä ‘aᵉa’ᵘ i‘kwä ändāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-bagidinaad gii-giiwe a’aw ikwe endaawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And so, when she let it go, back went the woman to where they lived.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-bagidinaad (and so, when she let it go) gii-giiwe a’aw ikwe endaawaad (back went the woman to where they lived).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(18) Ogī∙a∙‘tōnạn mỉsạn gayädạc ạsỉnīn ogī∙ạ∙sān i∙i∙māⁿ tcostỉgwâni‘pạn; ogipạdạgwana∙ā∙n idạc.¹</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ogii-atoonan misan gaye dash asiniin ogii-asaan imaanh ch-oshtigwaaniban; ogii-badagwana’aan idash.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*She put some fire-wood in place, and a stone she laid there for the head; and she covered up (the figure).*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Ogii-atoonan misan (she put some fire-wood in place) gaye dash asiniin ogii-asaan imaa ch-oshtigwaaniban (and a stone she laid there for the head); ogii-badagwana’aan idash (and she covered up the figure).</span>

*¹ This is not clear. The woman made a figure of some one asleep in order to deceive her husband, and cause him to think that it was she.*

<span class="l-orig">(19) Mē∙i∙dac kā∙i∙jikāzut pīndik ändāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii idash gaa-izhi-gaazod biindig endaawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon she hid inside of where they lived.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii idash gaa-izhi-gaazod biindig endaawaad (thereupon she hid inside of where they lived).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(20) A‘pidạc pījyᵘ pạdagwicing pä‘ka ubināsi‘kān ändāwāt.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Apii dash ba-dagwishing bekaa obi-naazikaan endaawaad.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when Lynx was arriving home, softly he approached where they lived.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Apii dash ba-dagwishing (and when Lynx was arriving home) bekaa obi-naazikaan endaawaad (softly he approached where they lived).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(21) Mīdạc wâbạmād awīya nỉbānit, mīdạc udäckạn pada‘kunạng.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash waabamaad awiiya nibaanid, mii dash odeshkan ba-dakonang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when he saw some one asleep, he then took his horn (chisel) up in his hand.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash waabamaad awiiya nibaanid (and when he saw some one asleep), mii dash odeshkan ba-dakonang (he then took his horn (chisel) up in his hand).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(22) Mī∙i∙dạc äjipajibaⁿwāt īni‘ᵘ asỉnīn mī∙i∙dạc kīki‘kändạnk asỉnīn pajibaⁿwāt, misạn gayä a‘tänig i∙i∙māⁿ.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash ezhi-bazhibawaad iniw asiniin mii dash gii-gikendang asiniin bazhibawaad, misan gaye atenig imaanh.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when he stabbed the stone, he then learned that it was a stone he had stabbed, and that some fire-wood too was there.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash ezhi-bazhibawaad iniw asiniin (and when he stabbed the stone), mii dash gii-gikendang asiniin bazhibawaad (he then learned that it was a stone he had stabbed), misan gaye atenig imaa (and that some fire-wood too was there).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(23) Mēdạc kā∙i∙jinạndawābạmād wiwạn tibikā∙i∙jānigwän; ạgwạdcīng kayä miziwä kīnạndawābạndcigät, kāwin ogīmi‘kawāsin.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-nandawaabamaad wiiwan dibi gaa-izhaanigwen; agwajiing gaye miziwe gii-nandawaabanjiged, gaawiin ogii-mikawaasiin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon he sought for his wife, who had gone he knew not where; and everywhere out of doors he sought, but he did not find her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-nandawaabamaad wiiwan (thereupon he sought for his wife) dibi gaa-izhaanigwen (who had gone he knew not where); agwajiing gaye miziwe gii-nandawaabanjiged (and everywhere out of doors he sought), gaawiin ogii-mikawaasiin (but he did not find her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(24) Kī‘pīndigādạc.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gii-biindige dash.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then he went inside.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gii-biindige dash (then he went inside).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(25) “Anōdc ningạtijictcigä; māgijā tabā‘pi tibikāzogwän.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Anooj ningad-izhichige; maagizhaa da-baapi dibi gaazogwen.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“All sorts of capers will I do; perhaps she will laugh, wherever she may be concealed.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Anooj ningad-izhichige (all sorts of capers will I do); maagizhaa da-baapi (perhaps she will laugh) dibi gaazogwen (wherever she may be concealed).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(26) Mīdạc kā‘tōdạnk:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-doodang:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, this was what he did:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-doodang (now, this was what he did):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(27) ogisāgisitōn udininīwiwin; ugīma‘katäwinān una‘kwayaī.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**ogii-zaagisitoon odininiiwiwin; ogii-makadewinaan onakwaya’ii.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*he stuck out his manhood; he blackened it at the end.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">ogii-zaagisitoon odininiiwiwin (he stuck out his manhood); ogii-makadewinaan onakwaya’ii (he blackened it at the end).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(28) “Cigāguskwä ugawīpä‘pitōn udaya∙ī∙m tibi∙a∙yāgwän, änạdō, änạdō, änạdō, änạdō!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Zhigaagokwe oga-wii-baapitoon odaya’iim dibi ayaagwen, endoo, endoo, endoo, endoo!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Let She-Skunk laugh at what is her own, wherever she may be, where, where, where, where!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Zhigaagokwe oga-wii-baapitoon odaya’iim (let She-Skunk laugh at what is her own) dibi ayaagwen (wherever she may be), endoo, endoo, endoo, endoo! (where, where, where, where! — vocable refrain, carried verbatim)”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(29) Awidac i‘kwä käga‘pī kipä‘pi kipä‘pi kạnawâbạmād ändodạminit.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aw idash ikwe gegapii gii-baapi ganawaabamaad endodaminid.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Now, the woman at last did laugh when she observed what he was doing.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aw idash ikwe gegapii gii-baapi (now, the woman at last did laugh) ganawaabamaad endodaminid (when she observed what he was doing).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(30) Nwāndawāt pā‘pinit wīwạn, kīsāgidcisä, kī∙ạ∙ndawâbạdcigä kīwi‘taiya∙ī∙ ändāwāt; kāwin ogīmi‘kawāsin.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Nwaandawaad baapinid wiiwan, gii-zaagijise, gii-andawaabanjige giiwitaya’ii endaawaad; gaawiin ogii-mikawaasiin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*On hearing the sound of his wife laughing, he rushed out of doors, he went seeking everywhere roundabout where they dwelt; but he did not find her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Nwaandawaad baapinid wiiwan (on hearing the sound of his wife laughing), gii-zaagijise (he rushed out of doors), gii-andawaabanjige giiwitaya’ii endaawaad (he went seeking everywhere roundabout where they dwelt); gaawiin ogii-mikawaasiin (but he did not find her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(31) Mīnawā gīpīndigä; pīnic nỉsing ī∙i∙‘ᵘ kītōdạm, ānawimīnawā kī‘pā‘piwan.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Miinawaa gii-biindige; biinish nising i’iw gii-doodam, aanawi miinawaa gii-bapiwan.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Back within he returned; even a third time he did it, still again she would laugh.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Miinawaa gii-biindige (back within he returned); biinish nising i’iw gii-doodam (even a third time he did it), aanawi miinawaa gii-bapiwan (still again she would laugh).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(32) Gāga‘pī∙i∙dạc kītäbā‘pi ‘aᵉa’ᵘ i‘kwä, kāwīn mīnawā kīpā‘pisī.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gegapii idash gii-de-baapi a’aw ikwe, gaawiin miinawaa gii-baapisii.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So at length the woman had her fill of laughing, no more did she laugh.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gegapii idash gii-de-baapi a’aw ikwe (so at length the woman had her fill of laughing), gaawiin miinawaa gii-baapisii (no more did she laugh).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(33) Pījiwidạc kī∙ā∙nawändcigä indawā.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Bizhiw idash gii-aanawenjige indawaa.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And Lynx ceased his merriment in consequence.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Bizhiw idash gii-aanawenjige indawaa (and Lynx ceased his merriment in consequence).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(34) Ānawi ogạnawâbạmigōn wīwạn, kāwīn dạc win owâbạmāsin.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Aanawi oganawaabamigoon wiiwan, gaawiin dash wiin owaabamaasiin.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Even though he was observed by his wife, yet he himself did not see her.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Aanawi oganawaabamigoon wiiwan (even though he was observed by his wife), gaawiin dash wiin owaabamaasiin (yet he himself did not see her).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(35) Mīdạc kā∙i∙jikāwicimat tcīgiskudä, ‘aᵉa’ᵘ pījyᵘ odạgozitōn u‘kāt ogidigwānk.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-gawishimod jiig-ishikode, a’aw bizhiw odagoozitoon okaad ogidigwaang.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And when he lay down to sleep beside the fire, Lynx hung one leg over the other knee.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-gawishimod jiig-ishikode (and when he lay down to sleep beside the fire), a’aw bizhiw odagoozitoon okaad ogidigwaang (Lynx hung one leg over the other knee).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(36) Mīdạc nānājinạnk unāzidi, mēdạc kā∙i∙‘kidot:</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash naanaazhinag onaazidi, mii dahs gaa-ikidod:**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Accordingly, as he rubbed the under side of his hip, this was what he said:*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash naanaazhinag onaazidi (accordingly, as he rubbed the under side of his hip), mii dahs gaa-ikidod (this was what he said):</span>

<span class="l-orig">(37) “Nindābā‘pi∙i∙gō māwin Nängawi sāga∙i∙gạning nīmi∙i∙ding nänäyāskināzidiyägōtcinān.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Nindaa-baapii’igoo maawiin Nengaawi-zaaga’iganiing niimi’iding neneyaashkinaazidiyegoojinaan.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“I should doubtless be made fun of if I were at the dance at Sandy Lake with the hair on my rump hanging down.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">“Nindaa-baapii’igoo maawiin (I should doubtless be made fun of) Nengaawi-zaaga’iganiing niimi’iding (if I were at the dance at Sandy Lake) neneyaashkinaazidiyegoojinaan (with the hair on my rump hanging down).”</span>

<span class="l-orig">(38) Mēdạc kā∙i∙ji∙u∙dā‘pinạng mō‘komān ugīmānijānidạc ubwām.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-odaapinang mookomaan ogii-maanizhaan idash obwaam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Whereupon, picking up a knife, he sliced a piece off his ham.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-odaapinang mookomaan (whereupon, picking up a knife), ogii-maanizhaan idash obwaam (he sliced a piece off his ham).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(39) Kā∙i∙jita‘kunạngidạc, “Nīngamīdcỉn,” inändạm.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaa-izhi-dakonand idash, “Ninga-miijiin,” inendam.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then taking up (a piece) in his hand, “I will eat it,” he thought.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Gaa-izhi-dakonand idash (then taking up a piece in his hand), “Ninga-miijiin (I will eat it),” inendam (he thought).</span>

<span class="l-orig">(40) Mēdạc kā∙i∙ji∙ạ∙bwät.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gaa-izhi-abwed.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon he roasted it upon a spit.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Mii dash gaa-izhi-abwed (thereupon he roasted it upon a spit).</span>
