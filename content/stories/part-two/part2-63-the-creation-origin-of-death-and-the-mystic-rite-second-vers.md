63. THE CREATION, ORIGIN OF DEATH, AND THE MYSTIC RITE. (Second Version.)

<span class="l-eng">*Narrated by either Wāsāgunäckạnk, or Midāsugaⁿj, or both (the roster’s own wording) — Series 4 (Nos. 62–78), Bois Fort (per Jones Part II Introduction p. ix; not credited on the story page itself).*</span>

<span class="l-fiero">***from Ojibwa Texts collected by William Jones (1919).***</span>

<span class="l-orig">(1) “Mīsa´, nītci, ‘iᵉi´ᵘ kä∙i∙ci∙ō∙ci‘tōyạnk kä∙i∙ciwäbạk mini‘k kā∙a∙‘kīwạnk; ga‘kina kägō ka∙ō∙ci‘tōmin. Kayä kīnawākin käkä‘k! —kayä kin, kōkō‘ko‘∙ō∙!—nigik! kayä kīn,—kīwīyābạtci∙i∙ninim tcimidäwit aᵘ pitcīnạg wâ∙u∙ci∙a∙‘k; ạnicinābä ta∙i∙cini‘kāsu.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii sa, niijii, i’iw ge-izhi-oozhitooyang* ge-izhiwebak minik gaa-akiiwang*; gakina gegoo ga-oozhitoomin. Gaye giinawaakin gekek! —gaye giin, gookooko’oo!—nigig! gaye giin,—giwiiyaabaji-ininim ji-midewid a’aw bichiinag waa-oozhi’ak; anishinaabe da-izhinikaaso.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Therefore, my friend, we are now to create what shall exist as long as the world lasts; everything now do we make. And you—you, O Bird-Hawk!—and you, O Owl!—O Otter! likewise you,——— I desire the help (that you can give), to the end that they whom I shall soon create may perform the mysteries; human beings is the name they shall be called.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">mii sa (therefore), niijii (my-friend), i’iw ge-izhi-oozhitooyang* (that-which we-shall-make), ge-izhiwebak minik gaa-akiiwang* (as-long-as the-world-lasts), gakina gegoo ga-oozhitoomin (everything we-make), gaye giinawaakin (and you (esp.)), gekek (Bird-Hawk(name)), gookooko’oo (Owl(name)), nigig (Otter(name)), giwiiyaabaji-ininim (I-make-use-of-you (call-on-you)), ji-midewid (that-(he)-perform-the-mide), bichiinag waa-oozhi’ak (whom I-shall-soon-make), anishinaabe da-izhinikaaso (person he-shall-be-named.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(2) Mīdạc kī∙ạ∙ntumāt ucīmäyạn. Cigwa dạc kā‘tạgwicininit kā∙i∙jikạnōnāt: “Mīsa cigwa tci∙ō∙ci∙ạ∙nk pa‘kān awīya kämạnitō‘kät ‘aᵉa´ᵘ mạnidō. Kīnawintidạc kīga nīgānisimin.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii dash gii-andomaad oshiimeyan. Zhigwa dash gaa-dagwishininid gaa-izhi-ganoonaad: “Mii sa zhigwa ji-oozhi’ang bakaan awiya ge-manidooked a’aw manidoo. Giinawind idash giga-niigaanizimin.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Accordingly he summoned his younger brother. And as soon as (his younger brother) had come, he then spoke to him, saying: “The time is now at hand for us to create another being who shall perform the miracles of a manitou. And you and I shall occupy leading place.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">mii dash gii-andomaad oshiimeyan (then he-summoned his-younger-brother(obv)), zhigwa dash gaa-dagwishininid (when he-arrived), gaa-izhi-ganoonaad (he-spoke-to-him), mii sa zhigwa ji-oozhi’ang (now we-shall-make), bakaan awiya (another being), ge-manidooked (who-shall-do-manitou-deeds), a’aw manidoo (the), manidoo; giinawind idash giga-niigaanizimin (and-we shall-lead.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(3) Kā∙i∙jimādci‘tāwāt kī∙u∙ci∙ā∙wāt īni´ᵘ mạnidōn.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaa-izhi-maajitaawaad gii-oozhi’aawaad iniw manidoon.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*After launching forth upon their work, they created the manitous.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">gaa-izhi-maajitaawaad (when-they-set-to-work), gii-oozhi’aawaad iniw manidoon (they-made the manidoo(obv/pl).)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(4) “Kāwīn ‘iᵉi´ᵘ wī‘kā ogawâbạmāsin aᵉa´ᵘ pitcīnạg kä∙u∙ci∙ạ∙nk.” Cigwa dạc kīmādci‘tāwạg kī∙u∙ci∙ā∙wāt mạnidōn; ändasōntānimạk upạgidināwān īni´ᵘ mạnidōn; kayä ạnāma‘kạmig kayä kīcigunk. Mīdạc ‘iᵉi´ᵘ pitcīnạg kīmi‘kwänimāwāt īni´ᵘ ạnicinābän. Nänabucu dạc kīsāga∙ạ∙m, wâsa kī∙i∙cāt. A‘kidạc ugīmạmōn, kī∙u∙ci∙ā∙t ạnicinābän. Gägä‘t ogīkacki‘tōn ‘iᵉi´ᵘ kī∙u∙ci∙ā∙t īni´ᵘ ininiwạn. Kā∙i∙cikạnōnāt: “Mī ōmā ayān. Pāmā wâbạnk kīgapi∙ạ∙ntawâbạmigō.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Gaawiin i’iw wiikaa oga-waabamaasiin a’aw bichiinag ge-oozhi’ang.” Zhigwa dash gii-maajitaawag gii-oozhi’aawaad manidoon; endaso-noondinimak obagidinaawaan iniw manidoon; gaye anaamakamig gaye giizhigong. Mii dash i’iw bichiinag gii-mikwenimaawaad iniw anishinaaben. Naanabozho dash gii-zaaga’am, waasa gii-izhaad. Akii dash ogii-mamoon, gii-oozhi’aad anishinaaben. Geget ogii-gashkitoon i’iw gii-oozhi’aad iniw ininiwan. Gaa-izhi-ganoonaad: “Mii omaa ayaan. Baamaa waabang giga-bi-andawaabamigoo*.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Never shall they whom we are to create see these (manitous).” And soon were they started upon the work of creating manitous; in every direction from whence blows the wind they placed the manitous; likewise beneath the earth and up in the sky. And then afterwards were they mindful of the human being. Now, Nänabushu went out of doors, afar he went. And some earth he took, using it to create the human being. Truly did he accomplish the work of creating a man. Speaking to him, he said: “In this place do you remain. Not till on the morrow shall we come to seek for you.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">gaawiin i’iw wiikaa oga-waabamaasiin (never shall-they-see-(them)), a’aw bichiinag ge-oozhi’ang (the-ones we-shall-soon-make), endaso-noondinimak (in-every-direction-the-wind-blows), obagidinaawaan iniw manidoon (they-placed the-manidoo(obv)), gaye anaamakamig gaye giizhigong (both under-the-earth and in-the-sky), gii-mikwenimaawaad iniw anishinaaben (they-thought-of the-person), gii-zaaga’am (he-went-out), waasa gii-izhaad (far he-went), akii dash ogii-mamoon (and-earth he-took), gii-oozhi’aad anishinaaben (he-fashioned a-person), Mii omaa ayaan (here remain), baamaa waabang giga-bi-andawaabamigoo* (not-till tomorrow we-shall-come-seek-you.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(5) Cigwa dạc kītibi‘kạdini; wayâbaninik icā īni´ᵘ ininiwạn, awänibạn. Mīsa´ kīkimūtimint. Kägä‘t māma‘kātāndam. A‘kawä kīwä. “Mīsa iᵉi´ᵘ kī‘kimōtimigōyạnk ‘aᵉa´ᵘ inini.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Zhigwa dash gii-dibikadini; wayaabaninig izhaa iniw ininiwan, awaniban. Mii sa gii-gimoodimind. Geget maamakaadendam. Akawe giiwe. “Mii sa i’iw gii-gimoodimigooyang a’aw inini.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*And now the night was coming on; on the morrow he went over to the man, (but he was) gone. It was that (the man) had been stolen from him. Truly did he marvel. First (before doing anything else) he went back home. “Therefore now have we been robbed of the man.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">zhigwa dash gii-dibikadini (now it-became-night), wayaabaninig izhaa iniw ininiwan (at-dawn he-went to-the-man), awaniban (he-was-gone), mii sa gii-gimoodimind (he-had-been-stolen), geget maamakaadendam (truly he-marvelled), akawe giiwe (first he-went-home), gii-gimoodimigooyang a’aw inini (we-have-been-robbed-of the man.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(6) Ugạnōnigōn ucīmäyạn: “Ānīc, mīnawā tci∙ō∙ci∙ạ∙t aᵉa´ᵘ inini. Kāwīn kuca kibwā∙ā∙nawi‘tōsīn kägō wâ∙i∙cictcigäyạnin.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Oganoonigoon oshiimeyan: “Aaniish, miinawaa ji-oozhi’ad a’aw inini. Gaawiin gosha gibwaa-aanawitoosiin gegoo waa-izhichigeyanin.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*He was addressed by his younger brother saying: “Why, you should make another man. You surely could not fail in accomplishing whatsoever you might wish to do.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">oganoonigoon oshiimeyan (he-was-addressed-by his-younger-brother), aaniish (why), miinawaa ji-oozhi’ad a’aw inini (again you-should-make the man), gaawiin gosha gibwaa-aanawitoosiin (you-surely-could-not-fail), gegoo waa-izhichigeyanin (at-whatever you-wish-to-do.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(7) Nänabucu dạc cigwa äjimādcāt. Mīnawā äci∙ō∙ci∙ā∙t īni´ᵘ ininiwạn. Kā‘kīci∙ā∙t: “Mī∙o∙mā ạyān. Pāmā wâbạnk kabi∙ạ∙ntawâbạmigō.” Kāwâbaninig, cigwa ogạnōnān ucīmäyạn: “Ạmbäsa, awi∙ạ∙ntawâbamātā ‘aᵉa´ᵘ inini!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Naanabozho dash zhigwa ezhi-maajaad. Miinawaa ezhi-oozhi’ad iniw ininiwan. Gaa-giizhi’ad: “Mii omaa ayaan. Baamaa waabang ga-bi-andawaabamigoo.” Gaa-waabaninig, zhigwa oganoonaan oshiimeyan: “Ambe sa, awi-andawaabamaadaa a’aw inini!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*So Nänabushu then departed. Another man did he then create. After creating him, he said to him: “In this place do you remain. Not till on the morrow shall we come to seek for you.” After the morrow was come, he then addressed his younger brother, saying: “Behold, let us go look for the man!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">Naanabozho dash zhigwa ezhi-maajaad (then he-departed), miinawaa ezhi-oozhi’ad iniw ininiwan (again he-made the man), gaa-giizhi’ad (when-he-finished-(him)), mii omaa ayaan (here remain), baamaa waabang ga-bi-andawaabamigoo (not-till tomorrow we-come-seek-you), gaa-waabaninig (when-morning-came), ambe sa, awi-andawaabamaadaa a’aw inini (come, let-us-go-look-for the man.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(8) Äjimādcāwāt ucīmäyạn; änitäbābantạmowāt i∙i∙mā ayāni‘pạn, awäniban. Kägä‘t māma‘kāda‘kạmig inändạm. “Kägō kanabatc kiticiwani‘tcikämin. Ạmbäsanō, mī∙i∙´ᵘ icikīwätā!”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Ezhi-maajaawaad oshiimeyan; eni-debaabandamowaad iimaa ayaaniban, awaniban. Geget maamakaadakamig inendam. “Gegoo ganabaj gii-dizhi-wanichigemin. Ambe sa noo, mii i’iw izhi-giiwedaa!”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Then departed he and his younger brother; on coming in sight of the place where (the man) had been, (they saw that he was) gone. It was truly marvellous, (his younger brother) thought. “In some sort of way we may perhaps have made a mistake. Pray, therefore, let us go back home!”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">ezhi-maajaawaad oshiimeyan (he-set-out (with) his-younger-brother), eni-debaabandamowaad (when-they-came-in-sight), iimaa ayaaniban (where-(he)-had-been), awaniban (he-was-gone), geget maamakaadakamig inendam (truly it-is-marvellous he-thinks), gegoo ganabaj gii-dizhi-wanichigemin (somehow perhaps we-made-a-mistake), ambe sa noo (pray, then), mii i’iw izhi-giiwedaa (let-us-go-home.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(9) Kä‘pīndigäwāt, mīsa´ cigwa kanōnigut īni´ᵘ ucīmäyạn: “Anin kä∙i∙ci∙ạ∙nicinābä‘kāk ubanän ‘iᵉi´ᵘ tciwạnicinugwän ānu∙a∙yātcin ‘aᵉa´ᵘ ạnicinābä? Tạga, ạntumi‘twäntạn kä∙i∙cictcigäyạng ‘iᵉi´ᵘ tci∙ạ∙nicinābä‘kāg omā.” Nänabucu dạc ugīmi‘kwäntān wâ∙i∙cictcigät. “Ạmbäsa, ninga∙u∙ci∙ā∙g īgi´ᵘ kägō mämindagä tcimi‘kwändāgusiwāt. Ạmbäsa, pinäsiwạg ta∙i∙nāwạg, kayä ạnimi‘kīg ta∙i∙nāwạg.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Gaa-biindigewaad, mii sa zhigwa ganoonigod iniw oshiimeyan: “Aaniin ge-izhi-anishinaabekaag obanen i’iw ji-waniizhinigwen* aano-ayaadin* a’aw anishinaabe? Daga, andomitwendan ge-izhichigeyang i’iw ji-anishinaabekaag omaa.” Naanabozho dash ogii-mikwendaan waa-izhichiged. “Ambe sa, ninga-oozhi’aag igiw gegoo memindage ji-mikwendaagoziwaad. Ambe sa, binesiwag ta-inaawag, gaye animikiig ta-inaawag.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*After they were within, then was he addressed by his younger brother saying: “How is it going to be in a world of people, if one be lost when the people happening by chance are here (on earth)? Search in your mind for a way by which we can bring it about that the place here may be peopled.” So Nänabushu became mindful of a plan that he would follow out. “Behold, I will create them that shall be deserving of remembrance for some special thing. Behold, Birds shall they be called, Thunderers too shall they be called.”*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">gaa-biindigewaad (when-they-were-inside), ganoonigod iniw oshiimeyan (he-was-addressed-by his-younger-brother), aaniin ge-izhi-anishinaabekaag (how shall-it-be-peopled), ji-waniizhinigwen* (if-(one)-be-lost), aano-ayaadin* a’aw anishinaabe (when-the-person-merely-is(here)), daga andomitwendan (so seek-in-your-mind), ge-izhichigeyang (what-we-shall-do), ji-anishinaabekaag omaa (to-people here), ogii-mikwendaan waa-izhichiged (he-thought-of what-he-would-do), ninga-oozhi’aag (I-will-make-them), gegoo memindage ji-mikwendaagoziwaad (deserving-of-special-remembrance), binesiwag ta-inaawag (Birds they-shall-be-called), animikiig ta-inaawag (Thunderers they-shall-be-called.)</span>

<span class="l-orig">(10) Mīsa´ gägä‘t Nänabucu mādci‘tād uci∙ā∙t ‘iᵉi´ᵘ pinäsiwaᵉ. Abā‘pic nībiwa kā∙u∙ci∙ā∙t, kīwätinung midāswā‘k ugīpạgidinā; kayä wâbạnung ugīpạgidînā; kayä iwiti câwạnunk ugīpạgidînā; mīnawā nīngābī∙ạ∙nung. Cigwa dạc kā∙i∙cikạnōnāt: “Ạmbäsa´, wawạtciwạnk icibābāgināyu‘k!” Gägä‘t īgi´ᵘ ạnimi‘kīg kīpāgināwạg wạtciwink. Kägä‘t kīsạgisiwạg īgi´ᵘ mạtcimạnitōg.</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**Mii sa geget Naanabozho maajitaad oozhi’aad i’iw binesiwan. Abaapizh niibowa gaa-oozhi’aad, giiwedinong midaaswaak ogii-bagidinaa; gaye waabanong ogii-bagidinaa; gaye iwidi zhaawanong ogii-bagidinaa; miinawaa ningaabii’anong. Zhigwa dash gaa-izhi-ganoonaad: “Ambe sa, wajiwang izhi-baabaaginayok!” Geget igiw animikiig gii-baaginawag wajiwing. Geget gii-zegiziwag igiw maji-manidoog.**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*Thereupon truly did Nänabushu set to work creating the birds. When he had created a multitude of them, away to the region of the north wind he let ten hundred go; and towards the dawn he let (others) go; and over towards the south he let (some more) go; and towards the west (he let the rest go). So then, speaking to them, he said: “Behold, against the mountains do you strike!” To be sure, the Thunderers struck against the mountains. Truly frightened were the evil manitous.*</span>

<span class="l-gloss">maajitaad oozhi’aad i’iw binesiwan (he-began making the birds), abaapizh niibowa gaa-oozhi’aad (when-he-had-made-many), giiwedinong midaaswaak ogii-bagidinaa (to-the-north he-sent ten-hundred), waabanong (to-the-east(dawn)), zhaawanong (to-the-south), ningaabii’anong (to-the-west), wajiwang izhi-baabaaginayok (strike-ye-against the-mountains), animikiig gii-baaginawag wajiwing (the-Thunderers struck the-mountains), gii-zegiziwag igiw maji-manidoog (the-evil-manidoog were-frightened.)</span>

*Footnote (Jones, p551): ² (Eng “evil manitous²”): “Serpents.” ∙ ³ : “A common Algonkin conception.” — maji-manidoog = evil/other-than-good manidoog; named, never explained.  ⚑ Conversion flag(s) (light, → final pass): izhi-baabaaginayok, gii-baaginawag — forms to confirm.*

<span class="l-orig">(11) “Mīdạc ‘iᵉi´ᵘ kä∙i∙cikanawänimäk ‘aᵉa´ᵘ ạnicinābä. Pitcīnạg nīwī∙u∙ciā ‘aᵉa´ᵘ ạnicinābä. Pō‘tcigu ninguting tạwạningwạci aᵉ ạbinōtcī. Kīcpîn aᵘ ạnicinābä iniwäti pawānāt ạnāma‘kạmig cigwa kā∙i∙citabạsi∙i∙näk, mīsa iᵘ tcimiguskādisit kāwạnimigut īni´ᵘ mạtcimạnidōn. Intawā dac ä‘ta mạnitōwiwin tā∙a∙yā,—nīgān kä∙a∙yāg, ‘midäwiwin’ ta∙i∙cini‘kādä, kayä dạc ‘unạmạnimitäwiwin,’ mīnawā ‘tcīpä∙i∙mitäwiwin,’ mīnawā ‘sāgimāwimitäwiwin,’ mīnawā ‘uskābäwisimitäwiwin.’ Mīsa´ ‘iᵉi´ᵘ kädasing onōᵘ untci iᵘ kīpā‘tātcigäyān kīwạnitōtạmān i∙i∙wä kī∙ō∙ci∙ạ∙gwā īgi´ᵘ mạtcimanitōg. Ạmbädạc, kạnawänimi‘k ogōᵘ ạnicinābäg!” ināwạg īgi´ᵘ ạnimi‘kīg. “Kīcpin ābiding ‘iᵉi´ᵘ kä∙a∙pī‘tcinībing papāwâpamāsiwägwa mī∙i∙´ᵘ tcinibut ‘aᵉa´ᵘ ạnicinābä; ānic osām tagicā‘tä. Mīdạc ä‘ta iᵘ a‘pạnä tcipạmi∙ä∙g ‘aᵉa´ᵘ ạnicinābä. Mīdạc ‘iᵉi´ᵘ mini‘k kä∙a∙‘kīwạnk tcipimādisit ‘aᵉa´ᵘ ạnicinābä. Mīsa´ icimādcāyu‘k, ändasōntānimạk tcī∙i∙cāyäk.”</span>

<span class="l-fiero">**“Mii dash i’iw ge-izhi-ganawenimeg a’aw anishinaabe. Bichiinag niwii-oozhi’aa a’aw anishinaabe. Boochigo ningoding dawaningwazhi a’aw abinoojii. Giishpin a’aw anishinaabe iniwedi bawaanaad anaamakamig zhigwa gaa-izhi-dabasi’inek, mii sa iw ji-migoshkaadizid* gaa-wanimigod iniw maji-manidoon. Indawaa dash eta manidoowiwin daa-ayaa,—niigaan ge-ayag, ‘midewiwin’ da-izhinikaade, gaye dash ‘onamani-midewiwin,’ miinawaa ‘jiibai-midewiwin,’ miinawaa ‘zaagimaawi-midewiwin,’ miinawaa ‘oshkaabewisi-midewiwin.’ Mii sa i’iw gedasing onow onji iw gii-baataajigeyaan* gii-wanitootamaan i’iwe gii-oozhi’agwaa igiw maji-manidoog. Ambe dash, ganawenimik ogow anishinaabeg!” inaawag igiw animikiig. “Giishpin abiding i’iw ge-aabi-jinibing babaa-waabamaasiiwegwaa mii iw ji-nibod a’aw anishinaabe; aaniish osaam da-gizhaade. Mii dash eta iw apane ji-bami’eg a’aw anishinaabe. Mii dash i’iw minik ge-akiiwang ji-bimaadizid a’aw anishinaabe. Mii sa izhi-maajaayok, endaso-noondinimak ji-izhaayeg.”**</span>

<span class="l-eng">*“Therefore shall it be for you to watch over the people. By and by do I intend to create the people. Perforce, there shall be times when children are destined with unlucky dreams. If the people dream of the things whom you have caused to dodge underground for safety, then therefore shall they be unfortunate throughout life, in that they had been deceived by the evil manitous. So therefore the one thing for them to have is an observance of manitou rites, one that shall last into future time; ‘mystic rite’ shall the name of it be called, so also ‘mystic rite of magic paint,’ also ‘mystic rite of the ghost,’ also ‘mystic rite of the serpent,’ also ‘mystic rite of the attendants.’ Thus accordingly shall the number of these (rites) be by reason of my having erred when I made the mistake of creating the evil manitous. So come, keep watch over these people!” thus were the Thunderers told. “If but once throughout a whole summer you fail to wander forth to observe them, then shall the people die; for too hot will the weather be.
